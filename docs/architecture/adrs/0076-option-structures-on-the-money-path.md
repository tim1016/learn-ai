# ADR 0076 — Option structures on the money path

**Status:** Accepted 2026-10-06. It gains a section as each part of #2913 it decides is built.
**Provenance:** Owner decisions in [#2913](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/2913), grilled on 2026-10-05 and revised on 2026-10-06 after a review against the code and Alpaca's documents.
**Related:** ADR 0012 (the Action Plan, and its 2026-10-06 amendment that defines an option structure), ADR 0022 (i) (the option expiry anchor), ADR 0035 (the Clerk's event-sourced store: schema v23 adds the two contract tables, both written only by folds of the custody log), ADR 0059 (the live envelope and the budget, which cover US equities only until this ADR's later sections extend them), ADR 0062 (the fleet: a Clerk asks the coordinator for the rate and the dividends, as it asks for chart history), ADR 0067 (the supervisor a preview's quotes come from), ADR 0068 (Python owns the math).

## Context

A bot may listen to one symbol's signal and trade option structures on any underlying. Nothing on the trading side could do that: an order was one stock leg, money was counted per share, and no rule said what an option position costs to hold or what the bot must have before it opens one.

Alpaca's order and account references (read 2026-10-06) describe no call that says, before an order, what that order will cost. So the bot works it out itself from Alpaca's published rule, and compares with what Alpaca took afterwards.

## Decision

### 1. A contract has one spelling

An option contract is its root symbol, its expiry, call or put, and its strike. The expiry is the date's 16:00 New York anchor. The strike is held in whole thousandths of a dollar, which is the unit the OCC symbol writes it in, so no float decides which contract an order names. The symbol Alpaca uses (`AAPL240119C00100000`) is built from those four and read back from them, and anything that is not such a symbol is refused.

A symbol cannot say that a contract delivers 100 shares or is American. Those are facts of the listing, to be checked where a contract is picked. The one thing the spelling shows is an adjusted contract, whose root carries a digit; it is refused.

### 2. Five money quantities, kept apart

All are whole cents. The fee provision is the one figure that rounds, up to the next cent, where it is worked out. Nothing else is rounded; an amount that does not come to whole cents is refused.

| Quantity | What it is | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Net entry premium** | What the entry pays or receives: bought legs' prices minus sold legs' prices, times ratio and multiplier | Positive is paid, negative is received. This is the sign Alpaca's multi-leg limit price carries |
| **Broker requirement** | Alpaca's maintenance requirement by its published rule. Premiums are left out | Zero or more |
| **Entry commitment** | What the bot sets aside when an entry is accepted: requirement plus premium plus the fee provision, for the structures requested | Above zero |
| **Loss at expiry, estimate** | The most the structure loses if every leg has the same expiry, is held to it, is never exercised or assigned early, and settles at its exercise value, before fees | Zero or more |
| **Free budget** | What the bot's budget has left for a new entry (ADR 0059) | Can go below zero |

- **The requirement and the commitment are different numbers by design.** A $5-wide call spread sold for $3.07 has a requirement of $500.00 and a commitment of $193.00 before fees. Comparing the two is never an error.
- **None of them is a cap.** The commitment decides whether an entry may start. A market exit, fees, an early assignment or a close that does not complete can each cost more than the estimate.

### 3. The requirement is the lowest point of the exercise payoff

The requirement sums every leg's exercise payoff as a function of the underlying's price and takes the lowest point. It is read at a price of zero, at every strike, and from the slope above the highest strike. The strikes alone are not enough: a put structure can be at its worst at zero, and a call structure can keep losing above its last strike.

- A loss that grows without limit above the highest strike is refused. That reason is given first, ahead of any about expiries.
- Alpaca's page says to find the price with the lowest payoff. Reading it at zero and from the slope above the top strike is this repo's way of finding it.
- A requirement plus premium of zero or less is refused. Such a result means the prices do not belong together.
- A structure priced under what it must be worth at expiry is admitted, with a loss estimate of zero. For one made of bought legs only, such as a 440 call and a 460 put bought for less than $20, those prices do not belong together either, and whether to refuse them is the owner's to say. It is not always a fault: a box of four legs is worth the same at every price and properly costs a little less, by the interest to expiry.
- A bounded payoff does not prove Alpaca will take the structure as one order. Cover is a separate check.

### 4. Mixed expiries

Alpaca's rule works a position with several expiries out expiry by expiry and takes the largest figure. Its documents do not say how that treats a sold leg covered by a bought leg that expires later.

- A structure with mixed expiries and a sold leg is refused: its commitment cannot be computed. This stands until the treatment is verified on the account.
- A structure with mixed expiries and bought legs only is admitted. Its requirement is zero under either reading.
- For any structure with mixed expiries the loss at expiry is not estimated, and is shown as "not estimated". A leg that outlives another is worth something no exercise payoff describes.

The owner chose on 2026-10-06 to keep mixed expiries on these terms. The recommendation had been one expiry per structure.

### 5. Round-trip cost

A per-leg limit on the bid-ask gap does not bound what a structure costs to trade. Two legs with mids of $5.00 and $4.90, each four cents wide, cost $0.14 to open against each other and return $0.06 closed at once.

- The **round-trip cost** is the sum over the legs of ask minus bid, times ratio and multiplier. It is always worked out.
- An entry is to be refused when it is more than the **structure cost limit**: a percent of the entry commitment, 10 by default, set at Deploy. The owner chose this on 2026-10-06. The comparison is built; the setting arrives with Deploy. The commitment is the measure because it is always above zero; a percent of the structure's mid value breaks down when that value is near zero.

### 6. The pricing model matches the contract

Stock and ETF options are American: the holder may exercise early. Everything this repo priced options with before is European Black-Scholes, which prices a different contract. Delta and implied volatility for a contract a bot may pick come from an American model.

- **The model is a Leisen-Reimer binomial tree,** with the exercise test at every step. It was chosen over a finite-difference solver because it can be proven: with no dividend it is the same construction as QuantLib 1.42.1's `BinomialLRVanillaEngine`, and the two agree to 1e-9 on 400 cases from thirty days to fifteen minutes to expiry (fixture `AM-001`). A finite-difference solver could only be shown to agree with another to within both grids.
- **Time to expiry is a fraction of a 365-day year, to the millisecond.** A date-based day count gives a contract expiring today no time at all.
- **Dividends are cash amounts on dates, given explicitly, and held in escrow:** the tree is built on the price less the present value of the dividends still to go ex, and a node is exercised at that plus the dividends not yet paid. There is no dividend yield, and no default of none: a caller pricing a stock that pays nothing says so.
- **With dividends the tree is proven only to its own discretization error. This is an accepted divergence from the strict tolerance.** No pinned engine builds this tree with cash dividends, so there is nothing to match to 1e-9. Against QuantLib's finite-difference engine under the same escrowed model, itself good to about 5e-5 on `AM-002`'s cases, the tree's largest measured gap is 5.9e-4 in price and 7.2e-5 in delta at 2,001 steps (fixture `AM-002`, held to 1e-3 and 2e-4). These are fixture measurements, not bounds. An implied volatility solved from that engine's prices is within 3.0e-4 of its volatility inside one standard deviation of the money on those cases.
- **The other cash-dividend model would move the answer more than the step count does.** QuantLib's "spot" model changes the same cases by up to 8e-2 in price and 3e-3 in delta. Escrowed is the choice; it is the one the tree can carry.
- **The default is 401 steps, with 1,009 steps for calls with any cash dividend before or at expiry.** The owner chose better near ex-date accuracy with an explicit cost review on 2026-10-09. A cutoff at two days to ex-date was rejected in review: it moved one valid call's price by 9.5e-3 when its ex-date moved one millisecond. An explicit step count is used exactly. The 401-step price and delta gaps of 3.2e-3 and 4.2e-4 are `AM-002`'s own maxima; its test holds them to half a cent and a thousandth of delta. `AM-006` adds the missing day-one high-dividend case: at explicit 401 steps its call price is 1.9e-2 below the second engine, while the adaptive default brings the largest price gap in its six cases to 2.9e-3. Delta rules retain their own 0.05 tolerance. **Measured cost on one development machine:** for that 59-day call with a $3 dividend on day 1, median valuation time rose from 1.18 to 3.48 ms, daily decay from 2.38 to 4.67 ms, and implied-volatility solve from 26.75 to 73.47 ms at 401 versus 1,009 steps. These figures describe cost, not a production latency guarantee; puts and calls without a cash dividend remain at 401 steps.
- **Daily time decay is the model's price 24 hours on less its price now,** at the held stock price, volatility and rate, with a dividend that goes ex inside the day taken off that price in full. It is one contract's figure, a share. A day is 24 hours by the clock, so over a weekend three of them pass before the next session. With no dividend it is the difference of two prices the reference tree also computes, and the two agree to 1e-9 on 400 cases (fixture `AM-004`). **With dividends it carries the same accepted divergence as the price:** against the difference of the finite-difference engine's two prices the largest measured gap on `AM-005` is 9.4e-4 at an explicit 2,003 steps, 8.1e-3 at an explicit 401 steps, and 8.9e-4 with the adaptive default. These are fixture measurements, not bounds. Each price uses the default step selection for its own valuation time, so a day's later price is the next day's current price.
- **A solve with no answer says why and carries no number.** The reasons are kept apart: under a minute to expiry; a price under what exercising now pays; a price no higher than the model's at the lowest volatility tried, which includes an option priced at exactly its exercise value, whichever way its cents round; a price above the model's at the highest; no convergence, which is also the answer when the tree resolves the strike at no volatility it tries (a strike far from the price within a couple of minutes of expiry); an input the model has no meaning for. A vendor's implied volatility or Greeks are never put in a failure's place (ADR 0071 Decision 1, which this extends from the Polygon recorder to any quote a bot prices).
- **A strike dozens of standard deviations from the price is not priced.** The tree's branch sizes are rounding error there. It answers nothing, never a guess.

### 7. A structure is checked after its contracts are picked

A plan's legs are rules, and independent rules can land on a shape nobody meant. A bounded loss does not prove the result is the structure the owner named. So the picked contracts are checked before an order is built from them, and wherever a plan is previewed.

- **Every structure.** One contract per leg of the plan, of the leg's kind, on the plan's underlying. No contract in two legs. Every sold leg has a bought leg of its kind, contract for contract, that does not expire before it. A resolved structure takes each leg's side and ratio from the plan, so it cannot differ from what was declared.
- **A named structure, also its own shape.** One expiry, and:

  | Structure | Must hold |
  |---|---|
  | Bull call spread | bought strike below sold strike; premium paid |
  | Bear put spread | bought strike above sold strike; premium paid |
  | Bull put spread | bought strike below sold strike; premium received, and less than the width |
  | Bear call spread | bought strike above sold strike; premium received, and less than the width |
  | Long straddle | one strike |
  | Long strangle | put strike below call strike |
  | Iron condor | bought put, sold put, sold call, bought call, strikes rising; premium received |
  | Iron butterfly | the sold put and sold call share a strike, with the bought put below it and the bought call above; premium received |
  | Call or put butterfly | lower wing, body, upper wing, strikes rising; wings the same width |

- **The name is part of the plan** (ADR 0012's amendment). A plan with no name is Custom and gets the first check only.
- **A failure is a typed refusal** that names the rule and the legs. At an entry it skips the entry; in a preview it is the verdict. Its reasons are spelled as the money's are (`OPTION_STRUCTURE_…`), so one screen and one stored entry read both.
- **A share-class ticker is not handled yet.** A contract is on the plan's underlying when its root is the plan's ticker letter for letter, and `BRK.B` is written `BRKB` in a contract symbol. Such a plan's picks are refused until a root is matched to its ticker. The broker's contract reference now carries both spellings (decision 11); nothing matches them yet.
- **Not asked here:** whether the loss is bounded, which the requirement answers (decision 3), and whether Alpaca lists each contract as standard and tradable, which waits for its contract reference.
- **Where a named structure starts** is a separate list: the legs and rules the builder fills in. Most hang on one leg picked by delta with the others a dollar distance from it; the strangle and the iron condor pick two legs by delta, a put and a call out of the money. Every value is a starting point the owner changes, and a deployed plan carries its own. **The values themselves are provisional.** Only the bull call spread's (delta 0.50, $5 above, the nearest weekly expiry) come from the builder's design (#2915); the 0.30 delta for a leg out of the money and the other eleven starts await the owner's word.

### 8. Rules become contracts from the quotes held, or not at all

A plan's legs are rules. At an entry one pure function turns them into one contract for every leg, or one typed refusal; nothing is resolved in part. It reads no feed and no clock: the chain's definition, the quotes held, the underlying's price and the times are handed in.

- **There is no snapshot of a chain.** There is the newest bid and the newest ask received for each contract, each with the time its callback delivered it. Four moments are kept apart: the decision bar's close, the selection time, each quote side's receipt time, and the recheck just before an order is sent.
- **A quote is usable or fails as one thing.** The tests are asked in one order and the first to fail is the fault: an older subscription generation; one side missing; not live data; a side that is no price; an ask of zero; a bid of zero where something is to be sold; bid above ask; a side older than the decision-data budget (ADR 0067), or received after the moment it is used at. The first two say the quote has not arrived whole since the last reconnect.
- **The observations must belong together.** Every picked leg's quote and the underlying's must have been received within a coherence window of each other, 2,000 ms unless set otherwise. Each being fresh is not enough.
- **The money is the decision bar's close; the model's price is the underlying's live mid.** Strike rules that count from the money use the bar the decision was made on. Delta is solved from each candidate's mid against the underlying's mid, which arrived with the option quotes.
- **A chain may name its expiries before the contracts of each are known.** An expiry rule chooses among every date named. A leg that comes to an expiry whose contracts are not known yet is a search not yet complete.
- **A rule picks the nearest contract with a usable quote.** Each contract stands one of three ways for a leg:
  - **Its quote has not arrived:** none is held, or one side only, or only one from before the last reconnect. Nothing is known of it and it could have been the pick, so it stops the search.
  - **Its quote has arrived and fails a test,** or the rule picks by delta and the model cannot turn the quote into one. It is passed over for the next nearest.
  - **It is usable.**

  | Refusal | When |
  |---|---|
  | Search incomplete | A contract that could be the pick has a quote that has not arrived. The contract is named, and its fault when a quote is held |
  | No usable quote | Every contract the rule may pick was passed over. The first one and its fault are named |
  | Nothing close enough | The chain lists nothing within the tolerance |

- **A rule by delta walks toward its target.** It cannot know the delta of a contract with no quote. From the usable contract nearest the target it goes strike by strike toward the target, until it meets a usable contract beyond the target, or the last listed strike. It walks past the contracts passed over, and past a usable one still short of the target: far from the money a cent of quote can put two deltas out of order. A contract on the way whose quote has not arrived makes the search incomplete, even with a pick inside the tolerance. The way to walk takes delta to fall as a call's strike rises and to grow as a put's does; where the walk ends does not.
- **With no usable contract a rule by delta has nothing to walk from,** and the watched window is all it can judge by. While any watched contract's quote has not arrived the search is incomplete, whatever has arrived faulty, and the awaited contract nearest the money is named. Once every watched one has arrived and been passed over it is "no usable quote", naming the one nearest the money, though strikes nobody watches are listed beyond: what is wrong then is the quotes held, not the window.

- **A rule by strike uses what the chain's definition already says.** It knows every listed strike, so a target just outside the watched window is not "search incomplete" when the nearest listed strike is watched, and a target nothing is listed near is "nothing close enough" without any quote. This is stricter in meaning than "outside the window is always incomplete", and refuses less.
- **Ties** go as the plan's rules say: the money between two strikes is the lower; a delta or a percent as near two strikes takes the one nearer the money, then the lower; a distance from another leg takes the narrower spread.
- **Expiry rules count on the New York calendar from the decision's date.** The nearest weekly is the nearest listed expiry after that date on the last session of its week, so a week cut short by a holiday has its weekly on the Thursday. An expiry the chain does not list is refused as that, and so is one it lists on a day the calendar has no session for.
- **Nothing is opened too close to its last trade.** A contract is not offered once the selection is within the entry cutoff's lead of the session close on its expiry date, half days included. Time to expiry runs to that same close. A product that trades past the session close on its last day is counted as stopping with the session until each contract's own hours have a source.
- **Only standard contracts are on the ladder:** 100 shares, a multiplier of 100, American exercise. A rule counts strikes along the standard ones and never picks another.
- **The evidence is kept with the pick,** enough to reproduce it. For each leg: the expiries its expiry rule chose from and the strikes of its expiry, as the chain lists them, because a count along the ladder is made on those; and every watched contract of its right and expiry with its quote, receipt times, data type, generation, the provider's own delta, and why it was passed over; on a refused leg a usable contract says the leg was refused, not that another was nearer. The pick, and every contract a rule by delta asked the model about, also carries the model's inputs and its implied volatility and delta. Beside the legs: the watched window, the moments, and the model's rate and dividends with their sources.
- **The recheck picks nothing.** Just before an order is sent the picked legs' quotes are tested again at that moment. A fault, a missing quote, an unhealthy feed or observations now too far apart refuses the entry, and nothing is sent. So does a picked contract that has come inside its entry cutoff since the selection: the order is sent at the recheck's moment, not the selection's. So does a reconnect since the selection, even when every quote has arrived again in the new generation (ADR 0067); the feed is asked before any quote, so a reconnect is never reported as one of the quotes it left behind. The recheck is held to the limits the selection was made under and keeps the generation it saw. What the structure now costs and how wide its legs' markets are is not the recheck's question; those limits are asked where the entry's price is.
- **A refusal is a fact about the market.** An input that could not describe one, such as a bar close that is no price above zero or a selection before its bar's close, is the caller's mistake and raises an error of its own.
- **Not yet proven on a real chain.** The tests build their chain from the pricing model's own prices. A chain frozen from the provider during a session is still to be added as a golden fixture; taking one draws on the account's shared quote lines, so it waits for the owner's word.

### 9. Option quotes arrive through the Clerk's own supervisor, on a budget of lines

The Clerk's IBKR supervisor (ADR 0067) owns option quote lines as it owns the underlying's. It owns the connection, sees every reconnect, and drives the option side once a second. No route opens a line of its own. A fault in any step of the option side is logged and never stops the pass that keeps the stock quotes; when what is wanted cannot be read, what was wanted a moment ago stands. After such a fault the option feed reads unhealthy until a whole pass has gone through, so no contract is picked from lines left part way between what was wanted and what is.

- **A quote is what its callbacks delivered.** For each contract with a line: the newest bid and the newest ask, each with the time its own callback delivered it, and the kind of data IBKR says it is. Reading never renews a receipt. A side is first held when a tick of its price arrives on this line, and its price is that tick's own. The client library keeps one cache of prices for a contract across every request made for it, so a price found there may be from before a data loss or from a line given up long ago; nothing is read from it. Once a side is held, a tick of its size is a receipt for it, because the price stands, and a size of nothing says the side has emptied. A callback that carries neither side, such as IBKR's own model figures, renews neither.
- **A side IBKR delivers as -1 has arrived and has nothing there.** It is kept as zero: nothing bid, or nothing offered. A contract with no bid can still be bought at its ask and cannot be sold; one with no ask is passed over. The cost limit on a structure (decision 5) is what stops a contract whose bid went missing for a moment.
- **A switch between live, delayed and frozen data leaves nothing received.** A quote is of one kind. Delayed data is kept as delayed, so a rule refuses it as not live and not as a quote still to arrive.
- **The feed's generation is one number for the connection's epoch.** It moves on every reconnect and on every recovery from a data loss, IBKR's "data maintained" recovery included, and nothing delivered before it is kept: neither a receipt time, nor a price, nor IBKR's model delta. After it each side waits for a price tick of its own. The underlying's quote is handed over in the same generation. One contract's line being replaced empties that contract's quote and does not move the generation: otherwise one broken line would empty every quote.
- **The chain's definition is read from IBKR once a New York day.** Its expiries come in one answer and its contracts one expiry at a time, so only the expiries a plan's rules come to, and the expiries of contracts held, have their contracts read. A day's read replaces the last only when it lands, so yesterday's listing and the lines opened from it stay in service through the read. IBKR answers a listing request it cannot serve with nothing, not with an error; nothing is a failed read, tried again with a growing wait, and never a chain with no contracts. A contract is the one its own OCC symbol names, as IBKR gives it; one whose fields disagree with its symbol is left out. The multiplier is IBKR's. What a listing cannot say is taken as standard: American exercise, and one share per unit of the multiplier for a contract in the underlying's own trading class. Both are for the broker's contract reference to prove for each picked contract before any order; nothing asks it yet.
- **The watched window.** For each leg: the expiry its rule comes to today, and the listed strikes within a set number of the strike its rule aims at. A rule by delta aims at the money. The window is centred on the newest pricing bar's close, the price the resolver calls the money, so it moves at most once a minute; with no bar yet, on the underlying's live mid, taken at most once every fifteen seconds so that a mid hovering between two strikes does not open and give up the window's edges on every pass. When there is no price to centre it on, or only a quote no decision could use, it stays where it was: it is not given up and not moved. A window too small for a rule makes its search incomplete and never changes its pick.
- **A Clerk keeps to a line budget of its own.** IBKR grants the username a number of concurrent lines, shared by every Clerk and any open TWS, and no session can see the whole. The budget is the most option contracts the Clerk asks for at once, the ones its bots hold included. It is configuration, and it is zero until the owner sets it, because every option line comes out of the allowance the stock quotes of every bot draw on. A count of the Clerk's own lines never claims that IBKR has capacity.
- **One contract is one line, and lines go by priority.** Contracts a bot holds or has a working order in come first, are never given up for a lower class, and are asked for even past the budget, so that a refusal is IBKR's and is seen. Then the windows of running bots, nearest their centre first. Then previews and pages, which get only what is spare. A window that moves keeps the strikes it still wants; the lines it no longer wants are given up first, and the new ones asked for after, at most twenty in a pass. A wanted contract with no line is always reported with the reason, a held one included.
- **When IBKR refuses a line for capacity,** the contract has no quote for want of capacity, which is a different thing from the Clerk's own budget. The number of lines open at that moment becomes the ceiling until the next reconnect: the lowest classes give way, and a held contract is asked for again. Once a minute, while something waits, the ceiling is tried one line higher. A line refused for another reason, such as a missing subscription, stays refused until nothing wants it or the connection changes.
- **A quiet line is not a broken one.** A line that has gone the subscription timeout with no whole quote, both sides, is asked for again, which makes IBKR send its current prices. The wait counts from when the line was opened, or from when it last lost a side it held (a switch of the kind of data, a size reported on a side with no price); a line kept through a recovery counts as opened at the recovery. One that holds a whole quote and then had nothing new to say is left alone: replacing it would turn a stale quote, which a search walks past, into one not yet arrived, which stops it.
- **Not yet measured on a live connection.** How often a contract's quote is older than the decision-data budget in a calm market; what IBKR sends for a contract with no bid; whether a quiet contract's line still delivers model figures. These decide how often entries are refused, and the answers come from a session with the Gateway: `scripts/capture_option_chain.py` freezes a chain as the supervisor holds it and counts, second by second, how each watched quote stood.

### 10. One order, signed as Alpaca signs it

A structure goes to the broker as one order. The broker order contract gains the types that say so, beside the stock leg and not inside it: a stock leg, and every hash taken over one, is what it was.

- **Two shapes.** A structure of one leg is a plain option order on that contract. A structure of two to four legs is a multi-leg order: each leg's contract, side, ratio and intent, a number of whole structures, and a market order or one net limit price. A one-leg structure is never sent as a multi-leg order of one.
- **The net price keeps one sign from the quotes to the wire.** Positive is paid, negative is received. That is the sign `natural_net_price` returns and the sign Alpaca's order reference gives a multi-leg `limit_price`, so nothing between them flips it. A one-leg order's price is above zero and its side says which way the money goes.
- **A quantity is a whole number and a price is an exact decimal.** The stock contract holds both as floats because it began as a display surface. An option order's price is compared with what was set aside for it in exact cents, so no float sits between them.
- **Every leg says whether it opens or closes.** An exit's legs all close. The intent is there so a repeated exit cannot open the reverse position. That Alpaca refuses a closing intent beyond what is held is not in its documents and is to be shown on the account.
- **An option order is for the day and for regular hours.** A request has no field for a time in force or for extended hours, so neither can be asked for.
- **Ratios share no divisor above one, the legs are on one underlying, and no contract is in two legs.** The first is Alpaca's stated rule; an order that breaks it is refused before it is sent.
- **No price step is applied.** Alpaca's Trading API pages state none for an option or for a multi-leg net price. The contract carries the exact decimal it is given, and Alpaca's own refusal is the check until a recording settles it.
- **An accepted order is never reported as refused, and never as absent.** Once Alpaca has answered a submit with an order, an answer this repo cannot read is an unknown outcome, settled by an exact lookup. Nothing is asked of Alpaca a second time inside the submit, because that request's failure could be taken for the order's. The thirty seconds in which a not-found proves nothing start then too, exactly as they do after a submit that got no answer. They count again from any answer Alpaca gives for the order that is not taken as the order's, and end only when an answer is read whole and is about the order asked for. They cover the lookup by client order id, a read by the broker order id an unreadable answer named, and a read by any broker order id after an answer to it that was not taken.
- **An unreadable answer still names the order to cancel, when it can.** The refusal carries the broker order id only when the answer's own ids are the ones the caller asked by. Another order's id is never handed on.
- **An order goes out under its caller's own client order id, and an answer must be about that order.** An order with no client order id is not sent: Alpaca would make one up, and no lookup could then find the order. Nor is one sent under an id Alpaca could not keep as given: longer than the 128 characters its reference allows, or padded with spaces. An answer that names another client order id, or another broker order id, is no answer; its fills are not counted and the outcome stays unknown. Broker order ids are compared as the ids they are, however they are spelled.
- **An answer is read whole or not at all.** The stock order reader reads a damaged row as far as it can, because one row must not refuse a whole list. The answer about one option order is different: a filled quantity that will not parse must not become zero contracts filled. An answer is refused the same way when it says more filled than was ordered, is for no contracts or no structures, names one contract in two legs or legs on two underlyings, has a multi-leg parent that states a contract, a side or an intent of its own, or prices one contract below zero (only a multi-leg net price is signed). From Alpaca, an order id or a leg's id that is not a UUID is refused too: the port could not read or cancel by it. A stock order found by an option lookup is refused, not read. A leg's quantity is not held to its ratio times the parent's: Alpaca's own example request and answer disagree on that, and a recording has to settle it.
- **Legs come nested under their parent.** Each leg is an order of its own, with its own id, filled quantity, average price and status. Alpaca documents the way to ask for nested legs on the read by broker order id only, so a multi-leg parent found by its client order id without its legs is read again by its own id. No undocumented parameter is sent.
- **A broker says whether it takes option orders.** Two capability flags, both no unless stated. Alpaca says yes to both. A Dry Run's simulation says yes to an order of one leg and no to several (decision 16). What a simulated broker does not take it refuses with a typed error that is never an unknown outcome. A shadow world serves its live broker's capabilities with the several-leg flag turned off: its own order book fills an order of one leg and takes none of several (decision 25).

### 11. A contract is checked against the broker's own listing

A resolved contract is looked up in Alpaca's contract reference, in the Trading API. It is not a market-data read. One rule says whether a bot may trade it, and gives the first reason it may not, by the contract's symbol:

| Refusal | When |
|---|---|
| Not listed | Alpaca answers that it has no such contract. A contract IBKR lists and Alpaca does not ends here |
| Not the contract asked for | The answer's symbol, expiry, call or put, strike or root is not the contract's own |
| Not active, not tradable | By the listing's status and its tradable flag |
| Not American | By the listing's style |
| Not standard | Its multiplier or the shares it delivers is not 100; or, when the answer lists its deliverables, they are not exactly 100 shares of its underlying |

- An outage is not "not listed": only Alpaca's own not-found answer is.
- A listing that leaves out what the rule needs, such as its multiplier, cannot be read at all. It is never read as standard.
- The listing's words are taken exactly as Alpaca's reference writes them, in lower case (`active`, `american`, `equity`). A word it does not list is not taken for the one it resembles.
- **A share-class ticker is still not matched.** The listing's root symbol and underlying symbol are both carried as Alpaca writes them (`BRKB` and `BRK.B`). The rule holds the root to the contract symbol's own and matches nothing to a ticker. Decision 7's refusal of such a plan stands until that matching is built on these two fields.

### 12. The account's option fields are read beside the account, not into it

Alpaca's account answer carries an option buying power, the option level it approved, and the level in force. They are read into a model of their own, by a request of their own, from the same endpoint.

- The account snapshot a route serves is unchanged, so the served contract and the generated frontend types did not move.
- A field Alpaca leaves out stays unknown. It is never zero: a level of zero is Alpaca saying options are off. Alpaca's own example answer has no approved level.
- A level outside the 0 to 3 Alpaca lists is refused, not passed on as approval for everything. So is a trading level above the approved level: Alpaca's reference defines the first as the lower of the second and the owner's own maximum.
- The Clerk reads the option buying power around every option entry on a real account, to set its fall beside what was set aside (decision 26). Deploy's refusal for a missing approval comes later.

### 13. Custody counts a contract by its own multiplier

The Clerk's books counted every quantity as shares: a fill of 2 at 1.50 moved $3.00. An option fill of 2 contracts at 1.50 moves $300.00.

- **The multiplier is a recorded fact of the contract.** It is written once, when the contract first enters the books, in `option_contracts` (symbol, multiplier), and never edited. It is not read off the symbol: the symbol says which contract, never what it multiplies by. One symbol has one multiplier for good; recording another for the same symbol is refused, because an adjusted contract trades under another symbol. FIFO refuses a fill that counts differently from its symbol's open lots.
- **An order bound to a contract is that contract's, wherever its owner is asked for.** Its bot is registered under the underlying. So the check that lets an exact execution take a recovered total's place, and the operator's recovery of an execution, both name the order's contract, never the bot's symbol. An execution in any other symbol is refused by the contract's own rule first, and a replacement refused that way is a refusal its route answers, with nothing written.
- **A coverage proof for an option order still uses the listed-stock price increment.** No option increment is known (decision 10). The stock increment is never larger than a listed option's, so the proof can refuse a replacement that an option's own rounding would explain, and can never accept one it should refuse.
- **Questions about the market are asked of the underlying.** A held contract is watched through the symbol its bot is registered under and is never put on the stock watch list. Whether the Clerk has work in flight on a contract is read from the orders bound to it. Whether a close's market is halted is asked of the contract's root, which for a ticker with a share class is written without its dot and is not matched back.
- **An order trades one instrument.** `order_contracts` says which contract an order trades. The row is written by the same fold that creates the order, so that an option order never exists without its contract, and the contract's terms must already be recorded. Decision 15 names the two folds that write one. A fill names its order, so a fill's contract and multiplier are read through the order.
- **Absent, not defaulted.** A stock order has no row in either table. Its fills are shares and are counted by the same expressions as before. Nothing stored before this decision has a row. The readers that turn a fill into money or a position join the two tables: the three economic projections, the entry's cash claim and the execution history. Readers that only total an order's quantity do not, and need not. A store no Clerk has opened since is read on a private copy brought to the current schema, as any older store is (#2615), so no reader meets a file without them.
- **Positions are held per contract.** A position is keyed by the fill's own symbol, so two contracts on one underlying are two positions and a contract is never netted against the underlying's shares. The one write that moves a position refuses a fill whose symbol is not its order's contract, and refuses a fill spelled as an option contract on an order bound to none. Shape is used only to refuse, never to price. A coverage replacement writes an exact execution without moving a position, so each such row is held to the same test where it is written. A fill of an order that trades a contract is a whole number of contracts; a fraction is refused.
- **Where the multiplier is applied.** FIFO realized and open P&L, a lot's cost basis, the budget's position cost, the proceeds of a sale cash has not seen, simulated cash, and what an entry order claims while cash has not caught up with it: each is contracts × premium per share × multiplier, in the exact decimal arithmetic those modules already use. The account's day P&L is the broker's equity less the prior close's and transfers, and multiplies nothing.
- **An entry order's claim is counted as its position will be.** A fill cash has not seen claims its actual cost, by the multiplier, so the cash free to deploy is the same before and after cash sees the fill. The unfilled part of an order bound to a contract claims contracts × the reservation's reference price × multiplier: the reference price is read as a premium per share, as a fill's price is. A Dry Run's entry in one bought option records one (decision 15), with its limit price. A sold leg's entry commitment (decision 2) also holds the broker's requirement, which that row has no place for: whatever first records one must change this reading.
- **Option fees are predicted from dated, published rates.** Alpaca charges no commission on stock and ETF options and passes five fees through: the SEC fee and FINRA's TAF on sales, and the ORF, the OCC clearing fee and the CAT fee on both sides. The SEC fee is on the premium sold (contracts × premium × multiplier); the CAT fee counts a contract as its multiplier in shares; the rest are per contract. Alpaca sums each fee type over an account's day and rounds each total up to the cent. An entry's fee provision is its own fills settled as a day of their own. Every product, day total and conversion to cents is taken in the Clerk's exact money context, whatever decimal context the caller works in, and an input that is not a finite number of a supported size is refused. **Amended by #3306 (2026-10-20):** OCC is the exception to the day total. Alpaca Paper posts it as one row per execution, so each fill's OCC is rounded up to the cent and the cents are added (an entry's OCC is then exactly its share of the day's). The other four types are still summed over the day first.
  - SEC and TAF are dated by the regulators. Alpaca dates its schedule by revision and states no effective dates, so its ORF, OCC and CAT figures are dated by the revision that first printed them. A rate with no date to stand on is unknown, never zero.
  - The option model uses the stock model's date lookup, cent rounding and day settlement, and its unpinned-rate error is a kind of the stock model's. Whatever takes an unpinned stock rate as an unknown takes an option's the same way.
  - Every figure is an estimate until Alpaca posts the day's fees. Observed fee activities stay the truth (ADR 0059 Decision 6).
- **The fee attribution prices an option fill by the option fee model, on every account.** The listed-stock model never prices one. A simulated account's option fills are charged by the model alone (decision 16). A real account's are an estimate until a fee row of their day is seen, and the day's rows are then shared among all of the day's fills (decision 19). An option fill is never by itself a reason an account's fees read unresolved.
- **Not done by this decision:**
  - who writes the two tables (the option order path; decision 15, for a Dry Run's one bought option);
  - the cash an option entry sets aside in full (the entry commitment, decision 2);
  - what the trade stream does with a fill the position write refuses: settled by decision 19, which withholds it as a lost answer is withheld;
  - a fill in an adjusted contract's symbol on an order bound to none: decision 1 does not read it as a contract, so the shape test lets it through as shares;
  - a sold leg in the budget, which is long-only today and refuses a short lot;
  - marks for an open option position in a simulation (decision 16, for a bought one);
  - an option order placed outside the Clerk, whose multiplier nothing records.

### 14. An assignment, an exercise and an expiry are read from the broker's activities and matched to lots

Three things end an option position with no order of ours: a sold option is assigned, a bought option is exercised, or an option expires. Alpaca says it does not send these over the trade-updates stream, so they are polled from the account's activities. Each is reported against the account, with a date and no time, as a signed change to the account's position in the contract. It names no lot.

**Reading the rows**

- **Read by category, not by code.** The read walks the account's non-trade activities and keeps the option ones. It asks for no activity code by name, because Alpaca's pages spell the exercise two ways (`OPEXC` in the reference and on the page of examples, `OPXRC` in the Account Activities table) and a request for a code Alpaca does not know could be refused. Both spellings are read as an exercise, and the one that arrived is kept on the record.
- **An activity is read whole or not at all.** A row that cannot be read whole is listed as unreadable with its id, its code, its symbol as written and the reason, and no figure from it. The other rows are still read, so one bad row does not hide the rest. A row of any kind with no id or code, or with a date that will not parse, refuses the whole read, because it cannot be placed in the walk. An option row with no date at all is listed as unreadable. No other row's figures are read at all, so a fee or dividend row cannot refuse it.
- **The sign must fit the kind.** A negative change takes from a bought position and a positive one adds back to a sold one. An exercise ends a bought position and an assignment a sold one. A row signed the other way is not something Alpaca's pages show, and may be one being undone, so it is unreadable and never booked as its opposite. The rule refuses such a change too.
- **Any code that begins `OP` and is not read is named, never skipped.** Alpaca's reference also lists an option corporate action (`OPCA`) and a cash deliverable (`OPCSH`). Neither is read. Each shows up as an unreadable row, as would a third spelling of the exercise.
- **A code is read exactly as Alpaca's pages write it.** One that is an option code once its padding and case are set aside (`opexp`, ` OPEXP`) is an option activity all the same. It is listed as unreadable under the code as it arrived: a row that may be an expiry is never walked past as a fee is.
- **An event states no cash of its own.** Every documented event carries a net amount of zero; the cash is the share trade's. An event that states some is settled in a way the books do not follow, so it is unreadable.
- **The date is held as 00:00 New York time on that date.** That is the anchor the adapter already gives every date-only activity. It is not the session open, because an activity's date need not be a trading day, and it is not 16:00, which is an expiry's anchor alone. A read's window is whole New York dates for the same reason: a row dated on the day the window starts is kept whatever the hour.
- **An option activity that states a time is unreadable.** Alpaca's Account Activities page gives a non-trade activity a date and no time, and every documented option row is so. A row is placed in the read's window by its date alone, so an instant on it can never put it before a window its date is in. If an account's rows do carry a time, every option row is unreadable and no read is whole: nothing is booked, and the reader has to be changed first.
- **An expiry is dated on its contract's expiry date or after it.** One dated earlier is unreadable: no option expires early, and booking the row would close lots that are still alive. An assignment or an exercise may come on any day.
- **Only an activity the broker reports as executed is matched to lots.** Alpaca's reference lists two other statuses, `correct` and `canceled`, and does not say which row either replaces or undoes. Such a row is read, kept in the list, and matched to nothing.
- **A read is whole only when it explains everything.** The walk reached the window's start, no row is unreadable, and the broker reports every row as executed. Only then does no event mean nothing happened. Otherwise the caller holds: it follows the walk to its end, and keeps a hold for each unreadable row and each row not reported as executed until the owner has settled what it did.
- **An event and its share trade are each kept once, by id among their own kind.** Alpaca's Trading API examples print one id on every row. If an account's rows share an id that way, one key across both kinds would lose a row.

**Matching an event to lots**

- **An assignment or an exercise takes the oldest lot first.** An assignment takes sold lots only and an exercise bought lots only. A lot may be taken in part.
- **An expiry takes the newest lot first.** It is what is left once the oldest were assigned or exercised. So the lots assigned are the same whichever row is matched first. That matters because the two rows need not come together or in order: one may arrive a poll after the other, and nothing Alpaca publishes says an assignment and the expiry it belongs with carry one date.
- **An expiry of a contract held on both sides is refused.** This repo takes an account's position in a contract to be one net figure: what was bought less what was sold. Nothing Alpaca publishes shows an expiry of a contract held both ways, so this is an inference. On it, when one bot has sold a contract another has bought, an expiry's row can only be that net, and no count of it says how many contracts each side lost. Nothing is matched, and the refusal says so by name. Whether Alpaca lets one account hold a contract both ways at all is not known.
- **An expiry closes the side its sign names, or the only side held.** When lots are held on one side only, an expiry signed for the other side still closes them. Every expiry Alpaca's pages show is signed negative, which this repo reads as a bought option expiring; neither page says which side expired. So the sign of a sold option's expiry is an inference, and the books hold nothing else the row could mean.
- **The lots taken hold exactly the activity's contracts, or nothing is matched.** The refusal says which thing is wrong: nobody holds the contract, every lot is on the other side, an expiry finds the contract held on both sides, the lots hold fewer contracts than the activity names, or the cash is not whole cents.
- **A lot opened on a later New York date than the activity is passed over.** It did not exist when the event happened.
- **The rows of one read are matched in one fixed order:** the oldest date first, on one date an expiry last, then by id. A read returns the newest first; the order makes the same rows give the same allocation however they arrived. It does not decide which bot is assigned, and it cannot place a row that arrives in a later read.
- **The rule chooses among the lots it is given.** A holding of the contract that is on no bot's books, such as one the owner opened by hand, must be offered as a lot of its own; otherwise an event that took it is matched to a bot's lot.

**What the books then show**

- **The lot closes at a price of zero.** The premium paid or received when it opened stays in the lot's cost and is realised there. Alpaca's own activity removes the option position with a net amount of zero.
- **Shares and cash come from the contract's terms.** An exercised call or an assigned put buys multiplier × contracts shares at the strike; an exercised put or an assigned call sells them. The cash is strike × multiplier × contracts, in whole cents. Cash that is not whole cents is refused by name and never rounded: the event's total first, before any lot is looked at, and then any lot's part of a whole total that is not whole itself. Alpaca reports the same trade as an activity of its own (`OPTRD`), on the underlying's symbol. That row is evidence the books' figures are checked against, not their source: where it differs from the contract's terms, the difference is named.
- **Shares sold are sold whether or not they were held.** A call assigned to a bot with no shares leaves that bot short the stock, and its books show the short position.

The Clerk calls it from decision 22. No account has been assigned, has exercised or has let an option expire under this code: every shape is Alpaca's published documentation, read 2026-10-06.

### 15. The Clerk takes one bought option, and works its entry out from facts

**Who takes an option plan today.** A Dry Run's account, a Live Shadow (decision 25) and a Paper account, and only a plan of one bought call or put. A plan with a sold leg, or with more than one leg, is refused by name. A Live account refuses to register one and refuses an ENTER while one setting is off, which it is unless a deployment turns it on. It still takes an EXIT, which closes what its books hold (decision 24). The books do not record an account's mode, so that refusal is the facade's, asked at registration and again at every effect. Beneath it are two more fences that know their own account: a Live Shadow's port holds no Alpaca client at all, and the Alpaca port refuses to send one with a leg that opens on a live binding while that same setting is off (decision 19). Nothing about option orders has been checked against Alpaca.

- An option bot is registered under its underlying, on a Dry Run and on Paper alike. Its budget is committed with no minimum position cost: what one option position costs is known only from its contract's quotes, at each entry.

**The caller hands over facts, never figures.** An option entry cannot be rebuilt from the Action Plan inside the Clerk: its contract was picked from quotes. So the caller hands over, for each leg, the contract picked, its listed multiplier and the quote read just before, with the generation those quotes were read in and the bot's two limits. It hands over no price, no commitment and no fee. The money a bot sets aside must be the Clerk's own arithmetic: a caller that could hand over a commitment could hand over a wrong one.

From those facts the Clerk asks, in order. The first "no" refuses the entry with its own reason code, and nothing is sent:

1. the plan is one it takes;
2. the facts cover exactly the plan's leg, and each is what it must be: two limits that are percents of zero or more, a generation and a read time that are whole numbers, and the owner's order terms inside their bounds (decision 19);
3. the contract is a standard one, with a multiplier of 100. The multiplier is the caller's word until the broker's listing is read (decision 11), so no other is taken on it;
4. the contract's last session has not ended, each quote is usable at the Clerk's own clock (decision 8's tests, with the 5,000 ms decision-data budget of ADR 0067), and each side is priced in whole cents, as an order's price must be;
5. the entry's fee provision, from the option fee model: its own fills settled as a day of their own;
6. the structure check, each leg's bid-ask gap against the owner's limit, the natural limit price, the money, and the round-trip cost against the owner's limit;
7. the entry can still be seen through: it is not past the entry cutoff of the close its position would owe that day (decision 16).

- **Only a fault in what was handed over is told as incomplete evidence.** Anything else that fails while the money is worked out is a fault of the code and is raised as one.
- **The leg gap limit.** An entry is refused when any leg's ask less its bid is more than the owner's percent of that leg's mid, compared exactly. It is asked after the structure check and before the money, so the first "no" is the leg's own market. The starting value is 8. A leg can pass it and the structure still cost most of itself to trade, which is why the structure cost limit (decision 5) is asked as well.
- **A contract has expired when its last session has ended.** That is the calendar's session close on its expiry date, a half day's included, and the close of the session before when the date has none. It is not the expiry anchor's 16:00, and not the end of the date. From then on nothing is bought in it and nothing is sold (decision 16).
- **The quote is tested again just before the order is sent.** An entry is worked out under the intake lock and its order goes out after it, and a simulation fills from the quote it is handed whatever its age. So step 4's tests of the quote are asked once more, by the same function, at the clock the order is sent at, with each side's age counted then. A quote that fails them is refused under its own fault code: nothing is sent, and what was set aside is free again, as after any refusal before the send.
- **The Clerk tests the quotes it is handed;** it does not read them again itself. The two limits come with each call, from the one place the runner reads a bot's order terms (decision 21).

**An option entry is the stock ENTER's own fact with one more key.** It is recorded under `ENTER_ACCEPTED` and its close under `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED`. No transition kind is added.

- The ENTER's identity hash is taken over `{account, bot, decision, "ENTER", option_order: <the order>}`. The key is `option_order`, never `leg`, so an entry in shares and one in a contract cannot hash alike. The idempotency key is the existing one: one entry per bot and decision.
- The facts gain `option`, left out when absent. It holds the order as asked, the contract each leg resolved to with its multiplier, the quote each leg was priced from with both receipt times, the units, the limit price, the money in whole cents with the fee provision and the round-trip cost, both limits, and the Clerk's own admission time.
- The facts' `leg` is then the order's line in the same keys a stock leg has: the contract's symbol, the side, contracts, and the limit a share. It is not a stock leg and cannot be made into one.
- The EXIT's acceptance gains `option_contract`, the OCC symbol it closes, and `option_exit`, the quotes its decision read. Both are left out when absent.
- A stock ENTER's hash and facts are pinned by a test to the values `origin/master` computed before any of this.

**A decision sent again** is answered from the entry already on the books, before anything is judged again: its quotes have aged since, and a refusal would deny a position the bot may hold. Its price is expected to differ. A decision sent again for another contract, or another number of structures, is a second request under the first one's name: the same durable conflict it is for an entry in shares.

**An option entry passes the fences an entry in shares passes, and they are one function.** The bot is registered, the run is the bot's active one, nothing holds new exposure for it, a budgeted account has a commitment for it, and a budgeted deployment has its envelope. Then the account is on budgets, its reading is current, and the budget rule is asked: one function, told only what the entry requires. For shares that is the notional plus the stock fee provision. For an option it is the entry commitment in exact cents.

**The contract tables are written where the order row is** (decision 13 left this to come). The fold that creates an option order's row records the contract's terms and binds the order to it: the ENTER's fold from its facts, and the reducing order's fold whenever the symbol it reduces is a contract the books have recorded. The second covers every maker of a reducing order (a deciding program's EXIT, an ended run's close, the flatten, the watchdog's re-drive) without any of them having to remember it. That fold reads only the symbol of its fact. An entry in a contract the books hold with another multiplier is refused with a receipt, and nothing is written.

**The reservation counts by the contract's multiplier.** The reservation row keeps contracts and the premium a share; its claim, and the cost of a fill the cash reading has not seen, are counted with the contract's multiplier read through the order (decision 13). For a bought option, whose requirement is zero, that claim plus the fee provision is the commitment. A sold leg's requirement has no place in the reservation yet.

### 16. How a Dry Run fills, marks and closes an option position

- **The simulation takes an option order of one leg**, and can fill an opening order of several legs under decision 27. A shadow world's order book continues taking one leg only (decision 25). Both use the same one-leg fill.
- **The Clerk binds a quote to the order's id before it sends.** An opening order is filled from the quote its limit was worked out from. With no quote bound it is refused definitively: **the simulation invents no fill**.
- **The contract's multiplier goes with an opening order's quote,** as the books record it. The simulation holds no listing and never reads a multiplier off a symbol. It keeps the multiplier with the order, and states the position's cost and value in dollars by it: one contract bought at $1.94 a share costs $194.00, at an average entry price still of $1.94. An opening order that came with none is refused.
- **A fill is at once, in full, at the natural price.** The order's record keeps the order, the quote with both receipt times, the model and the quote's age.
- **A closing order never opens a position.** The simulation refuses a closing order for more than it holds, and one for a contract it does not hold.

**The quotes kept.** The quotes kept for a Dry Run's contracts are beside its simulated orders: an entry's as its order is sent, the mark the Clerk's own keeping states of each contract a bot holds, and what a held contract's line shows while one of its sides has still to arrive (decision 21). An exit decision's quote reaches the file only when its close is priced: an exit held for the open, or refused before it is priced, has kept none there. One refused as unpriced has kept its quote: that is how a quote with no bid becomes the newest. Two questions are asked of them.

- *Which is the newest?* By receipt time. A close with no usable quote of its own, and the close of an ended run, are priced from it and say so with its age. A quote stamped later than the Clerk's own clock prices nothing. The newest quote alone prices a close: when the side the close fills at has not arrived on it, or has nothing there, the close is not priced, whatever an older quote had on that side.
- *What was last stated to be the newest held?* By hand-over time. The simulated account marks an open contract at that quote's bid, while the hand-over is no older than a share bar may be, and only when the quote is whole: a quote with one side is never a mark, not even for its bid. It is passed over, so the whole quote stated before it stands for as long as any statement does. A quiet contract's bid can stand for minutes, so it is the hand-over that must be fresh. What may be stated is decision 21's. A mark ends with the contract's last session: from that close on the reading is the expired one, however lately a bid was handed over, and a reading taken before it is good no longer than the contract trades.
- **What is kept is bounded.** A hand-over every few seconds would grow the file without end. It is cut back as it grows to what a later reader can still be answered from: for each contract the newest quote by receipt whatever its sides, the last one handed over, and the last one handed over before each session's close, which is the mark a contract held overnight starts the next day from. The cut is made as of the writer's own clock, never as of a stamp in the file: a quote the Clerk puts back from its own facts is stamped at its own receipt, which can be ahead of the clock, and putting one back never cuts. What the file holds is also held in memory and read again only when the file has changed or been replaced by another, so a mark is never a scan of it. A fill keeps the quote it used in its own record; the kept row it cites may since have been cut.
- A file whose whole line is not a kept quote makes the quotes unavailable by name. Nothing is priced or marked from it.

**An option position is closed by its entry's own contract,** read from the entry's facts, in one market order with a closing intent, sized from the position confirmed when the order is made. The plan's rules are never asked again.

- One EXIT closes one contract. An EXIT of a bot that holds several is refused by name: closing one and answering "flat" would leave the others open.
- An exit's quotes are named by their contract's symbol, as the quotes kept are. A quote for another contract is refused.

**Who waits for the regular session, and who does not.**

- An order that closes an option position is sent only in the regular session. Every EXIT of an option position is recorded as regular-session-only, whoever made it, and takes no priced leg: a limit price for one is refused by name. Outside the session a deciding program's EXIT is held and sent at the next open, and the Clerk's own retry of a failed close waits for it. The hold has its own reason, `OPTION_CLOSE_WAITS_FOR_OPEN`, so that it is never told, or listed on the panel, as a bot that reached its scheduled end.
- **A Dry Run's close of what an ended run left sells at any hour,** as it does for shares (owner decision 2026-09-29): at the newest quote kept, saying that quote's age. It is the simulation tidying its own books, not an order a venue would take after hours. The hold's words say only what is true of the order they are about.

**The operator's Flatten of an option position** is priced as every other close of one is: from the newest quote kept for the contract. It sells exactly what is held, once.

- Whether a contract can be closed now is answered from its kept quotes: the newest one received by now has something on the side the close fills at, a bid above zero for a sale. The stock quote source is never asked about a contract, so a contract never joins the shares the IBKR supervisor watches.
- The Flatten is not offered while it cannot work: the contract has expired, the regular session is closed, or nothing kept prices it. Pressed anyway from an older plan, it is refused by name before any EXIT exists.
- A Flatten whose close is not taken after that check, because the bid went or the contract expired between the check and the sale, is told under the refusal's own code in the refuser's own words. It is never told as a broker rejecting an order, and an expired one sends the owner to no retry.
- A newer quote with a bid makes the next Flatten sell. The Clerk's own keeping states one, with no runner alive (decision 21). It also writes down a line that has lost its bid, so a Flatten is not offered at a bid the line no longer shows.

**What the books cannot hold is refused, not approximated.** A fill needs a price above zero. So a close with nothing kept to price it from is not sent, and a close of a contract nobody bids for is not recorded as a sale for nothing: each is refused with its reason and the position stays open. The Clerk retries three times in regular hours and then stops; the Flatten remains. A scheduled close is retried on a rule of its own (decision 20). The PRD's "unpriced, left out of P&L with a flag" is not built.

**A contract past its last session is neither bought nor sold.** What it is then worth is its exercise value, which no kept quote states. So nothing is priced from one.

- A close of such a contract is refused under `OPTION_CONTRACT_EXPIRED` before anything is priced: a deciding program's EXIT, an ended run's close, the Clerk's retry, the Flatten, and an EXIT held since before the expiry. It is judged where the order would arrive, as the session is, so a close too late to arrive is refused and never held for an open that will not come.
- Two fences. The Clerk's is asked first. The simulation's own refuses to bind a quote to such a close and refuses an order in such a contract.
- The position stays on the books. The notice names the contract, says it expired, and says it stays on the books until it is settled (decision 22): by the broker's own activity on a Paper account, by the simulation's settlement on a Dry Run. The Clerk then holds: it makes no EXIT and spends no retry on a contract that can never trade again, and it never gives up on it either.
- **That is so whichever road led there.** A close can fail before the expiry: a worthless contract has no bid. Its notices were then written while a retry or a flatten could still close it, and a stopped bot makes no later EXIT that would correct them. So the Clerk's first pass after the expiry rewrites the bot's open `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` and `EXIT_STUCK` notices once, in the same words, and later passes write nothing. The bot's page does not wait for that pass, because a Dry Run's Clerk makes none on a timer: from the moment of expiry it reads each of those notices in the expired words, and the expired hold over "stuck".
- **The owner's write-off is never offered for an expired contract, on any account.** A write-off takes the position off the books and leaves its lot open, so what the contract cost would never be a loss. It reads as unavailable for that reason from the moment of expiry, whether or not Reconcile now was pressed: never as available and then refused. Neither the notice nor the write-off sends the owner to a flatten. An expired contract is settled by decision 22, and one that nothing will settle is ended at the owner's word there.
- **A Dry Run whose simulation kept no price of the underlying at the close of the contract's last session settles nothing** (decision 22). No price comes by itself: only a running bot keeps bars, and a bot that holds the contract cannot be started. The contract cannot be flattened or written off, and the account's reading stays unavailable. The notice says so, and says what the owner can do: end the contract at their word. A Dry Run's contract is closed before its last session ends by its scheduled close (decision 20); it is held past that session only when that close could not be made.

**The deadlines an option position is held to.** Each is counted back from a session close the calendar gives, so a half day's are a half day's.

- The **close deadline** of a contract is the session close on its expiry date less the expiry close lead, which starts at 45 minutes.
- The **entry cutoff** is the close deadline less the entry wait (how long a resting entry waits for its fill, starting at 20 seconds), the allowance for proving a cancel (30 seconds) and a margin (30 seconds). It is the lead decision 8's resolver is given.
- **The Clerk asks the entry cutoff itself, at its own clock:** when it works an entry out, and again just before the order is sent. From the cutoff on an entry is refused under `OPTION_CONTRACT_PAST_ENTRY_CUTOFF`, the code the resolver's recheck refuses under, and nothing is set aside. The recheck asks at the runner's moment, and the order is sent later. Nothing is sold after a contract's last session, so an entry admitted seconds before it could never be closed again. The cutoff is counted from the close the position would owe that day, so a contract expiring another day has none. It is asked with the entry's own order terms, which come with the entry and are kept with it (decision 19); an entry accepted before entries kept theirs is held to the starting terms. A bot's runner hands the Clerk the terms its own recheck asked under, so the two ask one cutoff at two clocks (decision 21).
- A structure with a sold leg owes a close every session it is held, the sold-leg close lead before that session's close; the lead starts at 10 minutes. When that close and the expiry-day one fall on the same day, the earlier binds.
- The entry cutoff is asked, by the resolver and by the Clerk. The closes are taken by the Clerk itself (decision 20).

**A simulated account's option fees.** A simulated account observes no fee. Its option fills are charged by the option fee model: each of the five fee types summed over the day's option fills, rounded up to the cent, and shared among the bots by the one largest-remainder rule. A simulated day that also traded stock rounds the two populations apart, which is never less than rounding them together; how Alpaca rounds such a day has not been seen. A real account's option fills are an estimate by the same model until a fee row of their day is seen (decision 19). (Amended by #3306: OCC is rounded per fill, as decision 13 now says.)

### 17. A preview is the resolver's answer on the quotes held, and nothing else

Deploy and the structure builder show what a structure's rules would pick right now and what that would cost. One read on the Clerk gives them every figure and every sentence about money. It needs no bot, asks the broker nothing and changes nothing.

- **It is the resolver's answer.** The picks are `resolve_contracts`'s (decision 8), on the quotes the Clerk's supervisor holds (decision 9). Every way it can fail to pick is that function's own typed refusal, or one of the states the preview names: no quote source in this process, the feed not up, no quote line, the listing not read yet, quotes still arriving, no usable quote for the underlying, pricing inputs not known, the search not done. Only a resolution carries figures.
- **It has no decision bar.** A bot counts strikes from the close of the bar it decided on. A preview counts from the underlying's live mid quote, and its answer says so. The entry cutoff it picks under is the one the owner's own entry wait and expiry-day close make: the request carries both, and each starts at its default.
- **It asks for quotes as a page does, on a lease.** Its window goes on the watch list in the lowest class, for a minute, and every ask renews it. A page that goes away stops holding lines. A plan the Action Plan's rules refuse asks for no line at all.
- **A page asks IBKR for nothing while no line is spare for it.** A page comes after the contracts bots hold and the windows they watch. With every line taken by those, the supervisor does not quote the page's underlying and does not read its chain on the page's account, and the preview says there is no line, in words that say the count is this system's own. A bot's window that is not known yet, because its chain or its price is still being read, is counted at the most lines it can come to, so a page is not let in on room a starting bot is about to take. A bot's claim on its own underlying is untouched. A page kept out because bots hold every line IBKR is known to grant (decision 9's ceiling) counts as waiting for capacity: one more line is tried once a minute while it asks, as for a bot's contract, and its answer tells it to ask again. It is told that no line is free and that one more is asked for, not that IBKR refused it a line: IBKR was asked nothing on its account. Without this a ceiling learned long ago kept a page out until the next reconnect, with IBKR's lines long since given back.
- **Four pages may hold windows.** A fifth takes the one asked for longest ago, which is most likely a page that was closed and could not say so. The page that gave way is told that too many previews are open. While it keeps asking and all four are still taken it takes nobody else's, so the same pages do not turn each other out ask after ask. It is let in up to a minute after one of the four stops asking: a page's request stands for a minute after its last ask.
- **Four previews wait on the rate and the dividends at once.** One more is told at once that they are still being looked up, and asks again. On a fleet Clerk a preview is a POST, so it is worked on the capacity kept for commands (ADR 0062): with this bound, and one search running and one waiting, pages can take up six of those places and no more.
- **A Clerk that reads its market status from a shared source has no option quotes.** Option contracts are quoted by the supervisor's own pass (decision 9), which such a Clerk never makes. A preview there says there is no quote source, and an option bot's run is refused there as it starts (decision 21).
- **"Quotes are arriving" is said for ten seconds.** A window just asked for has no quotes yet. After ten seconds the same condition is reported as what it is: the resolver's own refusal, or no usable quote for the underlying.
- **Nothing stops at the first "no".** An entry refuses a structure whose round-trip cost is over the owner's limit (decision 5), or with a leg quoted wider than the owner's gap limit. A preview shows its figures and says which. Picks that are not the named structure are shown with the rule they break; their money is shown when it can be worked out.
- **One function works the figures out for an entry and for a preview** (`app/options/entry.py::entry_figures`). It asks an entry's questions in an entry's order and returns every refusal, never raising one. An entry raises the first, and asks nothing after it. A preview is shown them all: each leg over the gap limit, by name, the money's own refusals, and the cost limit. So an entry is never skipped for a reason the preview had no way to show. The answer lists them as an entry would meet them.
- **A gap or a cost over its limit is flagged, and is not a red verdict.** Both pass by themselves as the market moves. Red is kept for picks that are not the structure built, and for money that cannot be set aside.
- **The Clerk's entry cutoff is shown too.** The Clerk refuses an entry too close to the close its position would owe that day, and a position with a sold leg owes one every session (decision 16). The resolver knows only a contract's expiry day. So the preview asks the Clerk's own question (`app/options/deadlines.py::entry_cutoff_on`) of the moment its quotes were read, under the close terms the request makes, and lists it last among the entry's refusals, under the Clerk's code. It is flagged and not red: it passes by itself at the next session.
- **An entry's own fee rule has one home.** What an entry sets aside for its fees, each leg filled at the price it is entered at, is `app/broker/alpaca/option_fees.py::entry_fee_provision_cents`. The preview reads it there. The Clerk still works its one bought leg's fees out in its own lines, and a test holds the two equal for that case; they become one call when the Clerk takes several legs.
- **The verdict has two halves.** Whether the structure can be deployed at all is the Action Plan's own validation, and does not wait for quotes. Whether what it resolves to right now is the structure built is decision 7's check and the money's refusals. The second half is not known, and not red, when nothing was resolved. When the picks are the structure built and an entry would still be skipped, for a leg quoted too wide, a cost over its limit or a moment past the entry cutoff, the second half says so. It never says the structure can be entered beside an entry that would be skipped.
- **Three more figures at expiry, from the one payoff.** The requirement's exercise payoff (decision 3) also gives the most the structure gains, or that its gain has no limit; the underlying prices its lowest and highest results hold at; and the prices it breaks even at. They carry the loss estimate's assumptions and are not estimated for mixed expiries (decision 4). A break-even is an exact fraction: a leg ratio of three puts one where no cent is. The gain is held at zero, so a gain of nothing is said two ways: where some price brings the entry level, "gain up to $0.00"; where none does, that it loses at every price at expiry. Only the words differ. The figure keeps its definition.
- **What it leans.** The net delta is the sum over the legs of sign × ratio × our own delta (decision 6). Times the multiplier it is the shares of the underlying the structure moves like. A net delta under 0.05 either way is called about flat: that is a delta rule's own default tolerance. A structure that leans down gains when the underlying falls, and the preview carries the warning; it refuses nothing. What a day takes from the structure is shown beside it (decision 23).
- **Every sentence is the server's, and states the figure beside it.** A cost or a gap over its limit by less than the rounding shown is said to be just over it, never printed as the limit itself. A limit is carried back as the owner would write it: ten, not `1E+1`, and nothing, not `-0`.
- **Every amount of money comes written out in dollars too.** Beside each figure in cents the answer carries the same figure as a screen shows it (`…_usd`), so no screen does arithmetic on money. That holds for each point of the line at expiry as well, which is written with a minus sign where it is a loss. A premium is written without a sign, with a word that says paid or received. No sentence counts in cents: the money's own refusal does, and is turned into dollars on its way out.
- **A leg is named by its place.** The builder numbers its rows. The plan's rules, the resolver and the structure check name a leg by its id, because an id is what they are handed, and their words are kept with an entry as they were said. So the preview turns each such sentence on its way out: a quoted id becomes "leg 2", by the leg's place in the request. Typed fields keep the id. Two things are refused up front, by place, because the plan's own words for them carry an id nobody was shown: two legs given one id (each shared id is said with its own legs), and a leg measured from a leg that is not in the request. A leg whose id the plan cannot carry is refused by the plan itself, and its words are turned on the way out like any other.
- **A number nobody meant is said by its length.** A step of the ladder and a number of days have no top, so a rule can carry a number of hundreds of digits, and the resolver says it back. Any whole number of more than fifteen digits is said as "a number of 401 digits", in what was asked and in every refusal. A fraction is said as it was typed, however long. The resolver's other words are its own and are left: they carry contract codes and ISO dates.
- **What was asked is said back in words.** Each leg's rules as one line ("1 call · nearest 0.50 delta, within 0.05 · nearest weekly"), the structure's name as a list writes it and as a sentence takes it before its underlying ("a bull call spread on"), how far the search reached, how old the oldest quote was, and how the bot's orders go in one line.
- **The line at expiry comes as points.** The result at each strike, and at a whole-dollar price either side of the strikes and the break-evens, each in cents and in dollars. Between two neighbouring points the result is a straight line, so a screen draws it and computes nothing.
- **A preview's figures are good for a minute, counted from the read of their quotes.** They are a reading of one moment. The answer says when the quotes were read, until when its figures may be shown as they are, and the same span as a length of time for a page that will not trust its clock against the server's. Past that a page shows when the quotes were read in place of the figures, or asks again. A minute is also how long a page's request for quotes stands (the lease), but the two are not one span: the lease is counted from the ask, and the figures' minute from the second read of the supervisor, which comes after the rate and the dividends were looked up. So the figures' minute ends up to a few seconds after the lease does. The minute says how long figures may be shown. It does not say an entry could be made on them: an entry needs quotes no older than five seconds. The owner may want it shorter; it is one constant.
- **A percent the owner sets is no finer than a millionth of a percent.** The cost limit is compared exactly, in that unit. A request says the gap limit as a number or leaves it out, which is the limit an owner starts from. Null is neither, and is malformed.
- **What each number may be is said once, served, and refused in words.** Every number an owner types has a range: each rule's value and its "Within", a leg's quantity, the structures, the four order terms. A rule's ranges are read off the Action Plan's own fields, with the two its validators hold in code said from the same constants (a dollar distance: whole thousandths, under the largest strike a symbol writes; a fixed date: a year a symbol can write). The order terms' ranges are stated once, in `app/options/order_terms.py`, and the request's fields are built from them. The catalogue serves every range, so a form can refuse a number before it asks. A test sends a number each side of every bound through the route, so what is served cannot drift from what is enforced. A number's range is served as two exact decimals. The fixed date's range is two moments, and is served as two `int64 ms UTC` numbers in fields of their own (`min_ms`, `max_ms`), never as text: a time on the wire is a number (ADR 0022).
- **A number outside its range is an answer, not a 422.** The request's own validation still refuses it. When every fault in a request is a number outside the range the catalogue gives for its box, the preview answers as it does for a structure that cannot be deployed: which box, what was typed, what the box takes, with the leg named by its place. The number refused is said back as its default. Its legs and its order terms are not said back: it is no request, and is not typed as one. Any other malformed request, a missing field, a wrong type, a name nobody defined, stays a 422.
- **What was sent is read a second time there, so nothing there may fail on it.** The request's validation guards against a number too large to hold. The second reading only constructs a number, compares it and reads its digits. It never rounds one or writes one out. A number sent as text with an exponent past what the decimal context holds, such as `"1e1000000"`, once raised there in a box with no top. A test types such numbers into every box.
- **The order terms' ranges.** A gap limit above zero and up to 100; a cost limit from zero to 100; an entry wait of 1 to 30 seconds; an expiry-day close 0 to 390 minutes before the close. `app/options/order_terms.py` states these beside the Clerk's own limits (decision 19), and the Clerk reads its limits from there, so the two cannot drift apart. No range an owner is offered is wider than the Clerk's, and a test asks the Clerk's own check at each end. The entry wait offered is narrower than the Clerk's second to five minutes, by the coordinator's choice, for the owner to confirm: the bot's runner is to learn what an entry came to within fifty seconds of its decision bar's close, and the wait and the proof of a cancel both come out of that fifty, so a longer wait leaves too little time to take the entry. The runner's wait is the resting-entry work's and is not on this path; until a test holds the two together, thirty is a choice and not a proof. The catalogue serves the reason beside the range, and a wait refused for being above it is told the same. A step of the ladder and a number of days have no top the Action Plan holds them to, and the catalogue says so.
- **What How tells every option bot comes with the catalogue.** Entries are limit orders that wait; exits are market orders with no price limit; regular hours only; and what Alpaca's documentation says of an expiring position. That last sentence says it is the documentation's word and that it has not yet been seen on this account. A preview says where the owner's own expiry-day close falls against the hour Alpaca names. The owner's close is counted back from the session's close. Alpaca's hour is counted back from "expiry", in its own words, and on a day that closes early the two may not be one moment. So the comparison is made for an hour that ends when the session closes, and the sentence says that it is.
- **Fees are the entry's own, and an estimate.** Each leg filled at the price it is entered at, settled as a day of its own (the option fee model). A day with no pinned rate has no estimate, and nothing is said to be set aside.
- **The builder's lists come with the catalogue.** The read that gives the named structures also gives the strike rules and expiry rules the builder offers, each with its label, the unit of its value and its tolerance, and the four option order terms a Deploy starts from. A distance from another leg also names the field that says which leg, so every field the Action Plan's rule requires is one the catalogue gives, and a test builds every rule from the catalogue alone. A tolerance is the Action Plan's own default for the rule, and an order term is the default kept beside the code that enforces it: the entry's own limits and its close terms. A preview holds a structure to all four as asked: each leg to the gap limit, the cost to its limit, and the picks to the entry cutoff the two times make. A Dry Run's option bot runs under the defaults until Deploy stores a bot's own (decision 21). The catalogue keeps no second copy of either. It offers a strike by delta, by steps from the money, by distance from another leg and by percent from the price; a plan may also name one strike outright, which the builder does not offer.
- **Not done by this decision:** the account's approval and option buying power; the warning that a strategy is untested on options, and its acknowledgement; anything the Deploy request itself carries; the label of a structure on a bot's row. An entry on a real account is judged again at its own moment: nothing a preview showed is carried into one.

### 18. A search runs in a worker process, on a rate and dividends that say where they came from

**Where a search runs.** A search is the resolver's function (decision 8). A leg picked by delta prices every watched strike of its kind, about 20 ms each; an iron condor on the default window takes about a second. Run in the Clerk's own process it stops the event loop for that long, in a thread or not: the pricer lets go of the interpreter lock for under a microsecond at a time, and a thread waiting for it never gets in. That loop stamps the time each IBKR quote is received, makes the supervisor's pass, and is where bots decide and send. Measured on one machine: with the search in a thread the loop did not run for 932 to 959 ms of a 950 ms preview; with it in a worker process the loop's longest gap was 3 ms.

- **A search never runs in the Clerk's own process.** It runs in a worker process, and the loop only awaits its answer. Outside the resolver's own package nothing calls the resolver but the worker, and a test holds the code to that. The picks' daily decay is priced in the same pass, in the worker, and the same test holds it there (decision 23).
- **Two lanes, one worker process each: entry and preview,** so that a bot's entry never waits behind a page. A Dry Run's option entry is searched for in the entry lane (decision 21). A lane runs one search at a time, in the order asked. A worker is spawned, never forked: the Clerk holds sockets, threads and a running loop. It is started the first time its lane is used or warmed, which for a page is when its window is first asked for, and is stopped with the app. A process that never searches never starts one.
- **What crosses is plain data,** and what comes back is the resolver's answer as it made it: a resolution, or its typed refusal with its evidence. A caller's mistake comes back as the error it is.
- **A search that could not be run is not an answer.** A worker that could not be started, was not up twenty seconds after it was started, died during the search, or had not answered a search after twenty seconds gives a typed "not run": never a refusal of the plan, and never a guess. A worker found gone before anything was handed to it, as one killed while idle, is replaced at once and the search is run. An error the search itself raises is the search's failure, and leaves its worker as it was.
- **A lane holds its worker's process from the moment it is spawned, and never has two.** It reads the process off its pool's own record of what it started; a test holds that record to be there. A worker that is not up in time, or does not answer a search in time, is killed and reaped before another is started: by the search that was waiting for it, or, when none was, by the next use of the lane. A search that comes while a worker is still coming up waits for that worker and starts no other. Closing a lane kills and reaps its worker whatever it is doing, coming up, searching, hung or already dead, so that nothing is left to keep the Clerk's process from ending. Killing and reaping a worker stops the event loop for about three milliseconds, measured, and for two seconds at most.
- **The preview lane has rules of its own.** One search at a time for one page: a second ask while it runs is told the contracts are still being worked out, and no second search is begun. One search running and one waiting for all pages together; a third is told to ask again. An ask waits four seconds for its own search. A search that outlives its ask answers nobody, and the page's next ask searches again on the quotes it reads then. A late answer was first kept five seconds for that next ask; but it stands on quotes read before the search began, and the answer that says the search is not done tells the page to ask again in one second, so its next ask comes more than five seconds after those quotes were read: past the five seconds a pick may stand on (decision 8). A test holds the three numbers to that. A page whose searches take longer than four seconds is therefore shown no figures, only that its contracts are still being worked out.
- **The entry lane's rules are the runner's.** How late an entry may be is judged after the search returns (decision 21).
- **Each lane's worker is a second Python process in the Clerk's container,** with the resolver, the calendar and the pricer loaded: about 160 MB each, measured on macOS and not in the Linux image, and only on a Clerk that previews or trades options. A fleet Clerk's container limits (`compose.fleet.yaml`: 768 MB of memory, one CPU, 256 processes and threads) have not been weighed against it: two workers beside the Clerk share that memory and that one CPU, and a worker that cannot finish loading for want of memory is the likeliest way one never comes up.

**The rate and the dividends.** The American pricer takes a rate and the cash dividends still to go ex, and has no default for either (decision 6).

- **They come with their sources, and unknown is not none.** One function reads them for an underlying at a moment, for a preview now and a bot's entries later. The rate is FRED's Treasury bill rate for the days left to the latest expiry; when FRED gives none the fixed default stands in and is marked as the default. The dividends are the cash dividends declared with an ex-date from today to the latest expiry, each going ex at the open of its ex-date's session; from that moment on it is in the price and is left out. None declared is known, and is not none paid. A lookup that fails, a row that cannot be read, or a process with nowhere to look leaves the dividends unknown: no delta is shown, no lean is worked out, and a rule by delta picks nothing.
- **Each of the two is looked up apart from the other, inside a bound.** They are asked for at the same time, each in a thread kept for it, and a read waits eight seconds for each. A vendor that hangs leaves its own fact not known, with that reason, and takes nothing from the other: dividends Polygon answered at once are known however long FRED takes.
- **One lookup runs for one thing asked,** and none waits behind a lookup of something else: a slow answer about one ticker holds up nobody asking about another. A caller that finds its own lookup running waits for it to its bound. A page, which asks every second, is told at once that it is running.
- **What is kept.** Declared dividends are kept for an hour from when the vendor was read. A rate from FRED is kept for an hour by whoever learned it. What could not be learned, and a rate that is only the default, is asked again half a minute after its lookup ended, or after the read stopped waiting for it. A lookup given up on that learns the fact after all is kept. Only so many answers are kept, and the oldest go first.
- **These lookups run in threads of their own,** never the event loop's default ones, where the Clerk's execution-lease heartbeat runs. The threads are started when first needed and stopped with the app. A lookup given up on at its bound while it still waited for a thread is not begun: its vendor is not asked, late, for an answer nobody waits for.
- **A fleet Clerk asks the coordinator for both.** A Clerk holds no Polygon key (#2204) and is given no FRED key. The coordinator holds both, and answers one internal read beside the chart-history one (ADR 0062): for an underlying at a moment, up to the latest expiry, the rate and the declared dividends still to go ex. It reads them with the same function a process with its own keys uses, so there is one reading of the vendors and one keeping of it in each process. The role decides which a process does: `combined` and `fleet_coordinator` read the vendors, and a Clerk asks. The coordinator's whole lookup ends inside its eight seconds; a Clerk waits twenty.
- **On the Clerk each of the two is still known or says why it is not.** The coordinator answers a rate FRED did not give as the fixed default, marked as the default, with its reason; it answers dividends it could not look up as not known, with their reason, in a shape that carries no list. A coordinator that is not set up, cannot be reached, is late, refuses, fails, or answers what cannot be read leaves the dividends unknown and the rate the fixed default, each with a code and a sentence. Nothing on that path becomes "none declared", and the default rate is never used without saying so.
- **On the wire a moment is `int64 ms UTC` and a number is decimal text.** An expiry is its 16:00 New York anchor, and a dividend's ex-date is the open of that date's session. A rate or an amount is written as the shortest decimal that reads back to the same float, and a JSON number is refused. Every field carries a fact the other side could not work out.
- **What a Clerk keeps of the coordinator's answer.** The dividends only for what is left of their hour. A rate from FRED for an hour of its own, so a Clerk's rate can be up to two hours from FRED; FRED publishes once a day.
- **An ask that will not wait is not made to, at either end.** The Clerk tells the coordinator whether its caller waits. An ask that does not is answered at once that a lookup is running, and stacks no thread on the coordinator. A bot's entry waits: on a Clerk, for the coordinator's answer, twenty seconds at most.

### 19. An entry rests, and the strategy is settled on what is proven

**Amended by #2976 (2026-10-09):** the runner supplies its proof deadline; the Clerk judges the entry’s exact cancel time at acceptance and judges a past outcome limit immediately.

An option entry is a limit order that waits at Alpaca. Nothing waited before: every order was a market order, and the strategy counted itself in the moment the Clerk took the order.

**The deadline**

- **The wait has a deadline, stored with the entry.** It is the Clerk's own clock when it admitted the entry, plus the owner's entry wait. The entry's acceptance keeps it as `cancel_at_ms`, with the owner's order terms it came from. Both are left out of an entry accepted before entries could rest, so that entry hashes as it did. The terms come with each entry from the caller; with none given they are the defaults (a 20-second wait). A bot's runner hands its own with every entry (decision 21).
- **The terms are bounded where the Clerk takes them.** An entry wait is one second to five minutes. No other term is longer than a regular session. The limits are stated once, in `app/options/order_terms.py`, beside the narrower ranges an owner is offered (decision 17), and the Clerk reads them from there. An entry whose terms are outside those is refused by name, as evidence that is not what it must be, and never by an error out of the entry's own record.
- **The Clerk cancels what has not filled, with a task of its own.** The deadline keeper reads from the books which entries are past their wait and asks Alpaca to cancel each. It keeps no list, so nothing is lost with it. The reconciliation pass runs the same step on every turn: a keeper that died makes a cancel late by one pass and never loses it. The keeper starts no pass, so no other bot's entry is refused while it works.
- **What is cancelled is any option entry the books show working at Alpaca past its deadline, whatever its ENTER's state.** An ENTER voided as never sent whose order Alpaca later shows (the abandoned send landed) is cancelled like any other: its order can still fill. The worklist is the stopped-run step's own predicate with a deadline where that step has a stopped run, one definition in `reads.py`. It also lists an entry Alpaca has not answered about yet, under an ENTER that has not ended, whose turn at the deadline is a lookup.
- **The worklist is read from the orders bound to a contract, once a round.** A stock-only account's custody log is never read by it, however long. What is due and when to wake both come from that one read.
- **Each due entry is handled by itself, four at once, the oldest deadline first.** Each is cancelled and looked up under the operation claim of the effect it acts under. One that is slow does not hold the others. One that raises is logged and tried again next round. Every entry of one Clerk goes to the same broker, so no more than four are asked about at one time. The cancel is asked for within two seconds of the deadline for the first four entries due at once. A fifth waits for a place, the oldest deadline first. A place is free as soon as any one of the four has its answer, and each call is bounded by the Alpaca client's own timeout. So with more than four due together, a cancel can be asked for later than two seconds after its deadline, and its order can fill meanwhile.
- **A cancel that stays unproven is asked again every second for ten seconds, then every five.** An attempt that tells the books nothing new writes nothing, and the order stays on the worklist until a lookup shows it ended. **"Recorded once" is true of two cases only:** the keeper working alone, and a cancel Alpaca refuses while its lookup still answers. There the first record stands. While passes run and the lookup fails it is not true: "nothing new" is then read off the last record written for the order, each pass writes records of its own on that order, and so the uncertain cancel is recorded again after each pass, for as long as Alpaca cannot be read. One rule for every caller of the cancel is not built.
- **The deadline is a time on the Clerk's wall clock and is judged on it.** No other clock survives a restart. A clock set back to before an entry's own admission makes the entry due at once: an early cancel costs a fill, a late one leaves an order in the market past what the owner allowed. A clock set back by less delays the cancel by that much, and by no more than the wait.
- **The keeper always stops.** A stop that lands inside a broker call can come out of it as another error, because the claim is renewed after the call. Whatever is caught, a task that is being cancelled ends.
- **A Clerk that closes cancels every entry still resting.** An order left at Alpaca by a Clerk that has closed has nobody to cancel it at its deadline. So a closing Clerk first finishes every effect it had accepted, with the keeper still keeping its deadlines: an entry that was still being sent has by then reached Alpaca, or has not. Then the keeper ends its own timer and makes one last round. It cancels every option entry on its worklist, whatever is left of its wait, and looks each one up, by the same step and under each order's own claim, with the reason `ENTRY_CLERK_CLOSED`. Only then are the books closed. The last round has three seconds in all, so that an Alpaca that does not answer cannot hold the process's shutdown. Each entry is asked about once. One that Alpaca had not answered about is only found by that ask, because nothing can be cancelled without Alpaca's id for it, so it alone is asked about a second time. What is not proven by then is left as any unproven cancel is left: a lookup that still shows the order working leaves "cancellation requested", and a cancel stopped before its answer leaves "outcome unknown". The next start's stopped-run step, or its keeper's first round, finishes it. No other step of a round runs in the last round: a closing Clerk sends no new order. An entry a live EXIT owns is that EXIT's to cancel, here as in every round. A process that is killed closes nothing, and its resting entry is the restart's to cancel. A Dry Run's Clerk makes no last round, opened for a run or for a read, and nor does a Live Shadow's: neither rests an entry. No order in shares and no closing order is on the worklist.

**What is proven, and what the bot holds**

- **A cancel is proven by looking the order up, never by the answer to the request.** The request is recorded before it is sent, with its reason (`ENTRY_WAIT_ENDED`, or `ENTRY_CLERK_CLOSED` for one the closing Clerk asked for). Until a lookup shows the order ended, it is "cancellation requested". A fill that arrives during or after the cancel is counted by its execution id. The bot holds what Alpaca's final record says.
- **Held means filled.** A part fill leaves the bot holding the contracts that filled. The cash set aside for the rest is claimed while the order has not ended on the books, and is free once it has.
- **One state where the set-aside does not cover everything.** When Alpaca's stream says an order ended and counts a fill the books have not recorded yet, the unfilled remainder's claim is already gone and the unrecorded fill claims nothing until the pass's lookup records it. The entry reads "outcome unknown" meanwhile, so the bot's next entry is held; its cash is not. This is the rule an entry in shares has, unchanged.
- **No second entry is taken beside a working one.** While the books show a bot's option entry order working at Alpaca, that bot's next entry is refused by name (`ENTRY_ORDER_WORKING`), whatever the earlier ENTER's state: a second entry would be a second buy for one signal. Other bots on the account are not held by it. The check asks only about orders bound to a contract. A bot that trades shares has the same gap, for an ENTER voided as never sent whose order later shows, and it is not closed here.
- **What an entry came to is one read of the books** (`entry_outcome`): working, cancellation requested, filled, filled in part, unfilled, never sent, or unknown. An order that has ended while its recorded fills fall short of Alpaca's own count is unknown, never filled. So are books that hold more contracts than the entry asked for.

**The settlement**

- **The settlement is the Clerk's answer, recorded once.** A caller asks `await_entry_outcome` after an option entry is sent. Anything filled: the strategy is in. Proven to have ended with nothing filled, or never sent: the decision is dropped, as a refused entry is. Still unproven when the caller's limit is reached: it is in, because an exit will then come and close whatever turns out to be held. The Clerk records which it was as `ENTRY_SETTLED`, on the entry's own timeline, in the same locked read it was worked out in; asked again, the record answers, whatever the books say by then. A wait that ends only because the entry's own run is no longer the bot's active run is answered and not recorded. The wait holds the intake lock only while it reads, so no other bot waits behind it. Stock entries are not asked.
- **The limit is bounded, and judged only after the books are read.** The recorded answer and a proven outcome are read first. A limit more than a week ahead of the Clerk’s clock is refused for an unproven entry; the bound covers a daily bar across a long weekend. A limit at or before the Clerk’s clock is a request to judge now: the Clerk records the current outcome, including “entered” while still unproven. That answer can be replayed after a restart.

- **What has nothing to wait for.** An entry refused before the Clerk accepted it is not on the books, and asking for it is an error. An entry the Clerk accepted and Alpaca then refused is on the books and is answered: never sent, the decision dropped.
- **Asked after the fact, the record wins, else the books** (`entry_settlement`). With no answer recorded, the rule is applied to the books as they then stand, so two askers on either side of the moment an outcome is proven can be told differently.
- **The bot runner asks after every option entry the Clerk took, and waits inside its own loop** (`bot_trade_strategy._settle_option_entry`, one helper for a Paper account's runner and a Dry Run's).
  - A receipt that is a refusal is dropped without asking, as an entry in shares is. Any other receipt of an option ENTER is asked about, and the staged decision is settled on the answer: entered commits it, discarded drops it. A simulation fills inside the send, so a Dry Run is answered on the first read, with no pause. Both runners do this in one helper (`_settle_option_decision`).
  - **The settlement is always the Clerk's. The runner works out none of its own.** A later Start replays the entry by the Clerk's record, so an answer the runner made up could not be replayed.
  - While it waits the decision stays staged and the run asks its bar stream for no further bar: a run has one bar in flight (`startup_join.LiveStartBuffer`). So no later bar is ever decided on a state that is not settled.
  - **The runner's limit is fifty seconds after its decision bar closed** (`bot_option_run.ENTRY_OUTCOME_WAIT_MS`), whatever the bot's decision timeframe. It is not the close of the next decision bar. While the runner waits nothing reads its bar stream, and the IBKR stream calls a line stalled when its reader has seen no new bar for sixty seconds (`ibkr/bars.py`, `REALTIME_BAR_STALL_TIMEOUT_S`): it then invalidates the line for every bot on that symbol and the feed replaces it. A wait to the next decision bar would be a minute for a one-minute program and longer for every other. A wait asked for on time also ends before the next minute's bar closes. One asked for late does not (the next bullet). What holds either way is that no bar is decided on a state that is not settled. A test holds the two constants ten seconds apart.
  - **A send may outlast the runner’s limit.** The runner passes the earlier of fifty seconds after the decision bar closed and fifty seconds from its current clock. When that limit is past, the Clerk judges immediately and records its answer. This removes the quarter-second extension that could still expire between the runner’s calculation and the Clerk’s first read. A later Start replays the recorded answer, even if the order is subsequently proven unfilled.

  - **After a send that outran the wait, an unproven entry is counted as entered.** The keeper continues to cancel the resting order at its deadline. If it later proves unfilled, the recorded strategy settlement still controls replay.

  - **The limit is never further from now than the wait itself.** It is the earlier of fifty seconds after the bar's close and fifty seconds from the moment of asking. A clock set back while the send was out would otherwise put the limit that much further ahead, with nothing reading the bar stream meanwhile.
  - **The Clerk judges whether this entry can be proven inside the runner’s wait.** The runner hands over “proven by”: fifty seconds after the decision bar closed. Under the same write fence that accepts a fresh entry, the Clerk compares that deadline with the later of its current clock and the entry’s own `cancel_at_ms`, plus seven seconds for cancellation proof. If the sum is later, it refuses under `OPTION_ENTRY_TAKEN_TOO_LATE` before an order or reservation is written. The three-second contract-listing read and any wait for a newer account reading happen before this check, so neither is estimated by the runner. An already accepted decision is answered from its record before the check. The seven seconds are separate from the owner’s cancel allowance, which keeps an entry clear of the contract’s close deadline (decision 16). How quickly a real Alpaca cancellation becomes visible is still unmeasured.

  - **An entry can still be unproven when the wait ends,** when Alpaca is slow to show a cancel as ended or does not answer. It is then settled as entered. If it ends unfilled, the strategy counts a position the bot does not hold until its own exit comes. That exit cancels the entry if it still rests, proves that nothing was bought, sends no sale, and the next signal can enter.
  - A wait that ends because the run stopped is settled on its answer, which the Clerk did not record. A runner that is cancelled mid-wait settles nothing; the cancel at the deadline is the keeper's either way.
  - A receipt that is not a refusal whose decision has no option entry on the books is raised, and ends the run: the books contradict the receipt.
- **A replay settles by the same rule.** A run that goes on has always acted on an answer the Clerk recorded, so a replay reads that same answer back. The one answer that is not recorded is given to a run that had stopped, which sends nothing more. A Start replays the bars before it and reapplies each decision's own disposition. An option bot's ENTER is asked for its settlement (`SqliteDecisionReceipts.option_entry_settlement`, over `entry_settlement`): one settled as discarded is replayed as discarded, though its receipt still reads accepted. Replayed as entered, an unfilled ENTER would put the strategy in a position, stage its exit a few bars on, and that exit could be written down as a decision a crash left undecided. The ENTER still open where the receipts a Start reads begin is asked the same way, so an entry settled as entered before it was proven is replayed as held even though its ENTER later ended failed. A bot in shares is not asked: its receipts are read as they were.
- **A fill on an entry the strategy was told it does not hold.** A fill can arrive after an entry was proven unfilled, or on a voided entry's order before its cancel lands. It is counted: the bot holds the contract. The existing `FAILED_ENTER_FILLED` notice is raised, naming the contract and the order, and the bot's entries are held. The strategy believes it is flat and sends no exit. The owner stops the bot and flattens it, as the notice says; on Paper the Flatten is offered once the bot is stopped and goes out as a market order that sells to close exactly what is held. A close is owed by what is held (decision 20), so on its contract's expiry day the Clerk closes such a position itself. Before that day it waits for the owner.

**Restart, simulation, and which accounts**

- **A restart ends the wait early.** The Clerk stops every run when it starts, and no entry outlives its run, so a resting entry is cancelled at the restart whatever its deadline. The runner that waited is gone and no settlement was recorded, so the next Start's replay settles that entry from the books as they stand: dropped when nothing was bought, entered when something was, entered while nothing proves either.
- **A simulation cannot rest an order.** It fills at once or not at all. Its entry keeps a deadline like any other and is never on the keeper's worklist. A Dry Run's keeper is started only when its store is opened to operate, never for a read.
- **Paper takes one bought option, and so does a Live Shadow (decision 25). Live refuses an opening while its setting is off (decision 24).** A Paper account's close is a market order, sent with no quote of ours. A Paper account's bot reaches all of this through its runner (decision 21), which asks the Clerk whether the account takes its plan before it opens a stream (`require_option_plan_taken`, the rule a bot is registered under).
- **Live has a second fence.** While Live is switched off (decision 24), the Alpaca port refuses to send an option order with a leg that opens on a live binding, before anything is built: definitive, so the Clerk records the order as not sent. An option position opened on Live against the setting therefore needs two independent mistakes. An order whose every leg closes is always sent: a fence against opening never stops a close. The port reads the order's own legs. The fence asks the settings of the client that posts, when that client is bound to some, so a port built around a live client is refused whatever the process's own binding says. Alpaca itself takes option orders on a live account, so the capabilities still say so. Turning the setting on lifts this refusal on purpose.
- **On Paper the picked contract is first asked of Alpaca's own contract reference** (decision 11), outside the Clerk's intake lock: not listed, not the contract asked for, inactive, not tradable, not American, or not a standard contract of a hundred shares, each is refused by name, and so is a listing that could not be read. The read has three seconds, well inside the five the entry's quotes are good for; a listing that takes longer refuses the entry for that, by name. It is not asked at all for an entry that the closed session or an unhealthy channel refuses anyway, and such an entry is never taken without its listing if those answers have changed by the time it is judged. The listing is asked once an attempt, not once a decision: an entry that waits for a newer account reading is judged again from the top, and its listing is asked again, each time inside its own three seconds and outside the lock.
- **A Paper entry's quote is tested again at the send, as a Dry Run's is** (decision 15). Its limit order would rest at the price that quote gave. So a quote that passed when the entry was taken and is past the decision-data budget when its order is reached for sending refuses the entry there, under the quote's own fault code: nothing is sent, nothing stays set aside, and the entry reads never sent. The listing is read before the entry is taken, so its three seconds are spent before the first test of the quote, not between the two.
- **Every answer about an option order is held to the order asked for: its contract, its side and its contracts.** From the send or from a lookup, by the client order id or by Alpaca's own. One that differs in any of the three, or cannot be read, leaves the outcome unknown and is never counted.
- **An answer that states another limit price or another kind of order is taken, and both values are logged.** It is our order, found by our own id, and what it filled is real. Held to the limit and the kind as well, such an answer left a filled entry's bot with an EXIT that stayed unknown: its close was never sent, and no operator action settles an entry's order. The limit is compared as the exact decimal, so `1.94` and `1.940` are one price and nothing is logged for them.
- **One chooser picks the reader.** Every exact lookup of the Clerk's own order goes through it: the option port's strict reader for an order the books bind to a contract on a real account, the stock reader otherwise. That covers a resolution's lookup, the pass's look for a fill on a failed operation, and the operator's attestation. The attestation reads an order id the operator typed with the same reader and refuses another order in its own words.
- **A fill the books cannot hold is withheld, not raised.** A stream frame, or a row of the account's order list, can name another symbol than the contract its order trades, or a fraction of a contract. The write of such a fill is refused where it is written. So each of those two routes asks first, and such an answer is treated as a lost one: nothing of it is recorded, the order's effect folds unknown with what the answer said as the reason, and the exact lookup settles it or leaves it unknown. The stream stays up and the pass goes on.

**Fees on a real account**

- **A day's fee rows are shared among all of that day's fills, each by its own fee model.** An option fill is priced by the option fee model, never the stock model. Until a fee row of its day is seen it carries that model's estimate, and by itself it never leaves the account's fees unresolved. A posted row is shared among the fills of the day it is dated by the one largest-remainder rule, each fill weighted by what its own model predicts: for the row's fee type when it names one, else for all of that model's types together. A row of a type the stock model never predicts (ORF, OCC) then falls on option fills alone. The total is kept to the cent. The share takes the estimate's place and stops claiming cash at the first cash reading after the row, as a stock fill's does. A stock-only account is charged to the cent what it was.
- **The split is the models', the total is Alpaca's.** A share of a row reads as observed, as a stock fill's share of a row does. Nothing on a screen yet says that an option fill's part was shared out by the model; a state that says so is a served change and is not built.
- **No row's fee type is read yet.** The rule takes a type, and is tested with one. The Clerk's activity record does not carry Alpaca's sub-type, so every real row is shared by all types together: on a day with both kinds of fill, a row of a stock fee is shared mostly onto the option fills, whose per-contract fees weigh more.
- **Open, and named.** The fee module's docstring names the six that remain after #3308 closed (b).
  - (a) **A row is shared among the fills of the day it is dated.** If Alpaca dates an option fee by the day it posts, and that day has only stock fills, those stock bots are charged it as observed and nothing reads unresolved. The option fill it was for keeps its estimate, which no row then replaces and which goes on being held back from cash: the fee is counted twice, once on each bot. If that day has no fill at all, the row has no fill to follow and is unowned, which leaves the account's fees unresolved and holds every bot's entries, as such a row always did.
  - (c) **An estimate whose rows never come** goes on being held back from cash, as a stock fill's does; nothing bounds it.
  - (d) **No row's fee type is read** (the bullet above).
  - (e) **Any untyped row, of any size, ends every estimate of its day.** A row of one cent is shared out and leaves no estimate beside it; a row of nothing charges nothing and leaves none either. With (d), that is every real row.
  - (f) **A row dated to a New York day that has not ended is nobody's until midnight,** and holds every bot's entries until then. That is the stock rule, and harmless for stock fees, which post after the close. If Alpaca posts an option fee with the fill, the first option fill of a day stops the account's entries until midnight.
  - (g) **A negative row can never be linked to the charge it refunds,** because the Clerk's fee evidence carries no such link. So a negative row on an option day is nobody's and holds every bot's entries. The stock rule, unchanged; an option rebate would be a new way to meet it.
- **Outside option fills (#3308).** The fee evidence producer reads each contract's Alpaca reference, records its multiplier in the replayable custody stream, and prices the fill with the option fee model. A missing reference leaves account fee admission held. `sell_short` is an option sell for this calculation; equity short-sale activity still fails closed.

**Nothing here has been sent to Alpaca.** The shapes are its published documents. Whether an option order's frames reach the stream in a shape the Clerk reads, how long a cancel takes to show as ended, whether Alpaca states a limit price and an order's kind as they were sent, and how and when Alpaca posts option fees are still to be seen on an account. The runner's side is proven on a stand-in for Alpaca and a stand-in IBKR connection; whether an entry is cancelled and proven inside the runner's fifty seconds on a real account depends on the first of those.

### 20. Scheduled closes belong to the Clerk

A position holding a contract that expires today is closed 45 minutes before the session's close, unless the owner set another lead. A position with a sold leg is closed 10 minutes before every session's close it is held through. Both times are counted back from the calendar's session close (decision 16), so a half day moves them and a day with no session has none.

**The obligation**

- **A close is owed by what is held, not by a run.** Nothing is created when an entry fills, and nothing is stored that could be lost. Each time, the books are read: every option contract any bot holds, the entry that opened it, the order terms kept with that entry, and the calendar. An owner's Stop, a dead runner and a restart change no holding, so they cancel no close.
- **It closes what the strategy does not know it holds.** A fill on an entry already told as unfilled, what filled of an entry cancelled at its wait, what a stopped run left: each is read from the positions, never from the Signal Program's state, and is closed on its day. This closes the gap decision 19 named.
- **The worklist starts from the positions.** One read of the positions that are not zero, each asked of the recorded contracts. A stock-only account asks an empty table, takes no lock, writes nothing, and reads no row of its custody log however long it is.
- **The positions say first whether anything can be owed.** A close is owed on a held contract's expiry day, and every session by a holding with a sold contract. So a bot's entries are read only when it holds a contract that expires today or has stopped trading, or a sold contract. A bot whose contracts are all bought and all expire on a later day costs the same few steps however long its history is. On the day a close is owed the entries behind the holding are read, and that read does grow with the bot's own history.
- **An entry accepted before entries kept their terms is held to the starting terms,** as its cutoff is.
- **This is the second sale the Clerk makes for a run that is not running.** The owner's scheduled end is the first (ADR 0045, as amended).

**Taking the close**

- **It is one more exit.** A close that is due is accepted as a recovery EXIT under `scheduled-close-<yyyymmdd>-<hash of the entry's order>`: one for each holding and session, so asking again drives the same EXIT and never makes a second. Its acceptance records `scheduled_close`: which close it is, when it was due, and the session it was due in, named by its open. The key is left out of every other EXIT's acceptance, and five acceptances of other kinds are pinned to the bytes they had before.
- **It is taken while the run is ACTIVE, behind one fenced check.** In the locked run that accepts it the Clerk asks again: is the close still owed at the Clerk's clock now, is the contract still held, is no other EXIT of the bot working, and has the Clerk not stopped exiting this bot by itself? A deciding program's EXIT is accepted under the same lock. When the two meet, the first is accepted. A scheduled close that comes second is not taken. A program EXIT that comes second is answered from the EXIT already working. One closing order is made either way.
- **A close whose turn comes after its session has closed is not taken.** A close is read as due and then waits its turn. Four are sent at once, so a broker that is slow to answer four holds the fifth, and the lock a close is accepted under can be busy. Its turn can come after the session's close. It is then no longer owed, in that session's name or any other's, and nothing is accepted: no exit fails after trading has ended, and no decision of one session is sent at the next open. The next round or pass reads the contract as held past its close and records the close as missed. A holding with a sold leg owes the next session's close at its own time, as that session's decision.
- **From there it is an option close like any other** (decision 16). A market order in the regular session, sent with no quote on a real account. Held on a halt of the underlying, which is the symbol the halt is asked of, and sent when the halt lifts. Held by a hold on the account that refuses reductions, and tried again. Held too while an order the Clerk did not place stands unsettled in the bot's contract (decision 22): the close is taken and kept, no order of the Clerk's goes out, and it ends with nothing left to sell once that order's fill is booked. Refused, with nothing sent, for a contract past its last session.
- **Where the Clerk has stopped exiting a bot by itself, it takes no scheduled close either.** It cannot know whether its last order traded. The close reads as left to the owner, and is recorded missed.
- **Who acts.** The deadline keeper takes a close when it is due. It sleeps no longer than to the next due time, or to the end of a session a close is owed in. The reconciliation pass runs the same step on every turn, so a keeper that died costs one pass and never a close. On a real account a close once taken is driven on by the pass alone, as every unfinished EXIT is: a keeper that drove it too would look its order up three times as often. A Dry Run has no pass on a timer, so its keeper drives a taken close on.
- **Each step of the keeper's round runs by itself.** The round cancels entries past their wait and then takes closes. A step that fails is logged with its name and the other still runs: an entry that cannot be listed does not keep a close from going out. A lost execution lease ends the round, and so does a stop. The two steps still share the round, so a close that waits on the broker delays the next look at the entries. Once both have had their turn the round starts the read of option events and does not wait for it (decision 22). A step that failed does not keep that read from starting. A lost execution lease does.
- **A Clerk that closes takes no close.** It says it is closing first, before any of its tasks is stopped, under the lock a close is accepted under. Its keeper and its reconciliation loop both go on for a while after that: the Clerk finishes what it was sending, and the keeper makes its last round, which cancels resting entries and sends no new order (decision 19). That round first ends the read of option events the keeper had out, without waiting for the broker's answer, and starts none (decision 22). The loop is stopped only after that round, because its heartbeat keeps the hold on the account the round writes under. Whichever of the two looks in that time takes no close, and the watchdog sends no failed close again. The next start does both. A close accepted before the Clerk began to close is still driven, and the stop can cut it. Cut after it was accepted and before its order was sent, it stays accepted and the next start drives it, as it drives every unfinished EXIT. Cut inside its send, its order is looked up by its own id before anything is sent again. No close is lost and none is sent twice.
- **A restart.** After the deadline with the session open, the restart's own pass takes the close at once. How late it was taken is read from its acceptance: the recorded time less `due_ms`. More than a minute is told as late. After the session has closed nothing is sent, and the close is recorded missed.

**A close that fails**

- **It is tried again every 30 seconds while its own session is open.** The rule is asked of the books, and not of which order failed last: is the contract still held, does it owe a close in the session open now, and did the Clerk accept that close? The stuck-EXIT watchdog asks in its scan and carries the answer through the pass. While it holds, the episode is ready again 30 seconds after it failed, it is not counted out after three tries, and the Clerk does not give the bot up. The bot's own exit failing a second after the close changes none of that. Each re-drive made under the rule records the same close on its own acceptance. Every check a re-drive passes still stands, so a try can be held by one of them and send nothing.
- **The rule is asked once more before a bot is given up.** A pass can find the ordinary tries run out in its scan and take the close a moment later. That close's own rule holds from then on, so the same pass does not give the bot up.
- **What was tried under the rule is not counted afterwards.** A re-drive made under the rule, and the time it failed for, do not count toward giving the bot up. From the next session on the ordinary rule applies, from where its count stood before the close was taken. Usually that count is not full. The close is then sent again at the next regular open, and the bot is given up once three ordinary tries have failed, those made before the close among them. If three ordinary tries had already failed before the close was taken, the count is full. The next session then sends nothing, and the Clerk gives the bot up at its open. The missed-close notice tells the owner of both cases. Each session's close is its own decision.
- **A Dry Run's keeper takes a failed close again.** A Dry Run has no pass on a timer, so nothing else would. The keeper takes it thirty seconds after the bot's newest exit, while the close's own session is open, behind the same fenced check as the first try. Each try is a decision of its own, numbered after the first. One quote with nothing bid at the due instant no longer strands a simulated bot.
- **Two retries are never closer than that wait.** An event can start a pass on a Dry Run, and its watchdog retries a failed close too. So the watchdog's re-drive under the rule is asked the same wait again in the locked run that accepts it: has it been thirty seconds since the bot's newest exit? If the keeper has just made its try, the watchdog holds.
- **A close can be tried twice in one moment.** That is a try of the scheduled close beside a close of another kind, never two retries of the scheduled close. A close of another kind that has just failed leaves the contract held and the scheduled close still owed, so the same pass takes it. On a stopped Dry Run with nothing bid, the run-end close fails and the scheduled close then fails the same way. It happens in the other order too: a pass that read the bot's failed exit as ready, just before the keeper took the scheduled close, sends its own re-drive once that close has failed. Each is accepted only while the contract is still held and no exit of the bot is working, so one sale at most is made.

**A close is not an outcome**

- **A session can end with the contract still held:** a halt that did not lift, a refusal, nothing bid, the Clerk down. That is recorded as `SCHEDULED_CLOSE_MISSED`, a notice on that bot alone. The bot's entries are held, no other bot's are, and every reduction of what it holds stays allowed. The notice names each contract, how many, when its close was due, and what can now happen to it: an exercise, an assignment, a sale by Alpaca, or nothing real on a Dry Run.
- **Missed means still held after a close was owed.** A contract held after its last session, however long ago: a Clerk down for a week still says so. A structure with a sold leg held after the last session's close, when it was already held before that close was due.
- **One module owns the notice.** The step that reads what is held raises it, rewrites it when what it names changes, and ends it once the bot holds none of the contracts it names. Its words hold no clock value, so a later round that finds the same writes nothing. After a flat proof the notice ends on the next round or pass, not in the same write. One bot's notice that cannot be written or ended is logged and tried again on the next run. The other bots' notices are still written.
- **An expired contract also keeps its `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` notice** when a close of it had failed (decision 16). The two say different things: one that a close failed and the contract expired, the other that the Clerk's own deadline passed and what can follow.
- **Nothing is sent for an expired contract.** The bot stays held under its notice. An expired contract leaves the books by decision 22: the broker's record of its end is booked, a Dry Run's simulation settles it, or the owner ends it at their word. The owner's write-off is never offered for one, on any account, though this notice is one a write-off is offered under: decision 22's rule is asked first. The notice ends on the round or pass after the position has left the books. The bot may carry decision 22's `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` beside it, which says what the owner can do.

**What the owner reads**

- **One read says where a close stands** (`scheduled_close_state`): scheduled, sent, partly closed, failed, flat or missed, with when it was due, when it was taken, how late, whether it is left to the owner, and a sentence written with it. A close taken and held, by a halt or a hold, still reads as scheduled. Failed is said while the close's own EXIT has failed and no later try of it is working, whatever other exit of the bot came after, and its sentence says who acts next: the Clerk's next try, another exit of the bot, or the owner. A close that was sent and refused is never told as not sent. Flat is said only of a close whose EXIT proved the bot holds nothing, and its time is when the close was first taken. No route serves it whole yet: the time of a close still owed, or missed, reaches the bot page as a line of its exit estimate (#3100, decision 28).

**Sold legs.** No entry with a sold leg can be made through the Clerk, so a sold leg's close is shown only at the level of the calendar arithmetic, the worklist, the cutoff, and a holding whose sold leg is written by hand in a test. A sold leg is told by a position below zero: a holding whose entry had a sold leg and which no longer holds a sold contract owes no nightly close.

- **Known gaps:**
  - **A contract whose expiry date has no session owes no close.** The close is owed on the expiry date itself, and that date has no session. Such a contract is told as missed when its last session ends. A listed contract should not carry such a date.
  - **The first send of a close is not checked against the account.** It is checked against the books: once an order the Clerk did not place is recorded in the bot's contract and is unsettled, the close's order is not sent (decision 22). Until the Clerk has seen that order, on Alpaca's stream or in a pass, only the order's close intent stands in the way of a second sale.
  - **The owner's write-off is offered under the missed-close notice only for a contract that still trades.** It takes the position off the books and books no result. For an expired contract it is never offered (decision 22). The write-off's own executor does not ask whether the contract expired: the refusal is the recovery policy's, which every served path asks first.
  - **The keeper's two steps share one round.** A close that waits on the broker delays the next look at the resting entries, and a fifth close due in one minute waits for one of four to be answered.
  - **A close first taken in the last five seconds of its session is accepted and not sent.** It is still owed, so it is taken. But no reducing order is sent that would reach the broker after the close, and that guard is five seconds. An expiring contract's close then fails as expired, with its `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` notice. A sold leg's is held and goes out at the next open, as the earlier session's decision. It needs a close taken that late: a restart, or a broker slow to answer, in those seconds.
  - **Only a scheduled close asks whether the Clerk is closing.** A pass that overlaps the shutdown still drives on an exit already accepted, sends a failed exit again under the ordinary rule, sells at an owner's scheduled end and closes what an ended Dry Run left, as it did before this decision.
  - **A count already full before the close gives the bot up at the next open.** Nothing is sent that session. Only a holding with a sold leg has a session after its close, so whether a new session should count from nothing is to be settled when an entry with a sold leg can be made.
  - **The read's failed sentence promises a try about every 30 seconds.** It says so also while every pass is refused before it sends, while passes back off because the broker cannot be read, and for a Dry Run whose Clerk is closed. No route serves the read yet.
  - **One bot holding two contracts under a failed-entry notice has neither closed.** That notice allows a reduction of the one contract it names. If the close of the other contract is taken first, it is refused and stays accepted, and one EXIT works for a bot at a time. So the close that would be allowed is never taken, and both are recorded missed. It needs an entry voided as never sent that fills after all, and a second entry in another contract taken before that fill is seen. A Dry Run fills at once and voids no send. A Paper account's runner now trades an option plan (decision 21), so there the gap can be reached: a send abandoned as never sent whose order landed and filled, after the bot had bought another contract. No test builds it and it has not been seen.

**Nothing here has been sent to Alpaca.** Whether Alpaca takes a market order that closes an option with nothing bid or in a halted underlying, whether it refuses a closing order for more than is held, what its own sale near expiry looks like, and how the keeper keeps time under the real event loop are still to be seen on an account.

### 21. The runner trades one bought option, in a Dry Run and on a Paper account

An option bot in a Dry Run runs end to end: its Signal stream decides, its runner resolves the contract, and its account's Clerk admits, fills and closes (decisions 15 and 16). A Paper account's bot runs the same way through the trade runner: every step below is one code path for both, and what differs is the Clerk (decision 19). A Live Shadow's bot runs through the trade runner too, and nothing in the runner is its own (decision 25). Live refuses an option opening while its setting is off: the Clerk at registration and at every entry (decision 24). Each runner asks the Clerk before it opens a stream or asks for a quote line (`_option_run`), and raises the Clerk's own refusal by name.

**An entry counts when it is settled, not when it is taken.** As first built, the runner counted the strategy in the moment the Clerk took the entry, which was true of a simulation that fills inside the send. An order that rests is not held yet. Both runners now ask the Clerk what the entry came to and settle the strategy on the answer; decision 19 says how long they wait, what each answer does, and how a replay settles the same entry. A Paper run needs its source-bar ledger, as a Dry Run does: the money is read from it.

**The money.** "The money" is the close of the underlying's own bar for the decision's minute.

- A bot that listens to the underlying reads it from its decision bar. A minute with no trade in it has no money, and the resolver refuses the entry by that name (decision 8).
- A bot that listens to another symbol opens a second bar stream on the underlying, as a bot that trades another stock does (#2912). Its bar prices nothing here; it is the money. An entry with no such bar for its minute is refused as it is for shares.
- An option close waits for no bar: it is priced from quotes.

**Quotes are asked for from the start of the run.** A window asked for at the decision has no quotes yet, so its search could never be finished.

- When the run starts it puts its plan on the process's watch list, under its own key, in the class of a running bot's window. Until a bar closes the window is centred on the underlying's live quote; from then on each closed bar of the underlying that had a trade moves it to that bar's close. The request goes when the run ends.
- **A bot whose window cannot fit is refused once, as its run starts.** The most lines its plan can need (decision 9) is compared with the Clerk's own budget of option lines. Over it, the run ends before it asks for a bar, under `OPTION_WINDOW_OVER_LINE_BUDGET`, and the bot's page shows that reason. The budget is zero until the Clerk is given one, so no option bot runs on a Clerk that was given none.
- **A bot on a Clerk with no option quotes of its own is refused the same way.** A Clerk that reads its market status from a shared source makes no IBKR pass, so no contract is ever quoted on it (decision 17). The run ends as it starts, under `OPTION_CLERK_HAS_NO_OPTION_QUOTES`, before it asks for a quote line, a bar or a search worker, in words that say this Clerk has no option quotes of its own. It is asked first, before the line budget. A process whose quote source is gone for another reason, as before any Clerk is up, refuses each entry instead, under `OPTION_ENTRY_NO_QUOTE_SOURCE`: that can pass.
- **An entry’s proof window is judged at acceptance.** A long entry wait does not refuse the whole run at startup. The Clerk has the entry’s actual cancel time, including time spent reading the contract listing or waiting for fresh account evidence, and refuses an individual entry under `OPTION_ENTRY_TAKEN_TOO_LATE` when it cannot leave seven seconds for proof (decision 19).

- **A fault while the window follows a bar never breaks the stream.** The run listens to the underlying's bars on the stream the strategy decides from. A fault in that listening is logged with the bot, the run and the bar, and the bar still reaches the strategy. The window stays where it was.

**An entry is resolved by the runner and judged by the Clerk.** After the runner's own screens (one intent, Stop, the closing bar, lateness, the bar of the minute), in order:

1. the Clerk's IBKR supervisor is read on the event loop: the feed's state, the underlying's chain, the underlying's quote;
2. the rate and the declared dividends are read off the loop (decision 18). Unknown dividends refuse a plan with a leg picked by delta, and no other plan;
3. the supervisor is read again and the contracts are searched for in the entry lane's worker process (decision 18);
4. a pick listed with a multiplier other than 100 is refused by name, before the Clerk is asked. The resolver picks standard contracts only, so this guards the path and is tested with an answer made by hand;
5. the picks' quotes are tested again on a third read (`recheck`, decision 8);
6. lateness is judged again;
7. the Clerk is handed the contracts, their listed multipliers, the recheck's quotes with their generation, the bot's two limits, its order terms, and the proof deadline of fifty seconds after the decision bar closed (decisions 15 and 19). It is handed no price. The Clerk judges the entry's exact cancel time at acceptance.

- **Each wait is given no longer than the decision has left.** A decision is on time for twenty seconds from its bar's close. A read of the rate and dividends that outlives that goes on and is kept for the next decision; a search that outlives it runs to its answer and the answer is dropped. A decision whose time ran out during the read is refused as late before any search begins.
- **A fault in the read of the rate and dividends is that entry's refusal.** Not "the dividends are unknown", which the read reports, but the read itself failing. It is one blocked decision under `OPTION_ENTRY_PRICING_INPUTS_NOT_READ`, logged with its stack, and the run goes on. An entry waits for its read (decision 18). It can still be told the lookup is running, when the lookup it joined was begun by a page, which does not wait: that is the same one refusal. A search that comes back as a caller's mistake still ends the run: that is a fault of the code.
- **The rate and the dividends are read again in the background at every closed bar,** so a decision finds them kept and does not spend its own twenty seconds on a vendor, or on the coordinator.
- **A refusal on the way is one decision.** Whatever stops an entry before its order is taken, by the runner or by the Clerk, the decision gets one `blocked` receipt with a reason code and a sentence, the staged decision is discarded, and nothing is kept for a later bar. A refusal at the send is the next bullet but one: that entry was accepted, and its receipt says so. The codes are the resolver's own (`OPTION_CONTRACT_…`), the search's own (`OPTION_SEARCH_…`), the Clerk's own, the existing `DECISION_LATE` and `PRICING_BAR_…`, and six of the runner's: `OPTION_ENTRY_NO_QUOTE_SOURCE`, `OPTION_ENTRY_LISTING_NOT_READ`, `OPTION_ENTRY_DIVIDENDS_UNKNOWN`, `OPTION_ENTRY_PRICING_INPUTS_LATE`, `OPTION_ENTRY_PRICING_INPUTS_NOT_READ`, `OPTION_ENTRY_SEARCH_LATE`. The Clerk owns `OPTION_ENTRY_TAKEN_TOO_LATE`.
- **The entry cutoff is asked twice, under one set of terms.** The runner's recheck refuses an entry past the cutoff under the bot's own terms, and the Clerk is never asked. The runner hands those terms over with the entry, and the Clerk asks again under them at its own clock, later (decision 16). Its refusal is the same one blocked decision, written by the Clerk, under the same code. As first built the Clerk asked under the starting terms, so a bot with other terms could be passed by one and refused by the other for the terms alone.
- **A refusal at the send is the Clerk's, and the runner takes it as it does for shares.** As the order is sent the Clerk asks the session and the cutoff again and tests the entry's quote again at that clock (decisions 15 and 16). An entry refused there was accepted and never sent: its receipt is the accepted one (`enter_intent`), not a `blocked` one, and the books record the refusal as `ENTER_SUBMISSION_REFUSED` with its reason. The runner discards the decision, the journal lists no purchase, and what was set aside is free again. The receipt does not say it was refused, so the runner says it once in its log: the decision, the word "rejected", and the code the books recorded of the order. It reads the entry's standing for that (`option_entry_of_decision`, a read that waits for nothing and records nothing).
- **The limits are the deployment's.** The runner reads the bot's two limits and its close terms in one place, from the four order terms sealed with the bot on its binding (decision 24). A bot with none sealed is refused as its run starts, by name: nothing is assumed in their place.
- **A machine whose clock is behind the feed's** can be handed a bar a moment before, by its own clock, that bar closed. The selection is then stamped at the bar's close, never before it; its quotes are judged a little older than they were.

**An exit closes what the Clerk says the bot holds.** In a Dry Run the runner asks the Clerk which contract the bot holds and hands over the newest quote the supervisor holds for it, whatever its age, when that quote is live data. The Clerk judges whether a decision could have used it (decision 16). Delayed or frozen data is not handed over. With nothing to hand over the EXIT is sent all the same.

- **A real account's exit reads no quote and hands none over.** A Paper account's close is a market order (decision 19), and no quote prices it. So the runner asks the supervisor nothing for it: a quote source that is gone, a quote that is old, and a read that fails each leave the close exactly as it was. As first built the Paper runner read the quote too, and a fault in that read ended the run before the sale was sent, with the contract still held. The runner asks the Clerk whether it fills its orders itself, the question it already asks before it waits for a bar to price a simulated sale of shares.

- **A quote with one side is handed over as it stands.** It is then the newest kept, and the newest quote alone prices a close (decision 16). With no bid on it the close is not priced: the owner is told the position was not closed, under `OPTION_EXIT_UNPRICED`, the bot goes on holding, and its EXIT is tried again on the next bar. A line shows one side after a reconnect or a data loss, until the other ticks.

**A Dry Run's contract reference is IBKR's listing.** The simulated broker has no contract reference to ask. The multiplier handed to the Clerk is the one IBKR lists the contract with, the resolver picks only standard contracts, and the entry's row in the bot's journal says where the listing came from. On a Paper account the runner hands over the same listed multiplier, and the Clerk asks Alpaca's own reference about the contract before it takes the entry (decisions 11 and 19).

**The Clerk keeps quotes for what its bots hold, runner or no runner.** In a Dry Run a contract a bot holds is the Clerk's demand, not the runner's.

- **On a real account the bot's own run asks for what it holds.** The keeping below follows a Dry Run's books only: it exists to state marks, and a real account's marks and its close are Alpaca's (a close is a market order, and the account's reading is the broker's own). So nothing on a Paper account prices from a held contract's quote. A Paper run still names, on its own watch request and beside its window, the contracts on its underlying that the account's books say must be quoted, as held lines (decision 9): one a bot holds, or one an order is working in. It is the keeping's own question of the books, one read whatever their length, asked through the Clerk (`option_contracts_for_run_quotes`). On a Dry Run's account the Clerk answers none: its own keeping asks for those. So both runners ask the one question, and neither is told which kind of account it is on. The run asks again as an entry is settled, when the contracts to name have changed, so a part fill is asked for from then on, and with each bar of the underlying that moves its window, which is when a contract that has been sold since stops being asked for. Both of a run's bar streams show the underlying's bars to the run (`_shown_to_the_option_run`), so a bot that listens to another symbol moves its window with the second stream's bars, in a Dry Run and on Paper alike. The window itself is asked for from the start of the run to its end, whether an entry rests or not. A contract past its last session is not named.
- **Known gap: a stopped Paper bot that still holds a contract has no quote line,** unless another option bot on the same underlying is running on that account. Its run's request went with the run. Nothing needs the quote today. A screen that shows a held contract's live quote, or a close priced from one, will: the keeping must then follow a real account's books for demand, without stating marks.

- Each Dry Run's Clerk is followed while it is composed. Its books are read for the contracts its bots hold: a position in a recorded contract, or an order working in one.
- Those contracts are asked for as held lines (decision 9), so a held contract keeps its line when its runner is gone, and when the bot's window has moved away from it.
- **A mark is the newest whole quote the contract's line holds, restated every fifteen seconds while the session is open.** It is kept when the feed is healthy and the quote is live data, of the feed's current generation, with both sides arrived, something offered and not crossed, and both sides received since today's session opened. Its age is not tested. A calm contract's sides tick only when they change. Its bid can stand for many minutes and is still what the contract could be sold for, so each hand-over says so again (decision 16).
- **The row keeps both sides' own receipt times.** Whatever prices a close from it knows the quote's true age. A close priced from a quote older than a decision could use says so, with the age: decision 16's natural price from an older quote.
- **What is never a mark:** a quote from before a reconnect or a data loss, since the generation moves and each side waits for a tick of its own (decision 9); delayed or frozen data; a quote with one side; a crossed one; one with a side last received before today's session. Outside the session nothing is restated. The last quote of a session is that session's mark, and no mark of the next.
- **A line that shows one side is put back as it stands, and is never a mark.** After a reconnect or a data loss one side of a line can tick before the other. That quote is the line's last word. The keeping writes a live quote of the current generation that fails as a mark only for its missing side into the account's file as of its own receipt, at once and again when what it shows changes. It never states it as the newest held, and the account's reading passes over a quote with one side: it is no mark, and it ends none (decision 16). One from an older generation is never written.
- **So a close is priced by one rule, runner or no runner.** The newest quote alone prices a close (decision 16). With no bid on it a sale is not priced: an owner's Stop or Flatten sells nothing and says the close is unpriced, exactly as a runner's EXIT that hands over the same line. With only a bid on it a sale is priced at that bid, on both roads, and says it used an older quote. As first built the keeping wrote only whole quotes, so with no runner alive a close after a data loss sold at the bid kept before the loss, a price the line no longer showed.
- **A quote a decision uses is still held to five seconds.** The entry's and the exit's quotes are tested by the resolver, the recheck and the Clerk as before. Only the mark is not.
- **This rule is the coordinator's choice, for the owner to confirm.** As first built, a quote was kept only when both sides had ticked within five seconds of one pass. A calm held contract then had no mark most of the time, and one with none in the last 80 seconds before the close cost the next day its starting equity and every entry of that day.
- A bid of zero is kept: it is what the contract could be sold for.
- **What an account held when its Clerk closed goes on being asked for and kept.** A Dry Run's Clerk is closed when its run ends. The books give their last word as it closes, and the process keeps the line and the file. So the close of what an ended run left sells at the newest quote kept; a contract nobody bid for at the Stop is sold by the next pass once it is bid again; and a session that ends with no runner alive has its mark at the close, which is the next day's starting equity.
- A contract past its last session is neither asked for nor kept.
- **Every mark after the entry's own is the keeping's.** The entry's quote is stated as the newest held as its order is sent, by the simulation that fills it, after the stricter five-second test. The runner hands over no quote between its two decisions, and the Clerk has no door to hand one through. The door first built for that judged nothing and had no caller left, and was deleted.

**The stock quotes never wait on the keeping.**

- The supervisor's pass begins a round of the keeping and goes on. It waits neither for the books to be read nor for a file to be written. The stock demand is read as it was before any of this.
- A round reads the books and writes each account's file off the event loop, and waits for each no longer than two seconds. One round runs at a time. Work a round went on without is left to finish, and no second one is begun beside it: a thread cannot be stopped, and a stuck lock would otherwise take one more every second.
- The books are read after a pass, so what they say is asked for from the next pass on, a second later.
- A fault in a round reaches no pass. It is logged once as it starts, with its stack when it has one, again every five minutes while it lasts, and once when it ends. That holds for a write that never returns, which is named with its account at each reminder, and for books whose answer cannot be taken up. One account's fault does not stop the accounts after it. A feed that is down is said once for the feed, not for each contract. A contract waiting for its first ticks of the day is said once and is no warning.
- A process begins no round while no Dry Run account is open and none that closed still holds a contract. A closed account that holds one does begin rounds: that is what keeps its mark.
- Nothing here has a timer of its own. The supervisor's pass drives it, and closing the supervisor ends the round under way. A Clerk that reads its market status from a shared source makes no pass: it begins no round, reads no books, asks for no line and keeps no mark, and nothing there raises.

**The journal.** A Dry Run's journal row for an option fill names the contract in `symbol`, as the Clerk's own fill rows do. It adds the underlying, the contract's multiplier as the books record it, the fill model and the age of the quote side used, and on an entry where the listing was read. Each new field is left out of a row that has none, so a stock bot's rows are what they were.

- **What an entry came to is said in six words** (`app/options/entry_came_to.py`, worked out in one place from the Clerk's standing of the entry, `bot_option_run.entry_came_to`). The runner logs the word for every option entry it settles, and for one the Clerk took and then refused, in a Dry Run and on Paper. The standing is all the runner is told, so a word covers every case its standing covers:
  - **filled**: every contract asked for filled;
  - **part filled then cancelled**: the order ended with some of it filled;
  - **cancelled unfilled**: the order reached Alpaca and ended with nothing filled. That is one cancelled at its deadline, by a Stop, by an exit or by a closing Clerk, one that expired, and one Alpaca rejected after it had taken it. The Clerk's order row keeps Alpaca's own last state for the order and so can tell these apart. The standing cannot, and neither can the runner;
  - **rejected**: the order never rested. The Clerk refused it as it was sent, or Alpaca refused it in its answer to the send, or it was proven never to have reached Alpaca;
  - **resting**: not proven when the runner stopped waiting, and the books show the order at Alpaca: working, or asked to be cancelled and not yet shown ended;
  - **unproven**: not proven, and nobody knows where the order is. Alpaca's last answer about it was lost, or Alpaca counts a fill the books do not hold. It is never called resting.
- **The journal spells no words of its own.** A row's word is one of those six, and never one of the two that mean nothing was bought.
- **The journal stays a record of fills.** An entry's rows say what the entry came to, in a field left out when absent. An entry that bought nothing is an ordinary ending: it has no row and raises nothing, and what it came to is in the log and in the Clerk's own record (`ENTRY_SETTLED`). As first built an accepted entry with no fill was raised as a broken simulation. What is still raised is a contradiction: the Clerk answers that contracts filled and has no fill of them, or that none did and has one.
- **A Paper account has no journal.** Its fills are the Clerk's books, and what its entry came to is the Clerk's record.

- **The runner asks the Clerk what the decision filled,** by the decision's own id. The answer is the fills of the order that decision made, an ENTER's opening order or an EXIT's closing order, on the contract and with the multiplier the books record, with the simulation's record of how it priced each. No order is taken out of a receipt by its place: an EXIT's receipt names the entry it closes before the order that closed it.
- An EXIT the Clerk accepted and holds for the session's open has made no closing order. It filled nothing yet and gets no row. Its fill, when it comes, is on the Clerk's books and not in the journal.

**A bot's money view.** The view of a running bot's budget prices no next entry for an option bot. Its next position costs what its contracts cost when they are picked, so the view says that, gives no figure, and says neither that the budget covers the next entry nor that it is short of one. A share of the underlying is never used as an estimate. A bot in shares reads as it did, and the served shape is unchanged.

- **It never says "Ready" about money it did not judge.** With something of its budget free the headline is "Its next entry is priced when its contracts are picked". As first fixed it read "Ready for its next entry", which a budget of one cent read too, before and after its entry was refused for cost.
- **With nothing of its budget free it says the next entry waits, and why.** No price is needed for that: every entry's commitment is above zero (decision 2). It is then not eligible to enter.
- **A budget too small for any contract still reads as priced when picked.** The view has no price to know that by. Each refused entry says the true figure in its receipt.

**The stock path is unchanged.** A bot in shares reaches none of this. A stock Dry Run run before and after the change wrote the same journal, receipts, transitions, orders, fills and hashes, byte for byte.

- **Known gaps:**
  - **After a restart, the first close of a stopped bot's contract is priced from a quote kept before the restart.** Nothing asks for the line while the account is closed, so no quote is kept until the account is opened again, and the close made as it opens runs first. The fill says it is an older quote and how old. A simulated close may use the newest quote kept; it never passes it off as a price of now.
  - **A stopped bot whose close failed is not tried again after a restart,** even once a bid is kept. Boot's restoration leaves the account open, and a Dry Run's Clerk makes no pass on a timer. The owner presses Flatten, or Start. The one close that is tried again is the scheduled close of decision 20: once it has come due and failed, the keeper takes it again while its session is open.
  - **A run refused as it starts is recorded as a crash,** with the refusal's own code and sentence. The outcome vocabulary has no kind for a refused start that carries a sentence. A refused warm-up of the feed is recorded the same way.
  - **A contract bought in the second its run ended is unasked for one pass.** The books are read a pass behind. The next pass asks for it.
  - **A contract still held on a Clerk that is switched to a shared status source gets no line and no mark there.** Nothing quotes it. Its close is priced from the newest quote kept before the switch, and says that quote's age.
- **Not done by this decision:**
  - the resolver's whole evidence (every candidate of every leg) is not written down with the entry. The Clerk keeps the contract, the multiplier and the quote, and the runner logs the pick. A pick cannot yet be reproduced from stored evidence;
  - the Clerk's own ENTER facts do not say where the multiplier came from;
  - a check, at the Clerk, of the terms a runner hands over against the ones sealed in the Clerk's own copy of the bot's configuration: the runner is the one reader of them (decision 24);
  - a quote line for a contract a stopped Paper bot still holds;
  - the run replay proof (`run_replay_proof.py`) settles a replayed decision from the live record's outcome alone. Nothing in the app calls it since its receipt was cut (#2755). Called on an option bot's run, it would replay an ENTER settled as discarded as entered;
  - the bot page's chart draws a fill at its price on the underlying's scale; an option fill's premium is off that scale;
  - a line with one side that never ticks today has no mark until it does, however calm the market;
  - the keeping's work off the loop runs on the Clerk's own pool of worker threads, the one its order and exit machines use. Stuck work takes one thread for the books and one for each account with a stuck write;
  - the runner keeps no room for the Clerk's test of the entry's quote at the send. A quote 4.9 seconds old at the recheck can be refused at the send, which fails closed.

### 22. The Clerk reads, books and holds on what ends an option position without an order of its own

Decision 14 says how an activity is read and which lots it takes. Nothing called it. This decision is the Clerk's side: when it reads, how it books, and what it holds a bot for.

**Reading**

- **One poll, two callers.** The deadline keeper's round starts it once its two steps have had their turn (decisions 19 and 20) and does not wait for it: the walk of the account's activities can take seconds, and neither a resting entry's cancel nor a scheduled close may wait behind it. The reconciliation pass runs it before its final look at the broker. The poll starts no pass and takes no account-wide gate. It reads Alpaca with no lock held; the Clerk's guarded ports refuse a broker call made under the intake lock.
- **A third caller, at the owner's word.** The owner's ending of an expired contract (below) makes the same poll, at once, whatever the cadence, and after any read already under way.
- **The option step can fail no one else.** A fault in it is logged with its stack. The keeper's round is not failed by it, and the pass goes on to its verdict, where a position the step did not book still shows as drift. A poll that raised is tried again a minute later. An error of the store itself is the pass's own to meet: the pass's option step raises it. The keeper's read is not one of its round's steps. It logs any failure of its own, the store's and a lost execution lease among them, and ends; the next round starts another.
- **When.** Every five minutes while nothing is wrong. Every minute while a read was not whole or a hold of this decision stands. At once after a restart, because the cadence is kept in memory. At once, and at most once a minute, when a pass's own snapshot shows a recorded contract's position differing from the books.
- **The window** starts on the date of the first fill in any contract a bot still holds, the date of the oldest row an open hold names, or the expiry date of a contract the owner ended at their word in the last seven days on a real account, whichever is earliest. Earlier than needed is safe: a row already booked is skipped by its id.
- **The walk is followed to its end,** up to fifty reads. A walk cut short, a walk longer than that, or a read that fails is not whole. Nothing is concluded from it and no hold is ended by it. What it did read is booked, and the bots that still hold an option after that are held until a whole read. The hold says the read failed and no more: the broker's own error is in the log, because its text changes from one failed read to the next and a hold whose words change is written again each time.
- **One read at a time.** A caller that finds a read under way neither waits behind it nor reads again after it. Only the owner's ending waits its turn.
- **A stock-only account is never read.** The worklist starts at `option_contracts`. On books that never recorded a contract that table is empty: no other row is read, Alpaca is not asked, and nothing is written. A stock scenario's books are byte for byte what they were.
- **A Live Shadow reads nothing from Alpaca here.** Its read port answers none, whole, as decision 14 left it.
- **A Dry Run's read reports none, too.** No broker reports a simulated account's option events. Its simulation ends its own positions in a step of its own (below), and the poll calls that step before it reads.

**Booking**

- **One batch function** (`book_option_events`). It runs inside the repository's write fence from its first read of the books to its last append, so no fill lands between a decision and its record.
- **An event is one transition for each bot that lost contracts:** `OPTION_POSITION_ENDED`, with the summary `OPTION_EXPIRED`, `OPTION_EXERCISED`, `OPTION_ASSIGNED` or `OPTION_SOLD_OUTSIDE`. Its fold writes everything in one transaction: the lot closes at a price of zero, and for an exercise or an assignment the shares arrive at the strike. One transition and never two, so the option cannot leave the books without the shares and cash it became.
- **A sale's record names the outside order it books,** and no other ending names one. The fold refuses a sale that names no order and an event that names one.
- **Two more endings are booked by the same transition and no broker reports either.** `OPTION_SETTLED_IN_CASH` is a Dry Run's simulation selling a contract at its exercise value (below). `OPTION_ENDED_BY_OWNER` is the owner's ending of a contract nothing will settle (below). Neither moves shares.
- **The rows are the ones every reader of money already reads.** A command, an effect, orders and fills. So FIFO, the budget, cash and the day's P&L count an event as they count any fill, and no table is added: the schema stays at version 23. The effect's kind is `RECONCILE`, which the schema had kept and nothing used, and it is written already ended, so no worklist of open work lists it.
- **The fold works the money out again.** It takes the shares and the cash from `expected_share_trade`, the canonical rule, and refuses a fact that states anything else. It refuses to close more than the bot holds on that side.
- **A price above zero reaches the books two ways only:** a sale by an order the Clerk did not place, and a simulation's settlement in cash. A simulated booking must carry the simulation's own record of the price it read. The fold works the exercise value out again from that record and the contract's own strike, and refuses a simulated booking with no record, or a price the record does not give. A booking no simulation made carries no such record.
- **Taken once, by its id.** The command's key is a hash of the broker and the activity's id, with the bot. The same id with other figures is a conflict, held by name; the first booking stands. A share trade's id is never a key in that space.
- **Two bots, and a stop between them.** Each bot's record carries the whole allocation. A run that finds one record finishes the bots it names and the books do not yet show, from the record. It never matches the event to lots a second time.
- **The lots offered to the rule** are every bot's own FIFO lots of the contract, each with the ledger sequence of the fill that opened it, and one lot for outside orders the books recorded in that contract. Those are the lots on the books. Inside one bot's books the lots close first in, first out, as every close does. Every one closes at zero, so among the lots a bot holds, which of them closed changes no total. After a write-off and a re-entry that once failed: the write-off closed no lot, so first in, first out closed the written-off lot, and the loss booked was that lot's cost and not the cost of the lot that ended (#2975). The canonical fold now carries each write-off as an event of its own and sets its lots apart, so a close takes the lot that ended and the write-off itself realises nothing. A set-apart lot stays an open lot for valuation -- the budget carries its premium and equity marks it, as this decision already records -- and the one fill that may close it is one whose credit a discharge withheld (#2854), the execution the discharge stood in for.
- **A position that left the books at the owner's word is set apart.** The owner wrote it off, which closed no lot, or ended it at their word after expiry, which closed the lot at nothing. The broker knows nothing of either, and a row that comes later still counts those contracts. So what the lots on the books cannot meet of a row is set against them, on the side the event ends. The row's count is then met and every lot still on the books is booked. Nothing is booked for the contracts that had left: an expiry moves nothing more for them, and an exercise or an assignment holds that bot by name, because its shares are real and on no bot's books. The booking's record says what the row counted of them, so the same contracts are never counted for a second row.
- **The lots on the books always come first.** A written-off lot may never have been at the broker. Booking the lots that are on the books is what leaves the books equal to the broker's position.
- **After a write-off the fills hold more than the position.** When the difference is exactly what was written off, the oldest lots are those contracts and the rest are the bot's own: a bot that wrote a position off and bought the contract again has the lot it holds now booked. A bot whose lots and position disagree in any other way is offered none, and is held by name when a row comes for its contract.
- **An event in a contract the books never recorded is nobody's.** No bot can hold such a contract: it is the owner's own trade in the same account. Nothing is booked, nobody is held, and it is logged. An exercise's shares then show as the pass's drift, as a hand trade's always have. An event in a contract a bot did trade and holds no more still holds the account, unless the owner's ending of that contract names the row (below).
- **When it happened, for FIFO and for the day's P&L:** one millisecond before the close of the last session on or before the row's date, and never before the newest lot it takes. An expiry falls inside its own session's P&L.
- **A share trade is evidence, never the source.** Each exercise or assignment is paired with a share trade of its date and underlying: first with one that meets the contract's terms exactly, then with what is left. The books' figures are the contract's terms either way. A row whose shares, cash and symbol are the contract's and whose printed price is not holds nobody: the difference is on the booking's record. Alpaca's own documented exercise prints such a price.
- **The shares can be sold by every existing reduction.** No ENTER bought them, and every reduction of a bot's symbol is keyed on an entry order in it. So the order the shares arrive on has the role `ENTRY`. It was never at the broker: an EXIT neither cancels it nor looks it up. The owner's Flatten, an ended run's close and the Clerk's own retry then sell those shares as they sell any others. The bot itself never trades them.
- **Fees.** The close of a lot at nothing is no trade: no fee is predicted for it and no fee row is shared onto it. That is an expiry, an exercise, an assignment and the owner's ending. The share trade is a fill in shares and is priced by the stock fee model, as an estimate until Alpaca posts the day's fees. Whether Alpaca charges anything for an exercise or an assignment is not known. A simulation's settlement in cash is a sale, and is charged by the option fee model as every simulated option sale is. Its fill is named apart from a close at nothing, and the fold holds each ending to its own name, so a sale cannot be filed where no fee is charged.

**Holds**

One family of reason codes. Each is raised for the bot it can be tied to and account-wide otherwise. Each blocks entries and admits reductions and the Flatten. One reduction waits whether or not a hold is yet raised: a bot's own order in a contract an unsettled outside order stands in (below).

| Code | Raised when |
|---|---|
| `OPTION_EVENTS_NOT_READ` | a read failed or was not whole |
| `OPTION_EVENT_UNREADABLE` | a row could not be read whole (a corporate action and a cash deliverable among them) |
| `OPTION_EVENT_NOT_EXECUTED` | a row is marked corrected or cancelled |
| `OPTION_EVENT_CONTRACT_NOT_HELD`, `_WRONG_SIDE`, `_HELD_ON_BOTH_SIDES`, `_MORE_CONTRACTS_THAN_HELD`, `_CASH_NOT_WHOLE_CENTS` | the rule refused, under the rule's own code |
| `OPTION_EVENT_ROW_CHANGED` | an id already booked now reads other figures |
| `OPTION_EVENT_SHARES_IN_ANOTHER_SYMBOL` | the contract's root is not the symbol the bot is registered under (a share-class ticker); nothing is booked |
| `OPTION_SHARE_TRADE_MISSING`, `_UNPAIRED`, `_DISAGREES` | the broker's own share trade is absent, belongs to no event read, or differs from the contract's terms in its shares, its cash or its symbol |
| `OPTION_EXPIRY_SIGNED_FOR_OTHER_SIDE` | an expiry closed the only side held against the sign of its row; it is booked, and the owner is told |
| `OPTION_EVENT_AFTER_A_LOT_CLOSED` | another bot closed a lot of the contract after the event's date and before its row was read |
| `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` | a contract past its last session is still on the books and the broker shows none of it or another net than the books', or a simulation could not settle it |
| `OPTION_SHARES_HELD` | the bot holds shares an event left; worked out from the books each round, so a stop between a booking and its holds loses nothing; it ends when the bot holds none |
| `OPTION_ORDER_NOT_OURS` | an order the Clerk did not place is in a contract the bot holds, and stands in its way |

- **A hold ends by proof or by the owner.** It stands while a whole read still has its cause and ends when a whole read no longer has it. Four end only another way: `OPTION_EVENTS_NOT_READ` at the next whole read, `OPTION_SHARES_HELD` when the bot holds no shares, `OPTION_ORDER_NOT_OURS` when the order has settled or its own review releases it, and `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` when the contract's end is booked or the owner ends it at their word. The other fourteen the owner ends by acknowledging them (below). No hold ends on a timer.
- **A hold does not block lane quiet.** The owner's write-off of a contract that still trades may proceed beside it.
- **No hold's words promise an end that cannot come.** `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` says what the owner can do, by the state its contract is in. On a Dry Run it says that no price will come by itself. On a real account there are three states, and one function writes the words of each and decides the press (below). A bought contract with no row in the way: the notice says no row came, or names the row that came and says it will not be booked, or that it is booked and counted fewer contracts than the books held; its next step says the contract can be ended at the owner's word once the next session has closed and the broker shows none of it. A bought contract with a row in the way that may be its end: the notice names the row with what is known of it and says the contract is not ended at the owner's word while that stands, and its next step names no ending. Of a row that changed under a booked id it says the broker's own record moved, and that no rule here can say which reading is the true one. A contract held sold: the notice and its next step say a sold contract is not ended at the owner's word. `OPTION_ORDER_NOT_OURS` has two next steps: while the order works, to wait for it or review it; for a fill that will not be booked, that nothing will book it, that marking the order reviewed does not release it, and that the contract can be ended at the owner's word once it has expired. A hold only an acknowledgement ends says the Clerk compares the positions once more itself. A hold of the account's own names no bot. Two cases are still wider than what is true, and are named under Known: a Dry Run's hold for a contract its simulation does not let go of, and a bot whose lots do not add up to its position.
- **A row read after its position left the books closes nothing twice.** An expiry's row moves nothing more: nothing is booked and nobody is held. An exercise's or an assignment's row moved shares and cash: nothing is booked for those contracts, and the bot they left is held by name under `OPTION_EVENT_CONTRACT_NOT_HELD`. A position that left on the other side than the event ends explains nothing.
- **The row is the truth, and the write-off is never offered for an expired contract.** A booked expiry closes the lot at zero, so the premium shows as the loss. A write-off realises nothing and leaves the lot open in the fills: the budget would carry the premium as a position for good. Where no row will come, the contract is ended at the owner's word.

**The owner's acknowledgement of a hold**

Fourteen holds name a row the Clerk could not book as it reads, or booked and noted something about. The row stays inside the read's window, so such a hold never ends by itself.

- **Two Clerk routes,** one for a notice of the account's own and one for a bot's, each with its fleet operation. No screen offers them yet.
- **It books nothing and moves no position.** So it must never hide that the books and the broker differ, and it is never a way round a booking.
- **Only a hold an acknowledgement ends.** An expired contract still on the books is refused: acknowledged, it would stay there with nothing ever raised for it again. Its exit is the owner's ending. The hold for an order the Clerk did not place is refused: that order's own review is its release. Each refusal says what does end the hold.
- **The request names the notice as the owner read it:** its id, when it was last written, and each thing it named. A notice written again since, or one that names other things, is refused, with what it names now.
- **A reconciliation pass is made for the request.** It reads and books the account's option events, as every pass does, and compares the account with the broker. The acknowledgement is given only when the pass read the broker, nothing was written to the books after the pass reached its verdict, and the broker's position, as the pass's plan compared it, equals the books' in every contract the hold names, in that contract's underlying, and in the symbol the hold's bot trades. A symbol that differs is named.
- **What that fence covers.** The newest record is read at the end of the verdict. The verdict's own booking of an outside order's fill is made after the plan compared the positions and before that read, so it is inside the fence: a sale booked there leaves the plan's comparison older than the books. The acknowledgement is then refused for positions that differ though they no longer do, and is given after the next pass. A record the pass writes after its verdict is outside the fence: while a bot's exit is being retried the pass writes one each time its decision changes, and every acknowledgement is refused as the books having moved until the retry has ended. Both err toward refusing, and nothing is acknowledged that should not be. Neither refusal can tell which case it is in, so each says what the owner does if it is that one: wait until the bot's retry has ended, or acknowledge again after the next pass.
- **Who acknowledges is the installation's operator,** never a field of the request.
- **A draining lane refuses it,** as it refuses the review of an outside order: neither stops a bot or reduces what it holds.
- **It covers each thing the hold named and what was wrong with it then.** The same row is not held for again. A new row is, and so is a new fault in the same row.

**A lot closed between an event's date and the day its row is read**

Paper publishes an activity the day after. In between, the bot whose lot the rule would take may close it. The Clerk books the event on the lots open when the row is read, so the books equal the broker's position. Which bot bore it cannot be known: the broker names no lot. The bot it was booked to is held under `OPTION_EVENT_AFTER_A_LOT_CLOSED`, naming the other. While the row is still unread the pass's account-wide drift hold stands as before, and that hold's own proof refuses a close of a contract the broker no longer holds.

**After a contract's expiry date**

A lot still open on a contract past its last session is set against the broker's position, once a whole read has booked what it could. Each holder is held under `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` when the broker shows none of the contract, or another net than the books'. That covers a row yet to come. It also covers a contract held bought by one bot and sold by another, which nets to nothing at the broker and may never get a row: nothing at the broker is no agreement while a lot is still open on the books. While the broker still shows what the books hold there is nothing to say yet. The bot that sold is told its side is not ended at the owner's word. The Clerk closes nothing by itself in either case. Such a holder also carries decision 20's missed-close notice, which the scheduled-close step raises whether or not the broker still shows the contract, and which ends on the round or pass after the position has left the books.

**The owner's ending of a contract nothing will settle**

Sometimes no settlement will ever come. A Dry Run that kept no price at the close cannot settle. A real account's broker may hold none of a contract and report nothing of how it ended. The position would then stay on the books for ever, and its bot with it.

- **The owner can end it at their word.** A recovery action, `end_expired_option`, carried by the routes that carry the Flatten and the write-off. The lot closes at a price of zero: what the contract cost is the realised loss, in History and in the budget. No fee, no shares, no order.
- **It is its own ending,** `ended_by_owner`, never "expired": nobody knows whether the contract ended in the money. The record says who said so, when, and why no settlement could be made.
- **It is booked through the same poll and the same batch function** as every other end of an option position. What the read and the simulation brought is booked first. So a settlement that has come takes the ending's place, and the owner is told that it did.
- **It is not the write-off.** A write-off leaves the lot open and realises nothing.
- **A draining lane still runs it.** It is among the panel's quiesce actions, as the write-off is. It sends no order and needs no active run, and for an expired contract it stands where the write-off stood. Without it a draining lane whose bot holds such a contract could never answer quiet. The acknowledgement of a hold stays refused there.
- **On a Dry Run it is offered** when the contract is past its last session and the bot's `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` hold is open and names it: the simulation's own answer was "no price". It asks for no stopped run and no failed close: a running bot whose feed was down that day has neither.
- **At the press a Dry Run's simulation is asked once more.** A price that has come since settles the contract by the simulation's rule. With still none, the simulation lets go of the contract first, in its own ledger, under the record a settlement would have used. So a price that turns up later settles nothing a second time, and a stop between the simulation's record and the Clerk's booking books the ending once.
- **On a real account it is offered** only when all of these hold: the contract is past its last session; the bot's `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` hold is open and names it; and the first session after the contract's last has closed, so the broker's record has had one full business day to come. One session is a named setting until the timing of Alpaca's row has been seen on an account.
- **At the press a real account is looked at once more.** One read of the option events, made now, must be whole and have no row in the way that may be the contract's end: an event for it, a row the Clerk cannot read that names it or its underlying, or a share trade in its underlying from its expiry date on. Two kinds of row do not stand in the way (below). A row that has come and can be booked is booked in the ending's place. When it takes only a part of the bot's position the owner is told it was a part and that the rest is still on the books; nothing is ended at a word in that same press. The broker's position must have been read and hold none of the contract.
- **A row the rule refused by name does not stand in the way.** The broker's row for the contract may have come and been refused by the canonical rule, under the rule's own name: for more contracts than are held, for the other side, for a contract held both ways. Nothing of it was booked and nothing will be while the books stand, so waiting for it settles nothing. Before this, such a contract had no way out: a row existed, so it was not ended; it had expired, so it was not written off; and the books differed from the broker, so its hold was not acknowledged. Now the ending goes ahead when every row that may be the contract's end is one the batch function refused under one of those three names in this same read, with the share trade paired with it. Each of the three says the books hold lots of the contract and the row cannot be matched to them, by its count or its side. The row is judged at the press, never remembered from an earlier read. Every other condition stands as it was. Any other row still stands in the way: one it refused under another name (it was offered no lot, or the cash is not whole cents) and one it never judged (unreadable, not reported as executed, waiting for an outside order's fill, held for under a name that is not the rule's).
- **Nor does a row that is booked whole.** The row came and is booked, it reads as it was booked, and every bot its record names has its record. It counted fewer contracts than the books held: two were held and the expiry was for one; the row took another bot's lot; it ended an earlier lot of a contract the bot then bought again; one of two was exercised on the last day. What such a row left on the books it will never take, and before this that contract had no way out either. Whether a row is booked whole is decided where the row is set against its record, in the batch function's booking, and nowhere else. Its share trade is set aside with it when the two agree or differ in the printed price alone. A share trade that states other shares may be a second exercise, and stands in the way.
- **A row that changed under a booked id keeps no exit.** The broker's own record moved after it was booked, and no rule here can say which reading is the true one. The booking stands as it was made, the row stands in the ending's way, and the notice says exactly that and names no ending. So does a row that a bot its record names could not be booked for.
- **An order the Clerk did not place that sold the contract, and that the books could not book, does not stand in the way.** It filled more contracts than the bots hold, or states no price, and nothing will ever book it. Before expiry that state has no way out; afterwards the ending is its one exit, so it still goes ahead. The amount is unchanged: what the contract cost is booked as the loss in full. The record names the order, what it filled and the price it states, and says that what it sold for is in the account's cash and on no bot's books.
- **The page offers the ending only where the press can succeed.** The function that writes the notice's words is the one that decides the press. While a row stands in the way it names the contract a second time, under a reference of its own, and the recovery policy reads that and does not offer the ending. A row in the way of the read that wrote the notice is in the way of the read a press makes, so the press could only be refused. What may differ at the press keeps the offer: a record still to come, and a broker that still shows some of the contract.
- **The record names each such row:** its reference, the name it was refused by, and the rule's own words, with its share trade's reference when one was read. Why no settlement could be made then says the broker's record could not be booked, and so do the served reason and sentence. A row that is booked whole is named under a key of its own, with what it counted and for whom, and its share trade's reference when that was set aside with it: the record then says a row came, was booked and counted fewer contracts than the books held. An outside order that could not be booked is named under a third key. The record never says that no record came when one did. A record with none of the three is what it always was.
- **Only a bought contract is ended this way.** A sold one that was assigned moved shares, and no word undoes that.
- **A row that comes afterwards is still read.** For seven days after an owner's ending on a real account the events are read, back to that contract's expiry date, though no bot holds an option. An expiry's row books nothing: the lot is closed. An exercise's or an assignment's row books nothing and holds that bot by name: the shares are real and on no bot's books.
- **A row the ending's record names is no late row.** It is read again on every read of those days and is judged again by the rule each time. It books nothing for the ended lot. While another bot still holds the contract that bot is held for it by name, as before. Once no bot holds the contract it is held for by nobody: the owner ended the contract knowing of that row, and an account-wide hold would say otherwise. Shares such a row moved are on no bot's books and show as the pass's drift. When the row was an expiry of a contract held both ways, it is matched to the side still on the books once the bought side is ended, by the rule for a contract held one way.

**A sale Alpaca makes itself**

It arrives as an order the Clerk did not place. Before, any such order put the whole account on a hold that refuses every reduction of every bot.

- **An outside order in a recorded contract that a bot holds, on the side that reduces it, is held against that bot** (`OPTION_ORDER_NOT_OURS`) and is left off the account-wide hold. No other bot is held, and the bot's reductions in every other symbol go on.
- **While the order is unsettled, no order of the bot's own to reduce that contract is sent.** Unsettled means still working, or filled and not yet booked. A second order beside it would sell contracts the outside order may sell, or has sold. Every reduction asks: a deciding program's EXIT, the Flatten, an ended run's close, the Clerk's retry and its scheduled close all create their order through the one admission of a reduction (`uncertainty.decide_capability`), which is refused under `OPTION_ORDER_NOT_OURS`. An EXIT already accepted is kept and driven on when the order has settled.
- **The fact asked is the books' own record of the order** (`option_event_books.outside_order_in_the_way`), written by the transition that observed it. No hold has to be raised first, so nothing is sent in the time between Alpaca's stream showing the order and the next pass or poll.
- **The owner's review releases it only while it has filled nothing.** An outside order the owner has marked reviewed, by the route that always reviewed outside orders, then stands in no order's way and is held for no more. The owner looked at an order that was working; what it fills afterwards they have not seen. So a fill not yet booked stands in the way again, and a fill that cannot be booked is held for by name, whatever was reviewed. The bot's hold itself is never acknowledged away: the order's review is the one release.
- **Once the order has ended with a fill, the fill is booked against the oldest lots,** as `OPTION_SOLD_OUTSIDE`, at the broker's average price, once, by the order's id.
- **A booked sale is counted once.** From the booking on, the order is left out of every read of outside orders that counts money or holds the account, as a manual chain's member is, and its executions in the account's activities are the booking's own. Two reads that count no money read the table of observed orders itself: History's list, which still shows the order as an outside order with its review state, beside the bot's own `sold_outside` ending, and the fee view's labels. So the fees price the one execution once, on the bot and by the option fee model, the sales the cash has not seen count it once, and no outside holding is named for it. Until it is booked a filled outside order is counted outside, as any is.
- **It is not booked** while another outside order may hold the same contract, when it filled more than the bots hold, or when it states no fill or no price. The hold then says which, and for a fill that will not be booked its next step says so (above, under Holds). An event in that contract is not booked either while an outside order in it does not state what it filled, and that is logged.
- **The Clerk's own retry of a close waits beside it.** The retry of a stuck exit is refused as a wait, never as a failure, so an order that works all day does not have the bot told as stuck.
- Nothing on such an order says whether Alpaca placed it or the owner did by hand. Both are recorded the same way.
- An outside order in a contract no bot holds, or on the side that adds to a position, is handled as it always was.

**A Dry Run's expired contract**

What follows is the simulation's rule. None of it is a broker's truth: no broker ends a Dry Run's positions, and a real account would have ended otherwise.

- **The simulation settles its own position, in one step of its own.** The poll calls `settle_expired_options` on a simulated account's trade port. The step writes the simulation's order ledger and hands back each settlement with the simulation's record of it, each contract it could not settle with why, and each it is still waiting for. The simulation's read of option events settles nothing and reports none.
- **It settles in cash, never into shares.** Alpaca says it sells an expiring position itself, within the hour before expiry, when the account cannot pay to exercise it. The simulation books that sale. Exercised, one call struck at 585 would buy $58,500 of shares on a $1,000 budget, and a put would leave the bot short.
- **The price is the contract's exercise value** at the close of its last session: what the underlying's price leaves over the strike for a call, or under it for a put. It is rounded down to the cent, against the bot. A value that rounds down to nothing is an expiry at nothing, so there is no jump between the two outcomes. The cash is worked out from the rounded figure, and both figures are on the record. A real account would have got the market's bid, which is not this figure.
- **The underlying's price is the close of one bar the simulation kept.** A bar that ends exactly at the close of the contract's last session settles at once. Otherwise the simulation waits two minutes after the close, for that bar to arrive, and then takes the newest bar of that session, if it ended no more than fifteen minutes before the close. An older bar is no price. Neither is a bar of another session, a bar the simulation made itself as the evidence of a fill, or an option quote. It is not an official closing price.
- **A sale is charged by the option fee model.** An expiry at nothing is no trade and has no fee.
- **It is booked one millisecond before the close of the contract's last session,** by the same batch function, as `settled_in_cash` or `expired`. It travels as an expiry, so the one rule that chooses lots chooses these and has no fourth kind.
- **The books' record says it is a simulation's rule,** and the rule in one sentence. Then the underlying's price, the strike, the exercise value as worked out and as booked, the contracts, the multiplier, the cash in whole cents, the bar, who delivered it, when it ended, the session's close, how long before the close the bar ended, when it was settled, and the fee the sale alone is charged.
- **With no price it settles nothing.** The contract stays held and the hold says why. No price comes by itself, so the owner can end the contract at their word (above).
- **A bot its settlement leaves holding nothing is cleared of its exit's notices where the settlement is booked.** A scheduled close of a contract with nothing bid fails and raises `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (decision 20). On a real account the pass ends that notice on its next clean look at the broker. A Dry Run has no pass on a timer, and its simulation is its own broker: it let go of the contract in the step whose record is booked. So the booking of a settlement, or of an owner's ending, ends the bot's `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` and `EXIT_STUCK` once it is flat in every symbol, as the owner's write-off ends its own. The missed-close notice ends on the keeper's next round. The running bot then carries no notice of a position and takes its next entry.
- A Dry Run never holds shares from an option.

**What the screens are handed**

History's run and Activity's row each carry how an option position ended. Both keys are left off the wire when absent.

- **A closed set:** `expired`, `exercised`, `assigned`, `sold_outside`, `settled_in_cash` (a simulation's rule only) and `ended_by_owner`.
- **Every figure is a field.** The contract as an object, for the one shared renderer. The date the position ended, as its own date-anchored field: 00:00 New York time on it, to be shown in the date-only New York mode, and never the day the Clerk booked it. The shares, their symbol, their price and their cash. The price a sale or a cash settlement closed at, and what it came to. Prices are exact decimals and money is whole cents, each with its dollars written out.
- **A simulation's settlement says so,** in a flag, in the price it read and how long before the close that price is from, and in the sentence. An owner's ending carries who said so and why nothing settled it. Its sentence says so when the broker's record of the contract's end could not be booked, when it was booked and counted fewer contracts than the books held, and when an order the Clerk did not place in the contract was not booked.
- **One sentence, written by the server.** It names no contract code.
- **History's counts of orders leave an ending's bookings out.** Nobody sent them. Their fills are transactions.

**Known, and not closed here**

- A bot left short the stock on a real account, by an exercised put today and by an assigned call once sold legs are taken, makes the long-only budget unavailable for the whole account until the short is covered. That is the budget's existing rule for any short lot, and it is not changed here: no other bot of the account takes an entry meanwhile. The refusal's words are true of it: they name the bot, the event and the shares, and say to stop the bot and then Flatten it. A Dry Run is never left short: it exercises nothing.
- For the one cash reading after an exercise is booked the account has no budget: the shares were never ordered, so nothing was set aside for them. The words say it passes at the next reading and that nothing needs doing.
- Two bots holding opposite sides of one contract past its expiry are held by name. The owner's ending ends the bought side only. The sold side has no way out yet.
- A sold contract, or a part of one, that a Dry Run holds past expiry is settled in no way and is not ended at the owner's word. Its hold's next step would still name the ending, and the page would offer it and refuse. No bot can hold one today.
- An expired contract with a row in the way still has no way out: a row the Clerk cannot read, one not reported as executed, an exercise whose shares are in another symbol or whose cash is not whole cents, an event waiting for an outside order to state its fill, a row that a bot its record names could not be booked for, and a share trade in the underlying that no booked event of that contract explains (one that states other shares than its event, one paired with another contract's event on the same underlying, one paired with none). The ending is not offered, the notice names the row and no ending, the write-off is not offered, and the books differ from the broker.
- A row that changed under a booked id keeps no exit, and that is decided, not left over: no rule here can say which of the broker's two readings is true. An issue is to be filed for it.
- A bot whose lots do not add up to its position, and to no write-off, is offered the ending and refused at the press (`OPTION_OWNER_ENDING_LOTS_DISAGREE`). No road to that state is known.
- An outside sale for more contracts than the bots hold, or with no price stated, is never booked, in whole or in part. Five things follow. The whole account is held for drift, not the bot alone: no bot of it takes an entry, and the bot's own sale of that contract is refused. There is no way out before the contract has expired and the session after its last has closed: the order's review releases nothing once it has filled, and neither the Flatten nor the write-off is offered. The owner's ending then books the whole of what the contract cost as the loss. What the sale brought is in the account's cash and on no bot's books. And the order stays an outside order for good: the money reads go on naming its contracts as sold short by outside orders, and its fill is priced by the stock fee model.
- A sale filled in several executions is booked at the order's stated average, a float. Three contracts sold at 0.40, 0.41 and 0.43 are booked at 0.41333333333333333 a share and realise -458.00000000000000100 where -458.00 is true. One execution is exact.
- A stop between a booking and what the same run writes after it loses what was to follow. An expiry's note (`OPTION_EXPIRY_SIGNED_FOR_OTHER_SIDE`, `OPTION_EVENT_AFTER_A_LOT_CLOSED`) is then never held for: the bot is flat and nothing is read again. A Dry Run's bot is then left with its exit's notices until a pass is made. Shares an event left are held for from the books each round and are not lost.
- After a write-off of an option contract that still trades, with nothing else held, the account's option events are no longer read. A row that then comes for it, an exercise's among them, is not seen; its shares show as the pass's drift. After an owner's ending the read goes on for seven days.
- The pass still holds the whole account for drift until Alpaca's row is published. `OPTION_EXPIRED_NOT_SETTLED` stands beside that hold, not in its place.
- What a row counted of a position that had left the books is known from the bookings made with it. A row that counted only such contracts books nothing, so it is known only while it is still read.
- An outside holding of a contract is known only from the outside orders the books recorded. One opened before custody began is not known.
- An outside order whose last state the Clerk never reads stays unsettled, and the bot's own order in that contract waits until the owner reviews the order. The pass looks an order up by its client order id once it has left the open orders; an order with none is never looked up.
- An order of the bot's own already working when an outside order appears is not cancelled. Both are then at the broker, and if both fill the broker is short a contract no booking explains.
- Option corporate actions and cash deliverables are listed as unreadable and held for. They are not read.

**Nothing here has been seen on an account.** How Alpaca's own sale near expiry appears, when Paper and Live publish these activities, what links a share trade to its event, and whether real rows carry the fields the documents show are all still to be seen.

### 23. What a day takes from a structure is each leg's own decay, summed, or nothing

The preview shows a structure's daily time decay beside what it leans. One contract's decay is decision 6's: the model's price 24 hours from now less its price now, with the stock's price, the volatility and the rate held.

- **The sum.** Each leg's decay per share, signed by its side, times its ratio, the contract multiplier and the structures asked. A sold leg gains what its contract loses. Below zero the position loses that much in 24 hours.
- **Each leg is priced as it was picked.** At the volatility solved from its own mid quote, against the underlying's mid, with the rate, the dividends and the tree that solve was given, at the moment the picks were made. Nothing about a leg is priced a second way.
- **No part-sum.** A leg with no figure leaves the structure with none, and the screen says "not estimated" with the reason. There are three: the pricing inputs are not known (dividends that could not be looked up, as for the lean); no volatility explains the leg's quote; the model has no price for the leg a day from now. The last is usual on a contract's last full day, where the tree no longer resolves a strike far from the price. A refusal is never a zero.
- **It is never held at zero.** A put deep in the money ahead of an ex-date gains with a day, by the interest on its strike. A structure can gain too, and is shown gaining.
- **Per 24 hours, by the clock.** Not per session: three such days pass over a weekend, and the figure is one of them. The words beside it say both.
- **A dividend going ex inside the day** is taken off the stock's price a day later, in full (decision 6). What that does to a call depends on its strike. A call deep in the money falls by about the dividend. A call at or out of the money hardly moves, because its price now already has the dividend taken off. Measured at a price of 585.11, 3.2 days from expiry, a volatility of 0.20 and $1.75 going ex inside the day: the decay moved by -1.60 a share at a strike of 540, by -0.74 at 575, and by two cents or less from 580 up. For a sold call that fall, of up to the dividend, is counted as a gain. It is a gain the seller may not get: a call in the money may be exercised just before the ex-date, and the sold leg is then assigned.
- **The warning is for a sold call in the money, and only that.** A sold call whose strike is below the underlying's price, with cash going ex inside the day, is named in a warning beside the figure. The warning says the figure counts that call's fall, by up to the dividend, as a gain, and that early assignment would take the gain away. A sold call at or out of the money is not named: most sold calls in these structures are, the model counts no such fall for them, and a warning there would call a real gain into doubt. The figure is the same either way.
- **A leg's own decay is not served.** It is its contract's figure, a share, and is not signed by the leg's side: beside a sold leg it reads as a loss where the position gains. Neither the PRD nor the screens ask for it. Only the structure's decay is served, in cents and in dollars.
- **Near an ex-date the figure is rough, and says so.** The tree is coarsest when the later price sits a day or so before an ex-date. At the default 401 steps the measured gap from the second engine was up to a sixth of the decay, for a call in the money two days ahead of a $3.00 dividend. When the underlying goes ex within three days the preview says the figure is a rough estimate.
- **It is priced where a search is, never on the event loop.** Two trees a leg. It is worked in the search's own pass, in the worker process (decision 18), and comes back beside the resolution. Measured on one machine at 401 steps: about 3.3 ms a leg, 13 ms for four legs, beside a search of a second or more. It is asked for only with a model that is known.
- **A model's figure, shown to the cent.** Nothing is judged by it and nothing is set aside for it.
- **Not yet:** an entry records no decay (the PRD asks for it on every entry: the entry lane can be asked for it the same way); the bot page shows none.

### 24. Deploy seals an option bot's plan and its four order terms, and opens a Dry Run and Paper

Decision 22 is other work's, and is written where that work lands.

Deploy takes an option structure as what a bot trades. What it writes is fixed for the bot's life, and what it refuses it refuses by name before the bot has one.

**One choice, and a bot in shares is unchanged**

- **A Deploy says what its bot trades once.** One field, `trades`, is one of three: the shares of the symbol it listens to, another stock or ETF, or an option structure. The field a different-stock Deploy has always used, `traded_symbol`, is still taken, and the two together are refused. Once read, a request has one spelling: only an option choice stays in `trades`.
- **So a bot in shares is the Deploy it always was.** Its request dumps to the same bytes, and its fingerprint, its review token, its binding, its configuration hash, its instance record, its seal and its money review are what they were. Each new field is left out of every dump when it is absent.
- **An option choice is the structure as the builder holds it:** the underlying, the name it was started from, its legs exactly as the preview takes them, and four order terms. Its size is the request's own sizing, in whole structures. Its plan is the one the preview builds from the same legs.
- **Every bot still carries exit terms.** The Clerk asks every registration for sealed ones. Nothing of an option bot reads them: its exits are market orders in regular hours.

**The four terms are sealed**

- **The widest gap a leg, the entry wait, the whole-structure cost limit and the expiry-day close** are said on the request, each a number inside the range decision 17's catalogue offers. None is filled in for one left out. The two times are whole numbers, strictly: a boolean is not a term.
- **They ride beside the plan** on the start request, the binding, the instance record and the seal, under the configuration hash and the seal's hash. The review token binds them too: a review of one set of terms is no review of another.
- **The runner reads them from the binding, in one place,** and hands them to the Clerk with every entry (decision 21). It assumes none: a bot sealed with no terms is refused as its run starts. A bot in shares may carry none.
- **The Clerk does not check them against its own copy** of the bot's configuration. The runner is the one reader.
- **The seal's terms are held to the binding's.** The seal's check table has a row for them, as it has for every field a seal carries: a seal made under other terms than the bot is bound to does not prove its build.

**What stops a Deploy**

Each is found by the authority that already holds the rule, and said in a sentence that names a leg by its place.

- **A structure no plan can hold:** the preview's own plan checks, in the preview's own words. They are one function that reads no quote, and the preview and Deploy both ask it. Deploy never reads an answer about quotes as an answer about the plan.
- **A structure the Clerk does not take yet:** the Clerk's own rule, one bought call or put (decision 15). The builder and the preview still show every structure.
- **The account's mode.** A Dry Run and a Paper account are open. A Live Shadow is refused: it is not built. Live is below. At the click this is asked before the owner's acceptance is: nobody writes an acceptance for a bot that cannot exist.
- **This Clerk cannot quote it:** it reads its market status from a shared source, or it counts too few quote lines free. The count is what this plan's window can need and what the option bots already running here can need, against the Clerk's own budget. That is stricter than the runner's own start refusal, which holds one window to the whole budget. It is this system's own estimate and cannot see another IBKR session, and the sentence says so.
- **On a real account, what Alpaca says:** the account's option level does not cover the structure (2 to buy an option, 3 for several legs; a level Alpaca did not report is not known, and not known is never approval); its option buying power is short of one entry, with both figures (not reported, that is a note and stops nothing); or Alpaca does not list the contract the preview picked. A read that failed stops the Deploy and says so. A Dry Run asks nothing of a broker.
- **What the preview finds right now:** picks that are not the structure built, or money that cannot be set aside. A leg quoted too wide, or a cost over its limit, stops nothing: the bot picks again at every entry, and the review says an entry would be skipped right now.
- **No price at the click.** An option bot is deployed only while one entry of its structure can be priced. A click whose preview gives no price is refused by name, in the price's own sentence: the rules come to no contract, or the quotes have not arrived after the click's wait. It is refused with a budget on the request or without one, on a Dry Run and on a real account. So every click that gets further was held to Alpaca's listing, to option buying power and to a red verdict.
- **Extended hours.** Every Deploy is regular hours, so this cannot be chosen today. The rule is held where a start takes its hours: an option bot is never started for extended hours.

A Deploy is refused for the first of these, and asks nobody further. The money review lists every one it finds, as rows for the page's "Can't deploy yet" list: it goes on to read the preview and to ask Alpaca after a structure, a mode or a quote-line blocker. Two things bound that list. A review of an account whose mode is not open asks Alpaca nothing. And what needs a price or a pick (buying power, the listing, a red verdict) is found only once the preview has resolved.

**The review says nothing of quotes it did not read.** When no plan can hold the structure, or this Clerk has no option quotes of its own, the preview is not asked. The review then says so (`preview_state: not_asked`) and carries no sentence and no reason code of the preview's.

**The money**

- **One entry is priced by the preview.** An option contract has no top of book to price a position from. The review asks the preview for the structure, on a hold of quote lines of its own, and reads the entry's own figures from its answer (decision 17). It works out one figure of its own: what the budget has free after one entry.
- **The rows are the five quantities by their names:** what is set aside, and the premium, Alpaca's requirement and the fee estimate that add up to it; the loss at expiry if held, an estimate, or "not estimated"; and what the budget has free after one entry. Each is whole cents with dollars written out beside.
- **The budget must cover one entry as priced now.** The Clerk's registration asks an option bot's budget for no minimum, because it cannot price a contract (decision 15). So Deploy asks, as it does of a bot in shares.
- **With no price there is no review to consent to, and no Deploy.** While the preview is only still arriving the review waits; otherwise it says why. At the click Deploy waits a few seconds for a preview that is still arriving, and then refuses by name (above). The preview wait is a deadline on a clock, counted from the click's first ask, so the time each preview ask takes is inside it. Subsequent broker readiness reads have their own bounds; together the click can take about fourteen seconds. The read made before the click waits for nothing and is not refused for a price that has not arrived: the page asks it again.

**The acknowledgement**

- **An option bot always trades something else,** options on the symbol it listens to included. The one rule (#2912) is asked of it without its plan: a Paper, Live Shadow or Live bot needs the owner's written acceptance, and a Dry Run is warned and asked for nothing.
- **The warning is its own:** options have no backtest here, and they lose value with time and move with volatility.

**Live is built and switched off**

- **One setting, `ALPACA_LIVE_OPTION_BOTS_ENABLED`, off unless a deployment turns it on.** No option order from this system has been checked against Alpaca, Paper included.
- **Three fences read it, through one accessor.** Deploy refuses a Live option bot by name and says what to do: check the first Paper option orders, then turn the setting on. The account Clerk refuses the plan at registration and refuses an ENTER. The Alpaca port refuses to send an option order with a leg that opens on a live binding.
- **A fence against opening never stops a close.** A Live account can hold a contract bought while the setting was on, and a restart that loses the variable is an ordinary accident. Off, every order that only closes still goes out: the bot's own EXIT, the Clerk's scheduled close (decision 20), and Flatten. Stop does not itself schedule a close. After a restart with this setting off, strategy monitoring cannot resume for the held option bot; the Clerk's scheduled close and the owner's Flatten remain available. The Clerk tells a close by the effect's purpose: an EXIT is built from the position its books hold and can open nothing. The port tells it by the order's own legs: every leg carries the closing intent it is sent to Alpaca with, and one leg that opens makes the order an opening one. Neither reads a flag a caller sets beside the order. Off, a bot that holds a contract cannot be started again, so its contract is closed by the Clerk on its day or by the owner.

- **A Live Shadow takes one bought option whatever the setting says** (decision 25). It rehearses real-account admission and fills only its own book, never an order at Alpaca.

**What the screens are told**

- **A bot's row names its structure** for a list ("Bull call spread") and for a sentence ("a bull call spread on"). A stock's row is written as before.
- **The review carries** Confirm's Trades and Orders lines, what the two sizing choices mean in structures, the sentence under the bot's end when a sold leg makes the Clerk schedule a close, the contracts picked, the rows, the blockers and the notes.
- **Notes for a real account,** each said once: this system's option orders have not been checked against Alpaca; option fees are this system's estimate until Alpaca posts them; and if Alpaca's trade stream does not carry option fills, the account stops taking entries at its first one. The first is about what has been checked, not about what has been sent, so it stays true after the first Paper order and goes when the check is done.
- **The Deploy answer carries the plan the start was handed,** never shares of the contracts' underlying.
- **Deploy again asks the plan whether a bot trades options,** not whether terms are sealed with it. It answers an option bot's structure and its four terms, and names no stock to trade. An option bot bound before Deploy sealed terms has none: it is refused by name, and no term is filled in for it.
- **The entry wait's reason is what the Clerk can prove.** A test holds the longest wait offered and the sentence an owner reads beside it to the Clerk's acceptance guard.

**Not yet**

- Several legs and sold legs through the Clerk, so every such structure is refused at Deploy.
- A list of the other bots on the same underlying that names option bots as such: the one-line note about the same symbol is the existing one.

### 26. An entry is set beside the broker's option buying power, and only an unexplained shortfall holds a bot

Decisions 24 and 25 are other work's, and are written where that work lands.

Alpaca says nothing, before an order or after it, about what that order cost. The account does state an option buying power (decision 12). So around an option entry on a real account the Clerk reads that figure twice and sets its fall beside what the bot set aside. An account-wide figure is not what one trade cost, so most of this decision is about when the two may be compared at all.

- **What is compared.** Whole cents, exact, with no tolerance. The fall is the first reading less the second. The cost of what filled is Alpaca's requirement for the units filled plus the premium paid for them, read from the executions on the books. What was set aside for what filled is the entry's commitment (decision 2): all of it when every contract filled, and for a part fill its share by contracts, rounded up to the cent. A part fill whose rest was cancelled is judged against the whole entry commitment: a fall above that is under-reserved; a smaller fall is unknown because release of the unfilled part is unverified.
- **Four results for a whole fill, asked in this order.** *Consistent*: the fall is the cost of what filled. *Under-reserved and unexplained*: the fall is more than was set aside for what filled. *Over-reserved*: any other fall. Alpaca took something other than the cost and no more than was set aside; it is recorded and harmless. *Unknown*: the pair was not judged, always with one reason.
- **The fee provision is not a difference.** One call bought at its $1.94 limit sets aside $194.00 of premium and six cents of fees. The comparison assumes the broker excludes the fee at the fill, so a clean fall is $194.00, and that is consistent. This behavior has not been observed on an account and remains on the live-data checklist. The provision is the room between "consistent" and "under-reserved": a fall has to pass the whole set-aside before it holds anything.
- **No tolerance, because every figure is whole cents.** A small difference inside the set-aside is over-reserved, which holds nothing, so a tolerance would only hide it. A reading with a fraction of a cent, or fills that are an average and do not come to whole cents, is unknown under its own reason. It is never rounded into agreement.
- **The first reading is made beside the read of the listing.** Decision 19 already asks Alpaca's contract reference before an entry is judged, outside the intake lock. The reading of the account is begun first and the listing is read while it runs, so it adds no step. It is the account's option fields and the day's cash transfers, with the Clerk's clock as the read began and as it ended. When the listing answers before both of its reads have finished, they go on while the entry goes on to its send. At the send the reading is used if both reads finished no later than the order was handed to the port; otherwise they are cancelled and the comparison records that the first reading was not ready. The entry never waits for it. (Changed 2026-10-13, #3136: the reads were cancelled at the listing's answer. The first Paper run found that cost every entry its reading, four of four, the listing answering in about 70 ms to the two reads' 65 to 150. Changed again 2026-10-14, #3136: the SDK answers from worker threads and the loop notes an answer only on a turn, so reads that had finished still read as ending after the send when the entry's own work made no turn (540 ms on Paper). The entry now gives the loop up to six turns, stopping as soon as both reads are noted and never waiting on one that has not finished, just before it hands the order over.) Its own 1,500 ms bound is inside the listing's 3,000 ms bound; the Clerk judges the proof window after both reads. It is made only for an option ENTER on a real account, and only when the listing is asked.
- **A reading that fails does not stop an entry.** A read that raises, is slow, or states no figure is kept as that fact, and the entry goes on. The result is unknown with the reason.
- **Fresh enough** is 45 seconds: the first reading must have ended no more than that before the order was handed to the port. It is the account reading's own staleness.
- **The second reading is the Clerk's own round's, never the runner's wait.** A round runs after each pass of the periodic sweep. Once the books prove what the entry came to (decision 19's four proven standings) the round reads the account again. It judges only after a later pass found the account clean. An unclean pass gives an unknown result and cannot attribute an account discrepancy to this entry. A clean pass proves only what that pass observed, not that an outside order could not have opened and closed between observations. With no later pass in 60 seconds the pair is unknown.
- **A pair is judged only when nothing else can explain a difference.** Each of these is a reason of its own: a reading failed or stated no figure; the first was stale; a figure was not whole cents; a cash transfer reached the account between the readings, or whether one did could not be read; another order of the account was working, or was recorded about after the first reading began; no pass checked the account after the second reading, or that pass was not clean; a first reading was not ready when the order was handed over; the comparison itself failed; nothing filled; the outcome was not proven within ten minutes of the entry's deadline; the Clerk restarted between the readings; the setting is off.
- **Another order is read from the books.** Any record written after the first reading began that names another effect, another order or another broker order: another bot's entry or exit, the bot's own exit, a manual order, an order from outside as the Clerk saw it. The comparison's own records are excluded: they are evidence about orders, not orders moving. And any other order the books still show working. "After" is the custody log's own order, not a clock: two records of one millisecond are still one before the other. Nothing ends at the second reading: the Clerk knows when it saw an outside order, never when that order traded, so everything up to the judgment counts.
- **Nothing filled is not judged.** The cost is zero then, and whether Alpaca gives the whole amount back at once after a cancel is not known. Judging it could hold a bot for Alpaca's own delay. Both readings are still taken and recorded for an order that was sent.
- **One record, once.** `ENTRY_BUYING_POWER_COMPARED` on the ENTER's own timeline, with a facts type of its own, so nothing that exists gains a field or hashes differently. It holds both readings as Alpaca wrote them, each with the time its read began and ended, the time the order was handed over, the fall, the premium paid, the commitment and its share for what filled, the result and its reason, and the account's own requirement before and after (next). It is written whatever the result.
- **The account's own requirement is recorded beside the commitment.** Alpaca values the whole account together, so two spreads that need $1,000 apart can need nothing together, and the bot's commitment, which is independent of every other bot's holdings (decision 28), cannot show that. The record therefore holds the account's collateral twice, in whole cents: over every option contract the books attribute to a bot as the first reading began, and as the second began once the outcome was proven. It is `incremental_account_requirement` over the signed holdings: two calls of `held_requirement_cents`, the one composition of held contracts at the multiplier the books recorded for each, over the same canonical portfolio requirement as decision 28. A bot's own held collateral (`option_position_money`) uses that same function. The figures are evidence. They change no result and hold nothing: holdings whose requirement cannot be worked out (a loss with no limit, a fraction of a contract) are recorded as absent and logged, and the comparison goes on. Both fields are left out of the record's text when absent, so a record written before them reads and hashes exactly as it did. An outside order the books attribute to no bot is not in them.
- **The judgment waits behind one setting.** `ALPACA_OPTION_BUYING_POWER_JUDGED`, off by default. Whether the figure falls by exactly an order's cost, and when it moves for an order that rests or is cancelled, has not been seen on an account. Off: everything above is read and recorded, a pair that could be judged reads unknown, "not switched on", and no bot is ever held. On: it is judged. The setting is read at the moment of judgment, and turning it on changes nothing else.
- **The hold.** An under-reserved result, judged, raises a notice of that bot's own, `OPTION_BUYING_POWER_UNDER_RESERVED`, in the uncertainty registry (ADR 0048). It names both figures. It blocks that bot's new entries and allows every reduction: its exit, the Clerk's scheduled close, a Flatten. No other bot is touched. It never ages out and no pass ends it. The notice is written before the record, so a Clerk that stops between the two writes leaves the bot held. The cause stores entries as a collection, although admission prevents a later opening entry while this notice stands.
- **The account is told, and not held.** A standing notice is also one line on the account's lane, worded for the account: it names the bot and both figures and says the same rule prices every option bot on the account, so the fault may be shared. It is read from the standing notice and adds no custody cause. Nothing is blocked by it: only the one bot is held (owner decision, 2026-10-06). The line ends with the notice.
- **Only the owner's review ends it.** A route of its own, in the pattern of the acknowledgement of an order from outside. It is not a recovery action: the Frontend keeps a complete map of those action ids, and a new id there is a change to the bot page, which belongs to the screens' work. The review is recorded as the notice's end under the operator's configured name. The comparison it named stays as it was recorded.
- **A simulation makes no comparison.** A Dry Run and a Live Shadow have no broker figure to read. Nothing is read and nothing is recorded there, and what is served says so by name.
- **A stock entry is untouched.** It makes no reading and writes no record. On books with no option entry the round's only work is one lookup of an empty table, once a process.
- **After a restart the first reading is gone.** It is kept in memory only. The first round of the new process records every option entry with no comparison as unknown, "the Clerk restarted".
- **Served.** A bot's entries, newest first (the newest twenty), each with its result, an authored label and sentence, every amount in cents and dollars, both readings with their times, the account's requirement before, after and its change with a sentence of its own, and the notice when one stands. Above them, the counts over all of the bot's option entries: how many were compared, how many were not, and why. An entry not compared yet is counted and served as that. Silence is never shown as agreement. The bot page draws these, and the owner's review sits there behind a confirmation.
- **Not known, and not done.** How and when Alpaca moves the figure: for an order that rests, at its fill, after a cancel. Fee and dividend postings are not read as cash moved; only transfers are. An order from outside that rests unchanged across both readings is not a reason by itself. A pass already under way when the second reading ended can reach its verdict after it and be counted. Several legs and sold legs: the requirement term is carried, and only zero has been exercised.

### 25. A Live Shadow takes an option plan

A Live Shadow sends nothing. For an option bot it is a real account's rules over a Dry Run's fill: its Clerk asks, refuses and schedules as a Paper account's does, and its order book fills as a Dry Run's simulation does. Almost all of both was already there, because the Clerk asks "does this port fill inside the send", and a shadow port always did. What was missing was the port's own option fill, the quotes behind it, and the places that said a Dry Run where they meant a simulation.

- **Who takes an option plan.** A Dry Run's account, a Live Shadow and a Paper account, and only one bought call or put. A Live account refuses new option openings while its setting is off, and continues taking closes (decision 24).
- **One fill for both simulations.** The option order of one leg that a no-submit port fills is one piece of code, used by a Dry Run's simulation and by a shadow order book: at once, in full, at the natural price of the quote the Clerk bound to the order, with every refusal of decision 16. A Shadow's fill goes into the shadow book, under the contract's own symbol and the shadow's own execution identity, so the Clerk folds it as it folds a Shadow's fill in shares. It is never priced from a bar. It never rests: a shadow book rests a limit in shares outside regular hours, and an option order whose limit the quote does not reach ends on the spot. An order of several legs is refused by name, and the Shadow's capabilities say one leg yes, several no.
- **The quotes are the account's.** They are kept in the shadow account's own folder, beside its order book: an entry's as its order is sent, an exit decision's, and the marks of the keeping. A Shadow's fill in shares is priced only from bars its own bot retained. An option close is priced from the newest quote kept for the contract in the account, whichever bot's entry or which round of the keeping wrote it. One contract has one market.
- **The Clerk's keeping follows a Shadow's books** as it follows a Dry Run's (decision 16). In the ordinary course a held contract is asked for as a quote line and marked every 15 seconds whether a run is alive or not, and a Shadow's run names no held line of its own. The account's reading marks a bought contract at the bid last stated, good for 80 seconds. A contract with no mark leaves the Shadow account with no reading, and every bot on that Shadow then waits for one, as it does for a share with no fresh bar.
- **Deploy rehearses the live account's admission.** A Shadow option Deploy reads that live account's option approval, buying power and contract listing, as a real-money Deploy would. The Shadow still submits nothing.
- **Alpaca's listing is asked.** Before an entry is judged, the picked contract is asked of Alpaca's contract reference, through the Shadow's read of the live account, within the same three seconds (decisions 11 and 19). A contract that is not listed, active, tradable, American and standard, or a listing that could not be read, refuses the entry by name. It is a read: a Shadow sends nothing. A Dry Run asks nothing, as before. The reason is the PRD's: a Shadow applies the refusals the Live Clerk would, and this is one.
- **An entry fills inside the send, or not at all.** The wait for what it came to is answered on the first read (decision 19). In the ordinary course its order keeps a deadline like any other and is never on the keeper's worklist. If the book's send raises, the keeper checks what happened and handles the entry by the same deadlines as a real account's.
- **A close follows a real account's send rules and takes a simulation's price.** It is sent only in the regular session and is held on a halt, as a Paper account's is. It is then filled from the newest quote kept: the exit decision's own, when it handed one. With none kept, or nothing bid, it is not sent and says so (decision 16), and an owner is not offered a Flatten the book could not price.
- **A Shadow has a keeper.** It takes a scheduled close (decision 20) on time. The Shadow's pass runs the same step and would do alone on a good day, but a pass that fails backs off to 300 seconds, and a close owed 45 minutes before the bell should not wait on that. In the ordinary course the keeper finds no entry to cancel and makes no last round. A close that failed is tried again by the watchdog, on the pass, as on a real account.
- **A stopped Shadow run keeps its contract.** Only a Dry Run is closed as its run ends. A Shadow rehearses a Live bot, whose Stop sells nothing. What a Shadow holds is closed by its bot's EXIT, the owner's Flatten, the owner's scheduled end when it is set to sell, and the Clerk's scheduled close.
- **A notice says that nothing real is held.** A missed close, the step after a close that was not taken, and the write-off that is not offered each speak of a Live Shadow, and never send the owner to look at Alpaca for an exercise or a sale that cannot have happened.
- **A contract held past its expiry settles as on a Dry Run.** The shared simulation step of decision 22 settles it in cash at its exercise value or at zero, never into shares, and the owner can end a bought contract for which no closing price was retained. Nothing is sent to Alpaca. A held contract no longer blocks the whole Shadow for good. A session close with no mark for a held contract still costs the whole Shadow its reading for the next session, as it does for shares; the next complete session repairs that baseline.
- **An unavailable reading says why.** When a held option, missing mark or damaged kept quote leaves a simulation without a reading, a bot's refused entry carries that reading's own sentence. Real-account words are unchanged.
- **Not built:** several legs on a Shadow; the PRD's note of whether a limit stayed marketable through the wait a real order would have had.
- **Not checked against Alpaca:** nothing here has been. The listing read on a live account is the request the Paper path makes, and that has never been sent.

### 27. One option order of several legs has one parent, and custody belongs to each leg

An order of several legs is sent and cancelled once, while its contracts can fill unevenly. Counting only the parent's net price would lose which contracts the bot holds; treating each leg as an independent order would send or cancel work that was never asked of the broker. We retain one parent order and an immutable row for each leg. The parent has no symbol, side or fill. Every execution belongs to its leg, bound to that contract's multiplier. A one-contract entry keeps its previous facts and rows.

- Whole structures filled are the smallest whole number that every leg's filled contracts and ratio support. Contracts beyond that number remain the bot's holdings. A terminal cancel with some contracts filled is a partial entry even when no complete structure filled. A parent reported filled without coverage of every requested contract remains unknown.
- A real submit answer acknowledges identities and states; an exact option lookup proves per-leg cumulative fills. A simulation retains exact executions for all legs together, or fills none. Repeated and reordered answers reuse the existing per-order evidence rules.
- The broker is contacted by the parent's client ID. The books' leg references are never sent or cancelled as independent orders. An early leg observation is deferred while the parent's answer still owes its identities; failure to recover that answer remains unknown rather than declaring the leg foreign.
- Every contract's listing is read concurrently within one three-second bound, alongside one account buying-power reading. A missing, inactive or unreadable listing refuses the whole entry. The existing entry deadline cancels the parent and proves every leg's result, including after a restart.
- A final uneven entry holds only its bot's new exposure. The notice names the confirmed contracts and uncovered sales, allows confirmed reductions and Flatten, and ends only when its named holdings are flat. Reconciliation re-derives a notice lost to a crash. A resolved notice for unchanged entry fills does not reappear when a later entry uses those contracts.
- A parent's reservation keeps whole units and the positive dollars one unit sets aside before fees: requirement plus signed premium. This field is a commitment, not a share price; no contract multiplier is applied again. Unseen purchases claim their actual cost, while unseen sales are handled by the account's existing sales projection, so the credit is not counted twice.
- Registration, entry, Deploy and the preview share one plan rule. The implementation limits that originally kept several-leg structures and Live Shadow closed are superseded by decision 28. `ALPACA_MULTI_LEG_OPTION_ORDERS_ENABLED` defaults off for Paper and Live; the separate Live opening switch also remains off.

### 28. A structure closes its confirmed contracts, and sold holdings retain collateral

The entry seam alone did not make a deployable structure: a close could reduce only one contract, and money readers refused every sold lot. Dry Run and Live Shadow now take supported structures of two to four legs. Real accounts retain decision 27's rollout setting; building this path does not enable that setting or authorize broker orders.

An EXIT captures the entry's durable contracts and cancels outstanding parent orders before sizing one market close from the signed holdings. Closing units are their absolute quantities' greatest common divisor and each ratio is its quantity divided by those units. An uneven entry can therefore close its exact contracts, including a lone residual. Every requested leg must still equal its confirmed holding before the first send. Expired contracts are left to the event path. The close has one parent identity, its executions land on contract-bound child rows, and success requires every captured holding flat. Restart, lost responses, and partial terminal closes use the same exact lookups and uncertainty fences as other orders. A terminal parent does not end custody until every child has terminal and execution evidence. The existing single-contract operator attestation is not offered or accepted for an EXIT that captured multiple contracts, including a lone residual close: absence checks and the recorded attestation must first be extended to prove every contract. Automatic exact lookups continue while custody stays held. A simulation validates every closing quote and every reduction before writing any leg; it sells bought legs at bid and buys sold legs at ask.

A budgeted bot's held commitment is signed FIFO basis plus the canonical requirement of its actual holdings. The account reserves that collateral separately from observed cash, including after Stop, so premium received cannot be offered as free money while still pledged. The money map subtracts collateral from cash and includes it in the held commitment, preserving wealth at cost and exact conservation. Unbounded or incoherent residual holdings make money unavailable rather than estimating collateral; confirmed reductions remain possible. Bought holdings mark at bid and sold holdings at ask. The entry test and the unreserved figure are one rule: an entry is admitted when its commitment fits the bot's free budget and fits the account headroom for that bot, the account's unreserved cash plus the bot's own claim, so collateral held by any bot, a stopped one included, is never spent by another bot's entry (#3096). A bot reserves its own commitment whatever Alpaca would net against another bot's position; a netting saving is never spent. One funding answer (`app/broker/alpaca/clerk/funding.py`, #3097) states that rule for the Clerk's admission and the bot page alike, so the same entry at the same moment is never promised by a screen and refused by the Clerk: in whole cents, the entry rounded up and each limit rounded down, so an entry that fits a limit only within the same cent is refused (this never admits more than the exact comparison did); the limit with the least room named with its shortfall; a figure that is stale or cannot be worked out is said so, never read as zero and never as a pass; and Alpaca's option buying power can bind but never changes what the Clerk admits. At Deploy the same module judges the budget typed (`deploy_funding_answer`, #3099), for a bot that does not exist yet: the budget is set aside out of the account's unreserved cash, so the account must fund all of it as well as one entry, and the Deploy review, the option review beside it and the Clerk's own re-check at the commit read that one answer, in the same whole cents, instead of each comparing money. A Dry Run's cash is the amount typed, so no account limit is said. Alpaca's option buying power reaches it as a figure that keeps the time it was read, and one that is missing or stale is shown as such and is never a pass. The option review shows, for one more structure, the account's requirement after it less before (`incremental_account_requirement` over the account's holdings with the picked contracts added count by count), set beside the requirement of the structure's own legs: when Alpaca would net the positions the review says so and says the bot does not spend the saving. It also shows how many whole entries fit under the limit with the least room, said as competing with every other bot for the same account headroom and never summed.

The four-leg limit belongs to a submitted order, not the bot's accumulated holdings. Held collateral follows Alpaca's portfolio rule: combine signed holdings for each underlying, multiplier and expiry, then retain the largest independently bounded expiry requirement per underlying and multiplier. Independent underlyings and multipliers add; a later-expiring long call cannot cover an unbounded earlier expiry. This keeps portfolio histories valued without weakening the order-shape or one-open-entry fences. A net credit at cost stays signed in the money partition, including after Stop; its visual width is zero, while its dollars still reconcile with cash. Once a scheduled close leaves the bot flat, its status names every captured contract from the immutable entry records, including completed closes recorded before this change.

One exit estimate (`app/broker/alpaca/clerk/exit_estimate.py`, #3100) says what getting out of a held option position costs at the quotes held, before fees, in two figures: closing everything as one order (each sold leg bought back at its ask, each bought leg sold at its bid), and buying the sold legs back alone, which Alpaca documents may need buying power the close's own relief does not fund. Each figure is in whole cents with its terms, and is unknown when a side it needs is no price, stale past the age a held contract's mark is allowed (one limit, `kept_option_quotes.HELD_MARK_MAX_AGE_MS`, counted from a side's own receipt and not from its hand-over, so a quiet contract can read stale while its mark is fresh), and never zero when it is not known. A crossed quote is no price. A Dry Run and a Live Shadow price it from the quotes they keep, as a simulated close is priced; a real account keeps none, so it is priced from the live quotes while the bot's run asks for them, and says so when none is held. It informs and blocks nothing.

One account picture (`app/broker/alpaca/clerk/account_picture.py`, #3101) says what the account owns, what is spoken for, where its collateral is and what it could be obliged to, as figures with their source, age and state. The cash and the marked equity are two figures, simulated and so named on a Dry Run and a Live Shadow. What is spoken for is a partition of the cash into the bots' free claims, collateral, entry orders, fees not posted yet, purchases from outside the system and what is left unreserved: the lines add up to the cash to the cent (the budget's own identity, one visible rounding line where a claim is not whole cents), each line opens by bot with stopped bots named, and a line that cannot be worked out makes the partition unknown and is never left out. Collateral is shown by underlying and expiry, each group valued alone, beside the account's own figure, which is the canonical portfolio requirement of every bot's contracts netted together: Alpaca takes the largest expiry of each underlying, so the rows can add up to more, and the bots' own reservations, which are independent (decision 28), can add up to more than either. The assignment obligation is the sold contracts' strike times multiplier, in whole cents by underlying, gross and apart from any loss. The cost to close every option position is the sum of the bots' exit estimates, and is unknown, with the count of bots that cannot be priced, when any is. It informs and blocks nothing, and no limit on combined exposure is added (owner decision 2026-10-06).

Simulation expiry applies decision 22's cash-only exercise-value rule to either held side: a bought contract receives that value and a sold contract pays it. Closing fees use the reducing side. The immutable settlement records the held side, cash sign and quote evidence; replay validates those figures and ends only the matching lots. It delivers no shares and claims no equivalence to real assignment. A sold contract with no retained closing price remains held; the owner's unpriced ending is available only for bought contracts. Real option events retain decision 22's assignment, exercise and share-residue rules.

## Alternatives considered

- **Ask Alpaca what an order costs.** Its references describe no such call. The account does report an option buying power, which Deploy is to read as a second check, but that is an account-wide figure and not the cost of one order.
- **Use the loss at expiry as the amount to set aside.** Rejected: Alpaca holds the requirement, not the loss, and the loss is not estimated at all when expiries are mixed. Keeping both lets the bot compare its own figure with Alpaca's.
- **Keep Black-Scholes and call it close enough.** Rejected: it gives a deep put less than exercising it pays, and it cannot see why a call is exercised the day before a dividend. The PRD allows an approximation only with its measured cost; the tree is the model itself, and its measured cost is the step count's.
- **Take delta from IBKR.** Rejected by the owner's rule that a number picking a contract must be one this repo can check. IBKR's delta is to be recorded beside ours, and blocks nothing.
- **Check the payoff only at the strikes.** Rejected: it reports zero for one bought 450 put against two sold 440 puts, whose payoff is above zero at both strikes and falls to minus $430 a share at a price of zero.
- **Add an option field to the stock leg.** Rejected: the stock leg's dump feeds durable hashes, and a new field moves every one of them unless it is left out on both the writing and the checking side for ever. New types beside it cannot move a stock order's identity.
- **Put the three option fields on the account snapshot.** Rejected for now: a route serves that model, so it would change the served contract for fields nothing on a screen reads yet. The cost is a second account request when a caller wants both.
- **Ask the lookup by client order id for nested legs with an undocumented parameter.** Rejected: Alpaca documents the parameter on the read by broker order id. A second, documented read costs one request and guesses nothing.
- **Read an option order as leniently as a stock order.** Rejected: leniency there protects a list from one bad row. Here it would turn an unreadable fill into no fill.
- **Apply the stock sub-penny rule to an option's limit price.** Rejected: that rule is written for stock orders, and an option trades in steps the documents read do not state.
- **Ask Alpaca for each option activity code by name.** Rejected: two of Alpaca's pages give the exercise different codes. Asking for one could miss the other, and asking for a code Alpaca does not know could be refused. The category walk costs more rows and guesses nothing.
- **Refuse the whole read when one option row cannot be read.** Rejected: a row in a contract this app cannot name, such as an adjusted one the owner traded by hand, would stop every bot's events from being read for as long as it stayed in the window. Listing the row keeps it in view and lets the others through.
- **Take the shares and cash from Alpaca's share-trade row.** Rejected: nothing on that row names the contract or the event it belongs to, and Alpaca's own example of one prints a price that contradicts the sentence beneath it. The contract's terms are certain; the row is checked against them.
- **Add the option fields to the existing activity model.** Rejected: that model is served and holds its numbers as floats.
- **Let an expiry take lots on either side, oldest first.** Rejected, after it was built. With a contract held on both sides it took the row's count from the oldest lot whatever its side, and moved the books the opposite way to the row. And an expiry matched before its assignment took the oldest lots, so the assignment fell on the bot that sold last.
- **Leave which bot is assigned to the order the rows are matched in.** Rejected: an order can be kept only among the rows of one read, and a row can arrive a poll after the one it belongs with. With an expiry taking the newest lots, an expiry and its assignment or exercise give the same answer in either order. Two assignments of different dates still do not: when a lot was opened between their dates and the later-dated one is matched first, the earlier is refused for want of contracts. Nothing wrong is booked, and the caller holds.
- **Refuse an expiry whose sign is not the side held.** Not done: every expiry the pages show is signed negative, so the sign of a sold one is an inference, and refusing on it would hold every sold option's expiry if the inference is wrong. The sign is kept on the record and is given to the rule.
- **Close every lot on both sides when an expiry's count equals their net.** Not done: when the two sides net to nothing Alpaca has no position to expire and may report no row at all, so a contract held on both sides cannot be settled from the activity alone. The Clerk sets such a contract against the broker's position once it has expired and holds both bots by name (decision 22). It does not close the lots itself: with no row it cannot know whether they ended worth something.
- **A new transition kind for an option entry.** Rejected: many readers key on `ENTER_ACCEPTED` and `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED` and on the order's symbol and quantity in them: the order projection, the exact-lookup worklists, the flat proof, the run-end close, the failed-entry fence. A new kind is silently skipped by every reader that is missed. The same kind with the option's own values is read correctly by all of them, and the places that would build a stock leg from it fail loudly.
- **Let the caller hand over the commitment, or the limit price.** Rejected: the Clerk would then set aside a figure it had not worked out.
- **Leave a posted fee row of an option day unowned until its attribution was built.** Rejected, after it was built: an unowned charge leaves the account's fees unresolved, which stops new entries for every bot on the account, stock bots included, and no command settles it. Sharing the row by each fill's own model can charge the wrong bot a few cents; it cannot stop the account.
- **Book the shares as a second transition.** Rejected: a second transition is a second commit, and a stop between the two would leave the option gone and the shares never booked. One transition's fold writes both.
- **A new table for option event bookings.** Rejected: every reader of money reads `fills` through `orders`, and each would have to be taught the new table. A reader that was missed would count a bot's money wrongly. The existing rows are counted correctly by all of them, and a stock store's bytes do not move.
- **Record an expiry's close as a fill at the last bid.** Rejected as in decision 16: nothing traded. The lot closes at zero and the premium is realised in it.
- **Hold the whole account for every option hold.** Rejected: one bot's unreadable row would stop every other bot's entries. A hold is account-wide only where its cause names no bot.
- **End a hold after some days.** Rejected: ADR 0048 gives a hold no clock. A hold ends by proof or by the owner.
- **Close both lots of a contract held on both sides once it has expired with no row.** Not done: with no row the Clerk cannot know whether the contract ended in the money, so it cannot say what each bot is owed. Both bots are held by name.
- **Let the simulation settle from the last price it kept on any day.** Rejected: that is not the contract's last price. With no price from the last session nothing is settled.
- **Exercise a Dry Run's in-the-money contract into shares.** Built first, then rejected: one call struck at 585 bought $58,500 of shares on a $1,000 budget, and a put left the bot short and the account's budget unreadable. The simulation books the sale Alpaca says it makes of such a position, in cash.
- **Settle a Dry Run's contract from the newest bar of its last session, however old.** Built first, then rejected: a read half a second after the close settled from the 15:59 bar before the closing bar had landed, and a bot stopped at 09:35 settled from that morning's price. The closing bar is waited for, and a bar more than fifteen minutes before the close is no price.
- **Settle a Dry Run's contract inside its read of option events.** Built first, then rejected: a write hidden in a read, with its record on a second channel. It is one explicit step of the trade port.
- **Add a fourth kind to the rule that chooses lots.** Rejected: that rule is the broker's three kinds, held to a golden fixture. A simulation's settlement travels as an expiry and is recorded as what the simulation's own record says it was.
- **Write off an expired contract nothing settles.** Rejected: the residue write-off leaves the lot open, so the premium is never a loss and the budget carries it as a position for good. The owner's ending closes the lot.
- **Offer the owner's ending as soon as the broker shows none of the contract.** Rejected: Paper publishes an activity the day after. An owner who ended a contract that was in fact exercised would have real shares on no bot's books. One full session is waited, the Clerk looks once more at the press, and a late row is still read.
- **Keep the account-wide hold for an outside order in a bot's contract.** Rejected: that hold refuses every reduction of every bot of the account for one bot's one contract.
- **Let the bot's own close go out beside a working outside order in the same contract.** Built first, then rejected: with Alpaca's sale working and unfilled the positions agree, so nothing else refused a second order to sell the same contracts. The bot's own order in that one contract now waits until the outside order has settled or the owner has reviewed it.
- **Refuse the bot's reduction through the hold's own proof.** Rejected: the hold is raised by a pass or a poll, minutes after the stream may have shown the order. The record of the order itself is asked.
- **Make the shares reducible by teaching each reduction about a new order role.** Rejected, after it was tried: the entry role is read in a dozen places. The shares' order takes the entry role, and the EXIT's two proofs that look an entry up at the broker leave it out.
- **Wait for the fill inside the call that sends the entry.** Rejected: it would hold the one path stock bots share open for the whole wait, and put the wait behind a lock other bots need.
- **A second, hand-kept worklist for the deadline keeper.** Rejected, after it was built: it asked that the ENTER had not ended, the stopped-run step it copied did not, and an entry voided as never sent whose order later showed was cancelled by nobody. One predicate is read by both.
- **Read the keeper's worklist from the custody log.** Rejected, after it was built: every round and every pass scanned the whole log under the store's write lock, on stock-only accounts too.
- **Record every uncertain cancel.** Rejected for the keeper: asked again every second, a cancel Alpaca kept refusing wrote two records a round for as long as it lasted. Every other caller still records each attempt.
- **Answer "entered" at once for a limit already past.** Originally rejected. #2976 accepts this as an explicit “judge now” request: the Clerk records the answer, which the quarter-second workaround could not guarantee if the clock advanced between calls.
- **Take any multiplier the caller names.** Rejected: a hundred contracts counted at a multiplier of one were admitted for one contract's premium. Only the standard 100 is taken until the broker's listing is read for the contract.
- **Keep every quote ever handed over.** Rejected: fifty contracts for one day is 390,000 rows and 80 MB, and every mark read parsed all of it. A fill keeps the quote it used in its own record, so nothing is lost by cutting the working file back.
- **Record a close with nothing bid as a sale for nothing, or as unpriced with a flag.** Not built: a fill row needs a price above zero, and FIFO, cash and fees all read it. Refusing the close leaves the books true and the position open.
- **Sell an expired contract at the last bid kept before it expired.** Rejected: that books a sale that could not have happened, at a price from another day. The true outcome is the exercise value or nothing, and that belongs to settling the expiry.
- **Hold a Dry Run's close of an ended run for the next open, as every other option close is held.** Not chosen: the owner's rule for a Dry Run is that its simulation closes what an ended run left on every kind of ending, overnight included, at the last price it knew. Holding it would leave a stopped Dry Run holding a contract nobody marks until the open. The owner may still prefer it; it is one rule in `exit_resolution._send_verdict`.
- **Ask the IBKR stock quote for a contract's symbol to price a Flatten.** Rejected: it never answers, and each ask put the contract on the watched list of shares for a minute.
- **Run a search in a thread of the Clerk's process.** Rejected: it does not free the event loop. The pricer lets go of the interpreter lock for under a microsecond at the top of each tree, and a thread waiting for the lock starts its five-millisecond wait again each time, so the loop's thread never gets in. Measured, the loop stopped for the whole search.
- **Hoist the pricer's array out of the tree and let one search run at a time.** Rejected as the fix: in a scratch run it cut the loop's longest gap to about 22 ms, but that rests on which numpy calls happen to let go of the lock at these array sizes. It is not something a money path should stand on.
- **One worker process for entries and previews both.** Rejected: a page's search would sit ahead of a bot's entry. Two processes cost memory, and only on a Clerk that uses them.
- **Fork the worker.** Rejected: the child would start with copies of the Clerk's sockets, threads and running loop.
- **One lock around every lookup of the rate and the dividends.** Rejected: a slow answer about one ticker made every other preview say "still being looked up", and a FRED that hung left the dividends unknown on every Clerk though Polygon had answered at once.
- **Have the preview call the entry's own preparation and catch what it raises.** Rejected: it stops at the first refusal, so a structure with a wide leg and a cost over its limit would show one of the two. One function that returns every refusal serves both, and the entry raises the first.
- **Edit the refusals at their source so that they name a leg by its place.** Rejected: an entry's refusal is kept with the entry in the words it was said in, and the resolver and the plan are handed ids, not a page's row numbers. The preview turns the words on their way out.
- **Loosen the request so that a number out of range reaches the plan's own validation.** Rejected: the request's legs are the Action Plan's own rule types, and a looser copy of each would be a second statement of every rule. The request stays strict, and its refusal is read back against the ranges already served.
- **Work the decay at 2,003 steps when a dividend is near.** Not done: each leg's volatility was solved at the default steps, and a later price from another tree would mix two step errors in one difference. The figure says it is rough there.
- **A yearly theta divided by 365.** Rejected with decision 6: it runs away in a contract's last days, and it is not a number the second engine computes.
- **Price the decay in a thread of the Clerk's process.** Rejected for decision 18's reason: the pricer does not free the event loop.
- **Give each Clerk a Polygon key and a FRED key.** Rejected: a Clerk holds no vendor key by a rule a test enforces (#2204). The coordinator already answers a Clerk's chart-history read; this is one more read beside it.
- **Run the keeping as a task of each Dry Run's Clerk runtime.** Rejected: that runtime is closed the moment its run ends, which is when a position the run could not sell most needs a quote. Holding the runtime open instead would stop the passes that reopening it runs, which are what retry the close.
- **Let the runner hand the Clerk a mark at every closed bar.** Rejected: a runner that is slow, stopped or dead hands over nothing, and one missed hand-over in the last 80 seconds of a session costs the next day its starting equity.
- **Keep a mark only when a decision could use the quote: both sides within five seconds.** Built first, and replaced. A calm contract's sides tick only when they change, so it had no mark most of the time. What makes a standing quote untrustworthy is a dead line, and the generation, the kind of data and the feed's health already say that. The age is still on the row, for whatever prices a close.
- **Restate a mark at any hour the line holds a whole quote.** Rejected: after the close it would state the closing quote as fresh all night, and at the next open pass yesterday's bid off as today's.
- **Read the books before each pass and wait for them,** so a contract is asked for in the pass after it is bought. Rejected: the stock quotes would wait on every Dry Run's write lock in turn. Reading a pass behind costs one line one pass, and only when a run ends in the second it bought.
- **Wait for the keeping inside a bound.** Rejected: any wait in the pass is paid by every bot in the process, and a status source that is late blocks entries.
- **Send the keeping's marks through the Clerk's door while its Clerk is open.** Rejected: the door judged nothing, so there would be two ways to write a mark and the test in neither. The door was deleted and the keeping tests before it writes.
- **Estimate an option bot's next entry from a share of the underlying.** That was the bug: one share of SPY at $585.13 shown as the cost of a call that cost $412.00. No figure is shown.
- **Say an option bot waits while less than $1.00 of its budget is free,** since a standard contract cannot cost less. True today, and not chosen: it rests on the penny tick, the multiplier of 100 and one contract a structure, each held by other code. "Nothing free" is true of every structure, because a commitment is above zero.
- **Have the runner hand over whole quotes only,** so that a one-sided line never reaches the kept quotes. Rejected: a close would then sell at the bid kept before a data loss, on both roads, though the line's last word had no bid.
- **Leave a one-sided line out of the file when no runner is alive.** Built first, and replaced: what an owner's Flatten did then depended on whether a runner had happened to hand the line over first.
- **Let a quote with only a bid be the account's mark while it is fresh.** Rejected: a mark is a whole quote, tested as one.
- **Let a quote with one side end the mark stated before it.** Not chosen: a whole quote stated before a data loss already stands for its 80 seconds when nothing has ticked since, and a one-sided quote in the last 80 seconds of a session would cost the next day its starting equity. The reading is the same with a one-sided quote put back as without one.
- **Refuse every entry on a Clerk with a shared status source, and let the run go on.** Not chosen: the bot would hold a search worker and decide all day to no end. The run is refused once, as it starts, as a bot whose window cannot fit is.
- **Take an order out of a receipt by its place.** Rejected: an EXIT's receipt names the entry first, and names only the entry while its close is held for the open. The Clerk is asked by the decision's id.
- **Record a refused start as "failed to launch".** Not done: that kind is the launch's own compensation, with one fixed reason, and carries no sentence. Nothing was added to a served vocabulary for this.
- **Refuse a bot whose window cannot fit at Start admission.** Not built here: admission has no option fact yet. The run is refused as it starts, by the same code Deploy's check will use.
- **Centre a running bot's window on the underlying's live quote throughout.** Not chosen: a strike rule counts from the bar's close, so the window follows the bar's close.
- **Wait for an entry's outcome until the bot's next decision bar closes.** That is what the PRD says, and it is not built. While the runner waits in its loop nothing reads its bar stream, and the IBKR stream calls a line stalled, and replaces it for every bot on the symbol, when its reader has seen no bar for sixty seconds. The wait is fifty seconds from the bar's close instead. To wait longer, the stream must first stop reading a reader that was away, and finds bars queued, as a stalled line: a change to the feed every stock bot uses, not made here.
- **Go on reading bars while an entry's outcome is waited for, and hold only the next decision.** Not chosen: a run has one bar in flight so that the feed is never told a bar was taken before the strategy decided on it, and the staged decision is settled by the one loop that staged it. Two tasks on one strategy would need a rule for which settles first.
- **Count an option entry as entered when the Clerk takes it, and correct the strategy later if it ends unfilled.** Rejected: a staged decision is settled once, and a committed entry is not taken back. An entry that ended unfilled would leave the strategy counting a position the bot does not hold until its own exit came.
- **Write a journal row for an entry that bought nothing.** Not done: a Dry Run's journal is its record of fills, and every reader of it takes a row for a fill with a quantity and a price. What such an entry came to is in the Clerk's record and the runner's log.
- **Have the Clerk's keeping ask for what a Paper account's bots hold.** Left: the keeping exists to state marks, which a real account does not take from it. The bot's own run asks while it lasts, from the same read of the books.
- **Have a Paper run ask the Clerk what its own bot holds, at every bar.** Not chosen: that answer walks every entry the bot ever made. The account's contracts to watch are one read, and the run keeps those on its own underlying. A stopped Paper bot's held contract has no line until the keeping follows real accounts for demand alone.
- **Replay every ENTER by its effect's state, option entries included.** Rejected: an entry settled as entered before it was proven, and then proven unfilled, ends failed. The live run went on as in a position, so a replay must too. The settlement the Clerk recorded is what the strategy was told.
- **Let the runner settle an entry as entered by itself when its send outran the wait.** Built first, and replaced. The Clerk recorded nothing for it, so a later Start replayed it from the books and could differ from the live run. The runner now asks the Clerk even with a limit already past; the Clerk judges and records immediately.
- **Leave the bound on the entry wait to Deploy alone.** Still not chosen: an entry taken late can outlast the runner's wait. #2976 moves the per-entry refusal to the Clerk's acceptance, using the actual cancel time rather than the runner's estimate.
- **Hand a real account's exit the held contract's quote, in case something reads it.** Built first, and removed: nothing on a Paper account prices a close from it, and the read could end the run before the sale.
- **Log the Clerk's own standing in place of the runner's words.** Not chosen: the owner asked for the outcome in a closed set of plain words. The set gained "unproven", so an order nobody can place is never called resting, and each log line carries the standing beside the word.
- **Tell each runner whether its run names held contracts.** Built first, as a flag set by the Paper runner, and replaced: the Clerk knows whether its own keeping asks for them, and answers the one question both runners ask.
