# ADR 0047 — Authority recovery is an offline ceremony, not a panel action

**Status:** Accepted 2026-08-25
**Provenance:** Decision ticket [#1779](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/1779), child of the Two-Tap fleet-freeze umbrella [#1773](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/1773). The finding and its correction are recorded in `docs/audits/bot-fleet-stress-2026-08-25.md` § S15c (original claim, lines 100-103) and its 2026-08-25 correction block (lines 121-127). Every claim below was re-verified against source on the `feat/wp3-symbol-scoped-deploy-health` tree before this ADR was written.
**Decision drivers:** The 50-bot stress campaign left one account (`g01-dv-spy-0825`, PA3KWXU1C4C3) frozen with every advertised remedy dead-ending, and the audit recorded `reset_authority` as the escape "now armed since the account is flat and order-free". It was not armed, and could not be. The panel has been advertising two recovery buttons that no production code path can enable, execute, or ever cure.
**Related:** ADR 0035 and ADR 0037 (SQLite is the sole Alpaca custody authority — this ADR governs how that authority is *replaced*), ADR 0030 (account-rooted Clerk), ADR 0027 (operator blocker disposition taxonomy — supplies the `fix_elsewhere` disposition this decision requires), ADR 0041 (the operator manual's Button Reference is generated from the backend copy map, so removing an action id removes its manual entry), ADR 0013 (judgment vs evidence — the backend authors what the operator may do), ADR 0039 (ADR status is decision standing, not code conformance: the surface changes below are follow-up work).
**Vocabulary:** none owed. This decision removes two action ids from an operator surface and re-homes their cure in an existing runbook; it introduces no new trading- or operator-domain term. The `CONTEXT.md` entries for custody authority, generation, and recovery are unchanged. Per ADR 0040 Decision 4.

## Context

The bot panel presents two authority-recovery capabilities, `rebuild_from_mirror` and `reset_authority`. They do not come from the lifecycle action registry — `ACTION_REGISTRY` (`PythonDataService/app/broker/v2panel/action_policy.py:376-464`) holds only `deploy`, `resume`, `pause`, `continue`, `stop`, `flatten_stop`, `retire`, `cancel_order` and `reconcile_now`, and neither recovery id appears in it. They reach the panel through the Clerk's own recovery-capability catalog: `_FAILURE_DESCRIPTORS` (`PythonDataService/app/broker/alpaca/clerk/sqlite/recovery_policy.py:228-260`), which `sqlite_panel_adapter._panel_action` (`PythonDataService/app/services/broker_v2_panel/sqlite_panel_adapter.py:310-332`) turns into `PanelAction` rows with `enabled=capability.available`.

They have never been available, and three independent mechanisms each guarantee that on their own.

**1. The proof they gate on is never constructed.** Availability is decided by `_failure_decision` (`recovery_policy.py:782-846`) from `ctx.recovery_proof`. That field is an `AuthorityRecoveryProof` whose defaults are `mirror_verified=False`, `broker_account_flat=None`, `broker_order_free=None` (`recovery_policy.py:83-89`), and `RecoveryPolicyContext.recovery_proof` defaults to exactly that value (`recovery_policy.py:108`). The repo contains **two** production constructions of `RecoveryPolicyContext` — `projections.py:256-275` and `services/sqlite_clerk_compat.py:107-121` — and **neither passes `recovery_proof`**. So `rebuild_from_mirror` is permanently `VERIFIED_MIRROR_REQUIRED` (`recovery_policy.py:795-796`) and `reset_authority` permanently `FRESH_FLAT_ORDER_FREE_PROOF_REQUIRED` (`recovery_policy.py:819-826`).

**2. Even with a proof, recovery refuses while the Clerk holds its lease — and the sweep is the lease holder.** All three destructive entry points route through `_assert_no_live_lease` (`recovery.py:956-987`), called at `recovery.py:532` (restore), `:661` (mirror rebuild) and `:762` (reset). It reads `execution_lease_owner` / `execution_lease_expires_at_ms` from `control_meta` and, on an unexpired lease, raises `RecoveryRefused("authority recovery requires the active Clerk process to be stopped")` (`recovery.py:986`). The lease is held and renewed by the reconciliation sweep: `ReconciliationSweep.start_lease_heartbeat` (`reconciliation_sweep.py:73-86`) and `_run_lease_heartbeat` (`reconciliation_sweep.py:100-116`) call `repo.renew_execution_lease` (`repository.py:346-355` → `:330-344`) at `lease_ttl_ms / 3` (`reconciliation_sweep.py:30`, `:58-61`) — every **10 s** against the 30 s default TTL (`repository.py:100`). Since #1776 the sweep is also the account's *sole* automatic reconciler. The bind is therefore structural, not a bug: the only component that could produce a fresh flat-and-order-free observation is the same component whose liveness forbids acting on it.

**3. The panel's execution path has no branch for either action.** `execute_recovery_action` (`recovery_execution.py:89-241`) dispatches `resolve_execution_coverage`, `stop_bot_decisions`, `reconcile_now`, `cancel_verified_working_orders` and `execute_safe_flatten`, then falls through to `raise RecoveryExecutionError(f"{request.action_id} is navigation, preparation, or offline authority recovery; it has no direct broker mutation")` (`recovery_execution.py:238-241`). The dispatcher already classifies these two as *offline* recovery. Arming the buttons would have produced a 4xx, not a recovery.

**What the operator sees instead of a cure.** The two capabilities only render at all when `authority_health != "healthy"` (`recovery_policy.py:851`), and `projections.py:261` hardcodes `"healthy"` — so the sole producer is `services/sqlite_clerk_compat.failed_sqlite_projection` (`sqlite_clerk_compat.py:112`), reached when an activated authority fails to boot. In that posture every capability in the failure catalog is unavailable, including `open_custody_timeline` (`recovery_policy.py:435-436`: available only when healthy). `_capability_blocker` then authors a `wait` disposition with **no move**, because `curable_here` is true only for `freshness == "stale"` and a missing observation reads `"unavailable"` (`sqlite_panel_adapter.py:357`, `:364`, `:368`; `recovery_policy.py:291-293`). And because no capability is available, `_custody_condition` takes its `primary is not None` branch and posts `wait` rather than the `terminal` disposition it reserves for an unhealthy authority with no capability at all (`account_operator_posture.py:216-232`). The panel tells the operator to wait for a refresh that no running process will ever perform.

**The offline path already exists, complete.** `PythonDataService/scripts/manage_alpaca_sqlite_clerk.py` is a working CLI with `backup`, `verify`, `restore`, `rebuild` (`:60`, dispatch `:154`), `reset` (`:73`, dispatch `:181`), `upgrade-v9`, `rollback-v9`, `dev-reset` and the cutover ceremonies. It is documented end-to-end in `docs/runbooks/alpaca-sqlite-clerk-recovery-and-cutover.md`, whose "Stop boundary" section (lines 83-127) states the same invariant the code enforces — stop every governed bot, stop the lease-owning process, and supply account-bound process-stop evidence when a corrupt database makes the lease row unreadable. The compat module's own docstring has said so all along: *"Offline rebuild/reset capabilities are present but unavailable until their external proof is verified after the service process is stopped"* (`sqlite_clerk_compat.py:90-92`).

The offline path also already specifies the proof source the umbrella asked to pin down. Reset consumes an operator-supplied `ResetBrokerProof` file — `account_id`, `observed_at_ms`, `proof_reference`, `positions`, `open_order_ids` (`recovery.py:177-185`) — read from disk by the CLI (`manage_alpaca_sqlite_clerk.py:279-297`) and validated at `recovery.py:1176-1217`: account-bound, freshness-bounded by `--max-evidence-age-ms`, every position flat under the canonical epsilon, no open orders, and the stopped-bot roster equal to the expected roster. That proof is captured *at recovery time from the broker*, independent of any Clerk. It is not the sweep's observation, which by construction dies with the authority it belongs to.

## Decision

1. **Authority recovery is an out-of-band ceremony performed against a stopped authority.** `rebuild_from_mirror` and `reset_authority` are not operator-panel actions and will not be made into any. The recovery module's stopped-process precondition (`recovery.py:956-987`) is upheld as designed, not worked around.

2. **The two capabilities leave the presented action surface.** Remove them from `_FAILURE_DESCRIPTORS` (`recovery_policy.py:228-260`), from `RecoveryActionId` (`recovery_policy.py:44-55`), from the panel's `ActionId` / `ACTION_IDS` vocabulary and `OPERATOR_COPY` (`app/broker/v2panel/vocabulary.py:100-101`, `:120-121`, `:329-336`), and from the regenerated artifacts that derive from them — `vocabulary.snapshot.json`, the OpenAPI contract, the generated Frontend types, and the operator manual's Button Reference (ADR 0041). The Frontend's local copy and tone maps (`Frontend/src/app/components/broker/v2-panel/lib/broker-v2-emergency-copy.ts`, `.../bot-detail-banner/lifecycle-action.ts`, `.../operator-lens/operator-bot-banner/operator-bot-banner.component.ts`) drop the same two ids. **Panel honesty is restored by absence**, which is the only remedy available: a control that can never enable is a false affordance, not merely bad copy.

3. **A failed authority presents no capabilities at all, and says so terminally.** `open_custody_timeline` fails the same test in the same posture — permanently unavailable, with a next step (*"Stop the process and verify the preserved database or finalized mirror offline"*) that is offline guidance wearing a button. `_FAILURE_DESCRIPTORS` therefore becomes empty, which routes `_custody_condition` into the `terminal` branch that already exists for an unhealthy authority with no capability (`account_operator_posture.py:229-232`). Only the *failure* catalog loses the id: `open_custody_timeline` stays in `RecoveryActionId` and in `_DESCRIPTORS`, where a healthy authority genuinely can serve it. This is a deliberate widening beyond the two ids named in #1779: the invariant being restored is *no surfaced action may be structurally unarmable*, and a third action fails it identically.

4. **The terminal blocker carries a `fix_elsewhere` move to the runbook.** Under ADR 0027 a real cure that lives off this surface is `fix_elsewhere`, and `fix_elsewhere` **must** carry a `primary_move`. The move names `docs/runbooks/alpaca-sqlite-clerk-recovery-and-cutover.md`. Without it this decision would trade a false affordance for a dead end, and the operator would be no better off.

5. **The flat-and-order-free proof is captured offline, at recovery time, from the broker.** It is the `ResetBrokerProof` file validated at `recovery.py:1176-1217`, never a live sweep observation. No online producer of this proof will be built, because the authority whose replacement it authorizes must be stopped before it is consulted.

## Considered and rejected

**Option B — an orchestrated quiesce protocol** (quiesce bot runners → stop the account's active authority → release the lease → run recovery → install the new generation → reactivate → resume surfaces), daemon-mediated because the data plane cannot orchestrate its own authority's death. Rejected on four counts:

- **It creates a window with no automatic reconciler.** Since #1776 the sweep is the account's sole automatic reconciler, and it is also the lease holder. Quiescing to permit recovery means the account is unreconciled during precisely the operation most likely to leave broker and authority disagreeing. The offline ceremony has the same gap, but it is bounded by an operator who is present and has stopped every governed bot; an orchestrated protocol makes the gap a routine, one-click state.
- **It requires new distributed-state machinery for a rare event.** A quiesce/reactivate handshake spanning the daemon, the data plane and every bot runner is a new failure domain — partial quiesce, reactivation that never arrives, a second process reopening the account mid-ceremony — guarding an operation that has run zero times in production.
- **It makes the riskiest operation depend on the most moving parts.** `reset_authority` invalidates every generation-`N` control identity. Coupling that to the largest orchestration surface in the system inverts the usual relationship between blast radius and mechanism count.
- **It must reproduce the offline guarantee anyway.** The protocol still has to prove the writer is stopped before `_assert_no_live_lease` will pass, and still has to supply a broker-captured flat-and-order-free proof. Having reconstructed both preconditions, its only remaining product is the click — which is the part of a destructive authority replacement least worth optimising. Authority replacement arguably *should* require a deliberate out-of-band act.

**"Populate the proof so the button works."** Unexecutable, per Context: the sweep that would populate it is the lease holder whose liveness forbids acting on it, and in the only posture where the buttons render, the sweep is not running at all because the authority failed to boot.

**"Keep the disabled buttons and improve the copy."** This is the status quo and it is the defect. The current copy already reads as guidance (*"Refresh Alpaca positions and open orders before reset"*) and is unfollowable. Under ADR 0041 the generated Button Reference documents both buttons in the operator manual, so the dishonesty is not confined to the panel.

## Consequences

**What an operator does today when they need authority recovery.** The whole cure is available now, and it is entirely offline:

1. Stop every governed bot on the account, then stop the data-plane process that owns the account's SQLite execution lease; confirm no second process can reopen the account.
2. If the authority database is corrupt enough that its lease row is unreadable, author an account-bound process-stop evidence file and pass `--process-stop-evidence` (default freshness window 120 s). A *readable* unexpired lease refuses recovery regardless of this file.
3. For reset, capture the broker's positions and open orders and author the evidence file described in the runbook's "Broker evidence files" section.
4. Run the ceremony, e.g. `python -m scripts.manage_alpaca_sqlite_clerk --artifacts-root <root> --account-id <id> reset --broker-evidence <file> --max-evidence-age-ms 30000 --expected-bot <sid> --stopped-bot <sid>`, or `... rebuild` for the mirror path.
5. Restart the data plane. The new generation is bound and activated by the ceremony; generation-`N` control identities are invalid.

**Tooling that must exist for the offline path to be real.** The CLI and the runbook exist and are complete — this ADR does not defer them. Two gaps remain, and both are follow-up work rather than assumed capability:

- **The panel does not point at the runbook.** Until Decision 4 ships, an operator staring at a failed authority is told to wait, with no move and no path to the ceremony that would cure it. This is the single highest-value item in the follow-up.
- **Nothing captures the broker evidence file.** The CLI only reads it (`manage_alpaca_sqlite_clerk.py:279-297`); no script in the repo produces it. The operator authors it by hand from a separately retained Alpaca response. That is acceptable for a deliberate ceremony — a hand-authored, retained artifact is arguably the right level of friction for authority replacement — but it must not be described as automated.

**Positive:**

- The surfaced-action set becomes truthful: after Decision 2 and 3, every action the panel presents is one the backend can actually perform. The invariant #1779 exists to restore is enforceable, and is the natural CI check for the follow-up.
- The `wait`-with-no-move dead end is replaced by `terminal` + `fix_elsewhere`, which is the taxonomy ADR 0027 already defines for exactly this shape.
- Zero new orchestration, zero new distributed state, and no relaxation of the stopped-process invariant that makes recovery safe.

**Negative / accepted:**

- **An operator in the UI has no one-click last resort.** Recovery requires leaving the browser, reaching a shell with the artifacts root, and stopping the data plane. This is accepted deliberately: the operation invalidates every control identity on the account, and the two prerequisites it needs — a stopped writer and a broker-captured flat proof — cannot be established from inside the process being replaced.
- **Removing action ids is a breaking contract change.** `ActionId` is a closed enum on the wire; the OpenAPI snapshot, the generated Frontend types, the vocabulary snapshot and the generated operator manual all move together, and the Frontend specs that reference `rebuild_from_mirror` must be re-authored.
- **`open_custody_timeline` disappears from the failed-authority surface**, so the panel offers no inspection affordance at all in that posture. The preserved database and finalized mirror remain inspectable offline via the runbook, which is where the capability's own next-step text already sent the operator.
- **This ADR records a decision, not a conformance claim** (ADR 0039). At the time of writing the panel still presents all three capabilities. The surface changes above are follow-up implementation work owed by #1779's exit criteria, and the issues for them are not yet filed.
