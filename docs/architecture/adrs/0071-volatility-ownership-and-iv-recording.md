# ADR 0071 — Python computes implied volatility from raw quotes; an opt-in .NET schedule records it, and gaps stay visible

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-30
**Provenance:** Entry 8 of [#2745](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/2745), merged from #2742 E, #2743 B and #2741 kept why 6. The decisions are §7.1–§7.11 of `docs/architecture/iv-ownership-research.md` (2026-04 to 2026-04-29, including two external reviews), recorded here so that section can be cut. Owner rulings on map [#2700](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/2700) (2026-09-30): ☆ the opt-in Backend IV recorder is not dead; ◇ validated volatility math stays even when nothing uses it. Written by [#2749](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/2749).
**Vocabulary:** none owed — IV30, VRP confidence and recorder slots are research-math terms, outside the live trading and operator domain that `CONTEXT.md` covers.
**Related:** ADR 0068 (Python owns the math), ADR 0069 (tolerances and sovereignty), ADR 0022 (`int64 ms UTC`; ET wall-clock slots go through `America/New_York`).

## Context

A historical implied-volatility series cannot be bought back later. Polygon serves live option snapshots, but it has no history of the chain as it stood at 09:35 on a past day. So the platform records IV forward from the day the recorder runs, and it computes IV itself. Each choice below protects one thing: a recorded number must be auditable, reproducible from the raw quotes kept beside it, and honest about where it is missing.

## Decision

1. **Python re-solves IV from raw bid and ask. Polygon's `implied_volatility` is never authoritative** (§7.1). The recorder (`PythonDataService/app/services/iv_recorder.py`) stores the raw per-contract bid and ask plus the IV from our own solver. Polygon's field is kept only as `polygon_iv_diagnostic` inside the raw chain blob. Why: we cannot audit Polygon's solver; its tenor and ATM conventions need not match ours; and re-solving from raw quotes lets a better solver replace this one without refetching. Rejected: the vendor field as a fallback when our solver fails. No IV is more honest than vendor IV of unclear provenance.
2. **There is no Polygon-IV fallback tier** (§7.10). When our solver fails, the recorder writes an error-tagged row. A reviewer proposed an opt-in `polygon_field_fallback` tier with a confidence cap of 0.2. It was declined: a new provenance variant, cap calibration, a UI flag and test surface cost more than they return in a research codebase, where a loud failure is the point. **Reversal trigger:** live trading with monitoring SLAs flips the trade-off.
3. **VIX-style IV30 is primary and parametric IV30 is the alternate. Both are recorded, never blended** (§7.2). VIX-style replication (`app/volatility/vix_replication.py`) is the industry method. It is model-free under standard assumptions and checkable against the published CBOE VIX (about 19 bps apart on SPY, 2024-12-20). Parametric stays because wing truncation makes VIX-style brittle on illiquid chains, and because parametric falls back to an ATM straddle when wings are missing. The gap between the two *is* the skew premium, not a bug. Rejected: blending at write time, which hides that information. The consumer chooses.
4. **The synthetic-price share is gated by variance, not by count** (§7.3). Both `price_source_mix` (count share) and `variance_contribution_synthetic` (variance share) are recorded (`app/volatility/iv_provenance.py`), but the operational gate is the variance share. Why: a chain whose synthetic strikes sit at the OTM wings can be 10% synthetic by count and 85% by variance, and the variance share is what decides whether IV30 can be trusted.
5. **Quote quality is `quality_score = 1 − half_spread / mid`, clamped to [0, 1], applied the same way to every price source** (§7.9; `app/volatility/price_normalization.py`). The sources are live OPRA mids, recorded mids and close-proxy synthesis. Why: the shape comes from the data, so an ATM proxy with a tight spread scores near 1 and a deep-OTM proxy scores about 0.5. Rejected: the obvious "1.0 at ATM, decaying outward" rule, which needs a model of where ATM is and bakes in the answer.
6. **The .NET host owns the capture schedule. The Python recorder stays stateless** (§7.5). Quartz jobs (`Backend/Jobs/IvRecorderJob.cs`, `Backend/Jobs/IvRecorderRegistration.cs`, configured by `Backend/Configuration/IvRecorderOptions.cs`) fire at each slot, in `America/New_York` with DST handled by the scheduler. Each job POSTs `/api/iv-recorder/snapshot` per configured ticker. Why: .NET already runs the job rail, so every cron has one operational story, and a stateless recorder is easy to test and hot-restart. Rejected: APScheduler or Celery beat in Python, which add a broker for one cron. **The recorder is opt-in:** `IvRecorder:Enabled` is `false` in the checked-in `Backend/appsettings.json`, and an opt-in feature is not dead code (☆).
7. **The slots are 09:35, 12:30, 15:55 and 16:00 ET, Monday to Friday, set in config** (§7.6). 09:35 avoids the opening 5-minute imbalance. 12:30 sits mid-session, away from cross-region handoffs. 16:00 captures the print. 15:55 has been recorded in parallel with 16:00 since 2026-04-28 as a trial: it is near the close but outside closing-auction microstructure. Keeping one, the other or both is decided by measurement per slot: solver failure rate, spread width, variance share, largest single-strike share, and IV30 stability. These are capture times in config. They are not session boundaries and need no calendar.
8. **Samples go to an append-only JSONL store per ticker during burn-in, then to Postgres** (§7.4). The burn-in is the first 30 sessions. The Postgres target is a `recorded_iv_snapshots` table keyed `UNIQUE (ticker, snapshot_ts)`, with `snapshot_ts` as `int64 ms UTC`, loaded once in bulk at cutover. Why: no migration surface while the recorder's schema may still churn, a forward-compatible schema, and a smaller blast radius for recorder bugs. Rejected: going straight to Postgres.
9. **Recorder data is never forward-filled at the service layer** (§7.8). Consumers want IV per bar, but the recorder writes one to four samples a day, so sparse stays sparse. Coverage is reported as `coverage.iv_first_ts` and `coverage.iv_last_ts` on the response. A caller that wants forward fill does it explicitly, per call, and never persists it. Why: filling at the boundary would make every consumer assume dense IV and hide the gaps, and the gap density is itself a signal. Rejected: a `?ffill=true` parameter.
10. **Confidence has a hard floor of 0.1** (§7.7; `app/engine/edge/confidence.py`, configurable per route). Below the floor, the signal is suppressed whatever `z_scaled` says. Why: without a floor, `confidence × |z_raw|` can clear the threshold on a huge z even when confidence is near zero, amplifying noise from a stale indicator. The value is heuristic. It is to be calibrated from out-of-sample reliability curves (hit rate, IC, Sharpe by confidence decile) once the recorder has 30 or more sessions.
11. **Edge reads fall back to recorded IV, and a missing health score drops a factor rather than imputing one** (§7.11). When a caller omits `iv_series`, the edge router reads the recorder (`app/routers/edge.py::_iv_series_from_recorder`) and reports `iv_source` as `caller_supplied`, `recorder` or `absent`. When `health_score` is missing (an absent key or an explicit JSON `null`, treated the same):
    - the VRP confidence path drops the health factor: `confidence = 1 − vcs`;
    - the regime-feature path sets `feature_weight = 0`;
    - both flag `health_imputed_now`, so the UI marks the bar.

    Why: the previous 0.5 prior halved every no-evidence confidence on no evidence. For a multiplier on a z-score, "no evidence on stability" means "trust the other input"; for a contribution weight, it means "contribute nothing". An explicit `null` must not crash the parser into a 500. `tests/routers/test_edge_recorder_fallback.py` pins both shapes. Rejected: `Optional` threaded through every consumer; synthesizing health from strike coverage, which conflates structure with stability; an empirical-median prior, which drifts and has no evidence behind it yet.

## Consequences

- The volatility math stays importable and tested even when no route calls it (◇). Retiring a route does not retire the solver, IV30 replication, realized volatility, basis conversion or `iv30_health`.
- Changing a slot, the floor, the quality formula or the fallback rule is a new decision recorded here, not a config tweak.
- The research doc keeps its tolerances (§6), its sources (§11) and its worked examples (Appendix A). Those are receipts, not decisions.
