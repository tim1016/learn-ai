## Trade-offs and confidence

The temporal rule explicitly chooses latency over completeness for a sparse extended-hours minute emitted after its grace period; late prints are ignored and counted. This can differ from later vendor history. Retaining the exact observed source and classifying that divergence is appropriate; the pass found no evidence supporting a reversal of that documented choice. It does not imply that provider feeds agree or that every missing minute in every market is corruption.

Confidence is moderate-to-high for the examined transition invariants, bounded by scripted cases and source inspection. Real latency distributions, broker delivery completeness, process crashes at arbitrary instructions, and actual retained-store health were intentionally not examined. An unmodeled transition crossing these boundaries, or a source event delivered before its durable explanation, would change this conclusion. Previously established valuation/build-identity defects remain findings; passing this different boundary does not negate them.
