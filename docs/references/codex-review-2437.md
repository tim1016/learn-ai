# Terminal inventory and backtest result semantics

Investigation: [[Codex] Terminal inventory and backtest result semantics](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/issues/2437). Baseline `10b5f31b529c8507bd19bb24015f3d85fa9aba43`; branch `research/codex-2437-terminal`.

**Result: no new Critical/High and no new finding in this bounded pass.** Terminal settlement, synthetic-exit labels and strict persistence checks are internally consistent on the examined paths. This is positive evidence about finalization/reporting, not a reversal of the previously reproduced stale valuation mark, first-session statistics or paired-risk metric findings.

## Question resolved

The engine deliberately treats an end-of-data liquidation as a marked terminal settlement when no later fill bar exists. That synthetic event is distinct from a real-world broker fill, an unfilled entry, and a partial reduction. The final response and saved trade label preserve the distinction. A completed backtest which cannot reconcile its closed ledger to its headline equity is refused by the strict save path rather than silently given a matching invented trade.

## Rationale and recommendation

The [terminal policy](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/blob/10b5f31b529c8507bd19bb24015f3d85fa9aba43/PythonDataService/app/engine/engine.py#L682-L700) explicitly settles at the final observed close “so final equity and the closed-trade ledger reconcile,” and says the tag discloses the synthetic exit. That reason is satisfied by the inspected propagation. [Restating the last point](https://github.com/tim1016/learn-ai/blob/10b5f31b529c8507bd19bb24015f3d85fa9aba43/PythonDataService/app/engine/engine.py#L746-L761) makes summary risk see closing costs while avoiding duplicate time observations. No reversal is supported by this evidence.

Retain those distinctions and controls. Do not interpret a synthetic terminal settlement as evidence that a live order could execute at the historical final close. A future design promising open-inventory persisted reports would need an explicit realized-versus-marked reconciliation policy; this review does not silently broaden the current contract or queue a speculative owner decision.
