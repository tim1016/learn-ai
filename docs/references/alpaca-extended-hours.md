# Alpaca extended hours — the window, the anchor, the clock, and unfilled orders

**Status:** canonical for ADR 0059 slice 3 (2026-09-08). Lineage: live.

## What was built

- `BrokerOrderLeg.extended_hours` (contract validator: LIMIT + DAY|GTC) and `BrokerOrder.extended_hours`; `to_alpaca_order_request` forwards the flag on every order body, `from_alpaca_order` ingests it.
- `BrokerCapabilities.extended_hours_window` — the declared session as ET minutes past midnight; `ALPACA_EXTENDED_HOURS_WINDOW` = 04:00–20:00 ET.
- `app/services/session_authority.py` resolves PRE / RTH / POST from the calendar's regular session and the declared window (source `broker_declared_window`, `extended_phase_proven=True`).
- `app/services/decision_clock.py::extended_trigger_instants` and `app/services/decision_session.py::RunDecisionSession` — one run-scoped value object carrying the run's session kind and declared window. It is resolved once at each run boundary (`run_trade_bot`, `run_dry_run_bot`, `RunReplayProofService._compute`, `shape_program_leg`), and `resolve()` returning `None` is the single place "extended run, no declared window" is judged; every boundary refuses loudly on it. It owns the bar filter, the session-close instant and the clock binding, and it is what `ContinuityPolicy` carries — so the feed's continuity floor refuses an unprovable minute for exactly the minutes the run decides on.
- `app/broker/alpaca/marketable_limit.py::marketable_limit_price` and `app/broker/alpaca/clerk/program_leg.py::shape_program_leg`; the reducing order's shape is durable in `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED` facts.
- `app/broker/alpaca/clerk/fill_models.py` — the two synthetic fill models: `immediate_fill_price` (what the `sim:` world can do — fill at the decision bar's close or cancel on the spot) and `limit_touch_fill` (the resting model for the slice-4 shadow port). The `sim:` world reports limit legs honestly and calls the first of those rather than deciding marketability itself.
- `app/schemas/run_admission.py::ExtendedHoursAdmissionFact` on the Start/Resume admission surface, and the `RunReplayUnavailableError` refusal that keeps an extended run's replay proof honest when no window is declared.

## The anchor

`buy: ceil_tick(close × (1 + entry_bps/10⁴))`, `sell: floor_tick(close × (1 − exit_bps/10⁴))`; tick 0.01 at or above $1, 0.0001 below, chosen by the pre-quantisation value so a sub-dollar close crossing $1 rounds to $1.01 rather than to a sub-penny tick the vendor would reject. Rounding is in the marketable direction. The allowances are the effective profile revision's `xh_entry_bps` / `xh_exit_bps` (ADR 0060; `ALPACA_LIVE_XH_ENTRY_BPS` / `ALPACA_LIVE_XH_EXIT_BPS` before cutover), required for any `use_rth=False` run; unset → Start and Resume refuse `EXTENDED_HOURS_ALLOWANCE_UNSET`. Each is bounded `0 <= bps < 10_000` — a 100 % allowance is not a price — and an anchor that still quantises to zero or below refuses the leg (`EXTENDED_ANCHOR_UNPRICEABLE`) rather than escaping as a contract-validation error.

## The clock

`extended` buckets run from `floor_et(04:00)` to 20:00 ET; the regular close is an ordinary bucket boundary; every bucket is decided on the minute that closes it (`bot_trade_strategy._drain_bar`, #2303), so the day's last bucket is decided at 20:00 (`RunDecisionSession.close_ms`). A decision at exactly 16:00 is POST (sessions are half-open); a decision at 20:00 is CLOSED and refused (`SESSION_CLOSED_AT_DECISION`).

**The day's last bucket is therefore always refused.** The last bucket fires *at* the declared close, and the decision instant is then CLOSED under the half-open rule, so the 19:45–20:00 bucket produces a `SESSION_CLOSED_AT_DECISION` receipt every day rather than an order. For a bucket that decided ENTER this is harmless — no exposure is created. For one that decided EXIT it is not: the position carries overnight with a rejected receipt explaining why, and the program's next chance to reduce is the following session's first extended bucket at 04:15. Sizing the decision timeframe so the day's last bucket is not one a program is likely to exit on is the operator's lever until a later slice gives the final bucket a decision instant inside the session.

## Liveness in extended hours

A declared window proves the **schedule**, never liveness. Alpaca's clock is RTH-only — it reports `CLOSED` through every scheduled PRE and POST session, so "CLOSED" carries no extended-hours information at all and cannot distinguish a live pre-market from an unscheduled PRE/POST closure. The symbol-status stream still blocks on `HALTED` (`compose_market_liveness` resolves HALTED before the CLOSED branch, precisely so the exemption below cannot see past it), but an unscheduled *venue* closure raises no symbol event.

So the CLOSED-clock exemption for a `use_rth=False` ENTER requires **two** facts, and `market_liveness.liveness_blocks_entry` admits only on both: `extended_phase_proven` (the declared window or a fresh capability snapshot resolves the instant to PRE/POST) **and** `extended_session_live` — the market-data feed connected, not stale, and printing bars for that symbol. A live extended session prints; a closed venue does not. The freshness half is one predicate, `market_liveness.market_data_bars_live`, read two ways: directly over `feed.health(symbol)` at the strategy's ENTER gate, and through the Clerk's `StreamHealthGate.market_data_live` at its submission-boundary recheck — whose `ChannelHealth.healthy` is computed from the same function — so the two gates cannot diverge (#1671). `stream_health_refusal` still refuses a stale or disconnected channel ahead of the recheck with `STREAM_HEALTH_HOLD`; the recheck's own conjunct is what refuses when *no* health evidence is installed at all. `broker_v2_panel/market_pulse.py` applies the same pair, so the panel cannot report an unscheduled closure as tradable while the gate blocks it.

## Manual tickets

Manual tickets are regular-session only. `extended_hours=True` on a leg is refused at the boundary — `preview_manual_order` returns the typed `UNSUPPORTED_MANUAL_ORDER_SHAPE` capability, and `manual_orders._require_supported_leg` raises `ManualTicketConflictError` (HTTP 409) before any durable write, which also covers the submit that skips the preview because its command already exists. `validate_manual_order_accepted_facts` stays as the last line of defence, not the boundary: reaching it means an untranslated `ValueError` and an HTTP 500 where an operator should have read a refusal. Ruling R6 is otherwise unchanged — the instruction hash omits the flag when `False`, so every ticket accepted before the field existed keeps validating.

## Admission

Start and Resume each carry an `ExtendedHoursAdmissionFact` (`app/schemas/run_admission.py`) whose state is one of `NOT_REQUESTED | READY | UNSUPPORTED | ALLOWANCE_UNSET`. Both failing states refuse in **every** mode — trade, dry-run and log-only alike — and there is no mode carve-out:

- `UNSUPPORTED` — the active broker authority declares no extended window — refuses with `EXTENDED_HOURS_UNSUPPORTED`.
- `ALLOWANCE_UNSET` — the effective revision's `xh_entry_bps` / `xh_exit_bps` are not both set — refuses with `EXTENDED_HOURS_ALLOWANCE_UNSET`. A Dry Run runs on the synthetic authority built with the same `ProgramLegPolicy` and routes every intent through `shape_program_leg`, which refuses the same condition per decision; admitting such a run would start a bot that then rejects every extended decision it makes.

`app/services/run_admission.py` maps `ExtendedHoursAdmissionFact.state` to the named `LegRefusal` values exported by `app/broker/alpaca/clerk/program_leg.py`, so the wording an operator reads at the gate is the wording on the receipt.

## Leg refusals

`app/broker/alpaca/clerk/program_leg.py::shape_program_leg` never guesses a leg shape; outside the regular session it raises one of five typed refusals, each a rejected receipt with no broker contact:

- `EXTENDED_HOURS_UNSUPPORTED` — no declared window (`policy.window is None`).
- `EXTENDED_ANCHOR_UNAVAILABLE` — no exact retained decision bar to anchor the leg.
- `SESSION_CLOSED_AT_DECISION` — the decision instant is neither RTH nor an extended phase.
- `EXTENDED_HOURS_ALLOWANCE_UNSET` — the window and the bar are both present but no allowance is configured.
- `EXTENDED_ANCHOR_UNPRICEABLE` — the bar's close and the configured allowance quantise to a non-positive limit price.

Inside the regular session (the decision bar's own phase resolves to `RTH`) the leg is always `regular_session_shape(side)` — market, DAY, `extended_hours=False`. An `rth` binding's ENTER is never shaped by the bar at all; its EXIT is shaped by the bar's close exactly as an extended binding's is, so the day's last EXIT goes out as an after-hours limit — see "The regular close" below (#2440). A *decision-driven* EXIT's shape is durable on `EXIT_ACCEPTED` (see "Unfilled orders" below), so whichever later pass creates the reduction rebuilds the deciding program's leg — if it can still be sent then. An EXIT with **no deciding program** is shaped from the current instant instead — see "Operator flatten outside the regular session" below.

Every decision reaches `shape_program_leg` with its exact retained decision bar: both runners resolve it through the one shared `bot_trade_strategy._decision_bar_evidence`, which also names that bar in the decision receipt's `bar_ref`. A runner that resolved none would refuse every `use_rth=False` decision `EXTENDED_ANCHOR_UNAVAILABLE`, RTH-inside-extended decisions included.

A bar is *filtered into* the run by its **open** (`start_ms`, `RunDecisionSession.includes`) and *shapes an order* by its **close** (`end_ms`, `shape_program_leg`). Both are correct and they are deliberately different instants: the session that produced the bar is the one it opened in, while the order it drives exists only once the bucket has closed.

## Unfilled orders (D5.4)

- **Unfilled ENTER.** A broker-acknowledged ENTER the vendor cancels, expires, or rejects with zero fills leaves position 0, raises no uncertainty, and `NEW_EXPOSURE` stays allowed for a fresh ENTER decision. Since #2006 its effect operation also reaches `failed`, through the `ENTER_UNFILLED` transition — the ENTER mirror of plan ruling R12. Before that it read `operation_state="in_progress"` forever, because `fold_order_acknowledgement` wrote `in_progress` unconditionally and `enter.py` drives no terminal state of its own; the instance therefore stayed in the nonterminal set `strategy_instances_with_live_custody` reads while provably flat, and in extended hours a 20:00 ET cancel made that residue a daily event.
  - **It folds on a later observation only** — `fold_order_evidence` (a poll, the reconciliation sweep, a trade-update frame), never on the broker's immediate submit response and never under simulated execution authority (owner decision, 2026-09-21). A terminal effect surfaces as an `EffectOperationState.REJECTED` receipt, which `bot_trade_strategy` reads as "discard this evaluation", and `SyntheticBroker`'s ruling R9 cancels every non-marketable limit on the spot with zero fills — folding there would flip Dry Run and Shadow from commit to discard for a whole class of decisions. #2006 is a custody-residue fix, not a decision-loop change.
  - **Where the receipt nests depends on who is in charge.** Every observation route hands the fold `active_exit_for_order(order) or order.effect_operation_id`. When the ENTER is in charge of its own order (#2006), `ENTER_UNFILLED` is appended against the order. When an EXIT is cancelling the entry (#2251), #1379's operation-first timeline requires every transition appended against `entry_ref` during the EXIT to carry that EXIT's effect id, so `ENTER_UNFILLED` goes on the ENTER's own timeline with no `order_ref` and names the order and the EXIT in its `why`. Either way the fold terminalizes only the effect that carries it: the EXIT is not ended, and reaches its own outcome. Without an `order_ref` the fold cannot resolve the order's unknown-outcome identity, so while the ENTER is `unknown` it declines; the ENTER stays reconcilable, and once the EXIT ends the next observation lands under the ENTER, resolves the episode, and folds the #2006 case.
  - It raises no uncertainty, because a zero-fill ENTER opened nothing. A **partial** fill the vendor then cancelled is not proven unfilled and is deliberately left alone — the durable fill row is read as well as the snapshot, so a snapshot reporting `filled_quantity=0` over a recorded execution does not fold either.
  - A `failed` ENTER that carries a `broker_order_id` is newly reachable, so `reconcile`'s outcome branches evaluate terminal failure ahead of the reached-the-broker heuristic; otherwise the audit row would read `RESOLVED_SUCCESS` beside a failure receipt.
  - Pinned by `tests/broker/alpaca/clerk/sqlite/test_unfilled_extended_orders.py`: `::test_enter_cancelled_by_the_vendor_unfilled_leaves_no_exposure`, `::test_enter_cancelled_by_the_vendor_unfilled_reaches_failed`, `::test_an_exit_owned_entry_cancel_ends_the_enter_without_breaking_the_nesting`, `::test_an_exit_owned_entry_fold_is_idempotent_across_routes`, `::test_a_partially_filled_entry_an_exit_cancels_keeps_its_enter_open`, `::test_an_unknown_enter_is_left_to_its_own_route_so_its_episode_resolves`, `::test_a_submit_response_that_returns_cancelled_does_not_end_the_enter`, `::test_a_recorded_fill_blocks_the_fold_even_when_the_snapshot_reports_zero`, `::test_terminal_unfilled_enter_folds_its_failure_once` and `::test_partially_filled_then_cancelled_enter_does_not_reach_failed`.
- **Unfilled EXIT.** A reducing order the vendor cancels, expires, or rejects with no recorded execution is now proven unfilled and raises `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` immediately, on the first pass that observes the terminal broker state (plan ruling R12). Before this task the same evidence left the effect operation parked `unknown` — "awaiting websocket or recovery evidence" that a dead order was never going to produce — which also blocked any second EXIT attempt on that entry, since the parked `unknown` effect stayed active. The next EXIT decision, once accepted, is admitted despite the standing `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` uncertainty (selling the remaining long still moves the position toward zero) and creates a fresh reducing order at the new decision's anchor; the dead order is never resubmitted. Pinned by `tests/broker/alpaca/clerk/sqlite/test_unfilled_extended_orders.py::test_exit_reducing_order_cancelled_unfilled_is_an_uncertainty_immediately` and `::test_next_exit_decision_reissues_at_the_new_anchor`.
- **A deferred EXIT keeps its shape.** The decision's leg shape is durable on the `EXIT_ACCEPTED` facts (`ExitAcceptedFacts.with_reducing_shape`), not only on the reducing order, because the two can be many passes apart: when the entry is still working, cancel-and-prove defers, and whichever later pass creates the reduction — the 15 s reconciliation sweep, the exit watchdog, restart recovery — knows nothing about the decision. `resolve_exit` therefore takes no shape argument at all and reads it from the EXIT's own acceptance, so the deciding runner's first pass and every re-drive of that EXIT build the same leg. Only a **non-regular** shape is recorded: the regular-session shape is exactly what a reduction with no recorded shape already builds, so writing it out would change the canonical JSON of every regular-hours EXIT acceptance — and every sealed receipt hashed over one — to say what the default already said. Pinned by `tests/broker/alpaca/clerk/sqlite/test_exit_reducing_shape.py::test_a_deferred_cancel_still_reduces_with_the_decisions_shape`.
- **A resumed reducing submission replays its leg exactly, or not at all.** The reducing order's shape is durable in its `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED` facts, so a resubmission after a crash or a `BrokerUnavailable` rebuilds the identical limit from those facts — priced against the decision bar of the decision that created it, not against the market as it stands now. It is a DAY order, so it dies at the session end rather than resting into a market that has moved; a genuinely stale one is cancelled by the venue and folds through the unfilled-EXIT path above. Since #2440 the facts also carry the end of the session the leg was priced for (`valid_until_ms`, omitted for a regular leg), and a resubmission past it — or of a market leg after the regular close — is never sent: the order already has its client identity, so it is not re-priced either, and the EXIT folds releasably through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (see "The regular close").
- **An EXIT with no deciding program never queues a market order (#2007).** Before #2007 every such reduction — the exit watchdog, safe-flatten, reconciliation's re-drive of either — stayed market DAY, which Alpaca holds until the next 09:30, so nothing reduced a position held from 05:00 before the open. Now the operator's safe flatten is priced from the current instant, and the automatic paths price their own limits in an extended session (#2229); see "Operator flatten outside the regular session".

## The regular close: an EXIT sent after the session it was decided in (#2440)

Owner decision of 2026-09-24 (#2431): when an exit can only be sent after the session has closed, it goes out immediately as an after-hours limit priced near the last close, using the marketable-limit pricing this path already uses. If it has not filled by the end of after-hours, the operator is told the position is still open. It is never queued as a market DAY order for the next open.

- **The day's last EXIT.** A regular-hours run decides every bucket on the minute that closes it, so its last bar (15:59–16:00) is decided *at* 16:00 — 13:00 on an early-close day, the close always from the canonical calendar (`session_state_at_ms`). That instant is POST in the broker's declared window, where a market DAY order would be queued by Alpaca for the next open. `shape_program_leg` therefore shapes a regular-hours binding's EXIT by the decision bar's close exactly as an extended binding's: the same extended-hours DAY limit, `floor_tick(close × (1 − exit_bps/10⁴))` to sell, `ceil_tick(close × (1 + exit_bps/10⁴))` to cover, with the sealed exit allowance. `ProgramLeg.valid_until_ms` — the after-hours close for POST (the declared window's close, or the calendar's earlier one on an early-close day: 17:00 on 2026-11-27, `session_authority.order_session_state_at_ms`), the regular open for PRE — is durable on the EXIT's acceptance (`ExitAcceptedFacts.reducing_valid_until_ms`), exactly as a recovery limit's is. An ENTER is not affected: a regular-hours ENTER keeps the market leg, and the liveness gate refuses a closed-market ENTER upstream.
- **When the extended shape cannot be priced** (no retained decision bar, no declared window, no exit allowance, an unpriceable anchor) the regular-hours EXIT keeps its regular leg rather than a rejected receipt — a refused EXIT would never reduce — and carries the refusal on `ProgramLeg.unpriced`, which the Clerk logs at warning with the strategy and account (`action="regular_hours_exit_unpriced"`; `program_leg.py` stays pure). The send-time rule below then re-prices that leg off the live touch after the close, or refuses to send it — loudly, through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT`, which the bot page and the lane's attention bell both show.
- **Start and Resume require the exit allowance (owner decision 2026-09-25).** A regular-hours run on an authority that declares an extended window but resolves no allowance — a paper or `sim:` lane with no `xh_exit_bps` — would hold back its last EXIT every day, so Start and Resume refuse it with the shared `EXTENDED_HOURS_ALLOWANCE_UNSET` refusal (`bot_start_admission.extended_hours_admission_fact`), whose next step names the broker profile's exit allowance. There is no built-in default. The allowances are one document (`ExtendedHoursAllowances` has no exit-only form), so the refusal is the shared one and the entry allowance is set beside the exit's. Live is unaffected: a live revision carries all six envelope values and arming seals them. An authority with no declared window at all cannot price the exit whatever it is configured with; its run is admitted as before.
- **The send-time rule — one decision point for every EXIT.** Whether a reducing leg may go to the broker is decided when it is sent, not when the EXIT was decided, by `exit_resolution` for every EXIT alike — a deciding program's, the operator's flatten, the watchdog's re-drive: `_leg_to_create` when the reduction is created, `_created_leg_may_be_sent` on every submission and resubmission, both through `recovery_reduction.reducing_leg_verdict` (a market leg only inside the regular session; a limit only before its `valid_until_ms`), judged at the latest instant the leg may reach the broker — now plus `EXIT_SEND_GUARD_BAND_MS` (5 s, `recovery_reduction.send_arrival_ms`) — so a market leg checked at 15:59:59.9 or a limit checked at 19:59:59 is already past its bound, and a re-price is priced for that instant too. Just before the open the same band lets a market leg rest at most 5 s for the opening auction. Before #2440 this guard applied only to recovery EXITs, so a program EXIT delayed past the close — by a working entry's cancellation, an outage, a restart, a transient refusal — was still sent as market DAY (Codex review #2419, finding R1). The rule is judged after the entry set is proven terminal: a working entry is cancelled whatever the session.
- **A delayed EXIT is re-priced for the session open now.** At creation no broker identity exists yet, so a leg that can no longer go out is replaced by `price_automatic_recovery_reduction` from the current instant — the watchdog's own pricing (#2229): an extended-hours DAY limit one sealed exit allowance through the live IBKR touch in PRE or POST, spread-capped, or the market leg inside the regular session.
  - **One pricing input, whoever drives the EXIT.** Every path that can drive an EXIT hands `resolve_exit` the same seam, `SqliteAlpacaClerkFacade.recovery_pricing`: the deciding runner, restart recovery, the reconciliation sweep and its watchdog, operator Reconcile Now (and everything that calls `reconcile_account` on the facade), and the operator's safe flatten (`execute_safe_flatten_plan` takes it as a required argument). `resolve_exit` and `resolve_accepted_exit` have no default, so no caller can silently drive an EXIT with a different input, and the same EXIT is never re-priced by one driver and folded by another. On a `sim:` authority the seam prices nothing (`UNPRICEABLE_RECOVERY`): a synthetic broker fills against retained source bars, never the live market (PR #2230 review), so there such an EXIT folds instead, whichever path drives it.
  - **The live touch is read on the event loop, and only to re-price.** The production quote source registers IBKR demand in the market-liveness store, whose symbol map the IBKR status loop iterates and replaces on the loop. The EXIT machine's repository steps may run on a worker thread (the sweep, restart recovery and the watchdog pass `off_loop=to_thread`), so the step that creates the reduction judges the leg first and, only when it must be re-priced, answers that it needs the touch having written nothing; `_resolve_claimed` then reads the touch on the loop (`RecoveryPricing.read` → a plain `PricingSnapshot`) and runs the step again with it. A leg that goes out as recorded registers no demand at all.
  - **An operator's confirmed limit is never replaced by a price nobody confirmed.** Whether the recorded price is the operator's is read from the EXIT's own acceptance (`reducing_confirmed_quantity` recorded, no `reducing_priced_by`), never from the caller's pricing seam and never from the side-reconciled shape: a limit confirmed for the other side becomes a market leg under R11, and is still the operator's, so it folds rather than being re-priced. It expires with its session as before.
  - **A leg the Clerk prices at send time says so.** Its `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED` facts record `priced_by="clerk"` and the IBKR quote it was priced against (`reference_bid`, `reference_ask`, `reference_quote_observed_at_ms`), omitted at their defaults so every earlier row hashes as before. Its fills report realized slippage from that quote (`confirmed_flatten_reference_price` reads the order's own facts first), and a fold — a resubmission past its bound — takes its copy from them: "The Clerk-priced limit … was not sent", never a confirmation nobody gave.
- **When nothing can price it, nothing is sent.** No session open (20:00–04:00, after 17:00 on an early-close day, weekends, holidays), no allowance, no live quote, or a spread past the cap: the EXIT folds releasably through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (`PROGRAM_EXIT_SESSION_ENDED` for a program EXIT; the recovery codes for a recovery EXIT), the entry is freed, and the watchdog's re-drive prices a limit once a session opens. The episode's explanation says why nothing replaced the leg — the refusal's own words, "extended-hours pricing is unavailable" when a Clerk that cannot price after-hours folds at 16:01 while after-hours is open, or that a created order is never re-priced — and, owner decision 2026-09-25, the episode carries **when the watchdog will next try** as `next_attempt_at_ms` (int64 ms UTC on `UNCERTAINTY_RAISED`, projected on the custody read and rendered by the shared timestamp display in ET): not before the 120 s re-drive age, and then in the first session the pricing seam can send in — typically the 04:00 pre-market open after an after-hours exit that could not go out (`recovery_reduction.next_redrive_at_ms`). A resubmission is never re-priced (it would change the leg under an existing client identity); past its bound it folds the same way. Nothing is resubmitted into a flat position, in the session or out of it: a resumed submission whose attribution is already flat proves the EXIT attributed-flat against the reducing order it would have resent, so the unknown outcome its lost submit left (`ORDER_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, keyed by that exact identity) closes with it.
- **A reducing order the broker refuses tells the operator.** A synchronous 4xx on the reducing submit folds the EXIT releasably as `ORDER_SUBMIT_FAILED` and, while exposure is still held, raises `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` with the watchdog's next attempt — before #2440's review it was a bare failed transition with no notice and no bell.
- **Unfilled by the end of after-hours.** The limit is a DAY order, so Alpaca ends it at the after-hours close. The first pass that observes the terminal state — the 15 s sweep, or a trade-update frame and the next sweep — folds the unfilled-EXIT path above and raises `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` with the headline "An extended-hours exit ended unfilled; the position is still open". That episode is the bot page's attention evidence and the lane's attention bell (`GET /api/brokers/{broker}/attention`, #2228), and carries the watchdog's next attempt. Nothing is resubmitted for the next day; the watchdog's next re-drive is the pre-market Clerk-priced limit. The Clerk does not rely on the broker ending the order: a reducing order the broker's exact lookup still reports working `REDUCING_ORDER_PAST_SESSION_GRACE_MS` (5 min) after its session ended — its own bound, or the regular close for a market leg — raises `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` once ("An exit order is still working after its session ended"), while the EXIT keeps custody of the order (not failed, entry not released, so the watchdog re-drives nothing over it). Only an order seen working, and only while its EXIT has no outcome: a lost submit or a failed lookup is an unknown outcome, not a working order, and an EXIT the send-time rule already folded keeps that notice and its next attempt.
- **Out of scope:** the backtest still fills a last-bar decision at the 16:00 close (#2467); a lost gateway during a run is #2411.

## Operator flatten outside the regular session (#2007)

Owner decisions of 2026-09-19. The session is `SqliteAlpacaClerkFacade.flatten_session` — the canonical calendar's regular session, widened by the window the broker declares.

- **Regular session — market DAY, as before.** A safe flatten confirmed inside 09:30–16:00 records no shape.
- **PRE / POST — an extended-hours DAY limit the operator prices.** Prepare safe flatten carries `reduction_pricing` (`app/routers/alpaca_clerk_sqlite.py::_check_recovery_action`): the live IBKR bid, ask and sizes, and a suggested limit — `floor_tick(bid × (1 − xh_exit_bps/10⁴))` to sell, `ceil_tick(ask × (1 + xh_exit_bps/10⁴))` to cover, through the canonical `marketable_limit_price` with the sealed exit allowance (`recovery_reduction.py::price_recovery_reduction`). The quote rides the market-status snapshot from the Clerk's own demand-driven IBKR subscriptions (`app/broker/ibkr/market_liveness.py::_quote`; `MarketLivenessStore.top_of_book`) — never Alpaca market data. The bot cockpit shows it in a ticket that re-reads the quote every 2 s, always with the Prepare token the panel presents *now* (the Clerk re-mints it each reconciliation pass; a rotated token refreshes the panel and retries once). The operator may send the suggested price or their own, only after an explicit confirm; Review freezes the price and the quote beside it, and Send sends exactly that, never a later refresh. The confirmed limit travels the bot-scoped custody execute route (`RecoveryActionExecuteRequest.extended_limit`).
- **What the Clerk refuses before any EXIT is accepted** (`recovery_reduction_shape`): a quote more than 10 s old or newer than the one the Clerk itself holds (`RECOVERY_QUOTE_STALE` — a client cannot vouch for freshness the server cannot see); no live quote at send (`RECOVERY_QUOTE_UNAVAILABLE`); a price Alpaca would reject, asked of `BrokerOrderLeg` itself (`RECOVERY_LIMIT_PRICE_INVALID`, and `allow_inf_nan=False` keeps `Infinity` a 422); a price further through the book than the band — the sealed exit allowance times a deploy-time multiple, default **2×** (`RECOVERY_LIMIT_OUTSIDE_BAND`; `ALPACA_LIVE_XH_EXIT_BAND_MULTIPLE`, bounded [1, 10], carried on `ExtendedHoursAllowances.exit_band_multiple` and deliberately not a ceremony number, #2229) — from the live bid/ask; and the regular session having opened since (`RECOVERY_SESSION_CHANGED`). Each refusal reaches the operator typed: the 409 carries `reason_code` and, for a clock refusal, `available_at_ms`.
- **Slippage is shown, not assumed — and the Clerk is the one who works it out.** Every execution and cost figure the operator reads comes from `recovery_reduction.py` and is rendered unchanged in the browser (AGENTS.md § "Python owns all math"). Pressing **Review** is therefore a round trip: the ticket sends the operator's price as `RecoveryActionCheckRequest.proposed_limit_price`, and the Clerk answers with `evaluate_proposed_limit` — how far through the touch the price reaches (`through_book_bps`), the worst case that costs on this quantity (`worst_case_cost` = `max(0, touch − limit) × qty`), whether it is past the band, whether the book at the touch is thinner than the order, and whether it merely rests behind the touch. That answer is what the confirm pane shows and what Send sends; a price the Clerk reads as outside the band never reaches the confirm pane at all. The ticket also warns when `quote_spread_bps` exceeds `RECOVERY_SPREAD_WARNING_BPS` (50 bps of mid). The quote the Clerk priced the confirmed limit against is durable on the EXIT (`ExitAcceptedFacts.reference_bid/ask`) — and a leg the Clerk re-prices when the reduction is created records its own on the order (`ExitReducingOrderCreatedFacts.reference_bid/ask`, #2440) — so each fill is reported against it in bps and dollars (`realized_slippage_bps`, `realized_slippage_cost`; positive = worse) on `RecentFillView`, rendered by `app-flatten-fills`.
- **A confirmed price covers exactly the reduction it was shown.** The quantity the operator reviewed is durable with the price (`ExitAcceptedFacts.reducing_confirmed_quantity`). Cancellation fixes the real reducing quantity several steps later, so if a fill lands in between and the attributed reduction no longer matches, nothing is sent: the EXIT fails through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (`RECOVERY_LIMIT_QUANTITY_CHANGED`) and the entry is free to price again. The executor refuses the mismatch earlier still, when the presented plan's leg is not the one the price was confirmed for.
- **Realized slippage is only reported for the leg that was actually priced.** R11's side reconciliation can replace a confirmed limit with a regular-session market leg on the other side; that leg was never priced from the confirmed quote, so `confirmed_flatten_reference_price` compares the created leg against the confirmed shape and answers `None` rather than measuring a fill against a price it never used.
- **"Sent" is only said on the broker's evidence.** A durably accepted EXIT whose reducing order has not reached Alpaca — a lookup outage, a transiently blocked REDUCE — is reported as accepted and pending, never as sent; the panel reads `broker_order_id` on the returned orders to tell the two apart.
- **A confirmed limit expires with its session.** `ExitAcceptedFacts.reducing_valid_until_ms` records the end of the session the price was confirmed in (the regular open for PRE, the declared close for POST). Past it, `reducing_leg_verdict` answers `expired`: nothing is sent, the EXIT fails through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (`RECOVERY_LIMIT_SESSION_ENDED`), and the entry is free for the operator to price again. A price is never carried into another session by a deferred entry proof, an outage resubmission or a restart.
- **No session open (20:00–04:00, weekends, holidays) — refused.** No reduction is sent; the refusal (`NO_SESSION_OPEN`) carries the next session's open as `available_at_ms`, which the plan renders in ET.
- **One rule guards every path to the broker.** `reducing_leg_verdict` is applied to the leg actually about to be sent — when it is created, after the R11 side reconciliation that can turn a confirmed limit into a market leg, and on every resubmission — for every EXIT since #2440, decision-driven ones included (see "The regular close"). A market recovery leg is `send` only inside the regular session; outside it, an unpriced one is re-priced from the current instant when the Clerk can, and otherwise the EXIT **folds releasably** through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` (`RECOVERY_MARKET_WAIT_ENDED`, #2229) — a waiting EXIT never holds its entry overnight, the operator's priced flatten of that entry is accepted, and the watchdog's re-drive prices a limit itself. The verdict is judged after the entry set is proven terminal, never before broker contact: the pre-contact recovery gate that saved one exact lookup per entry was removed with #2440, because the same early fold on a program EXIT would leave its working entry uncancelled.
- **Automatic re-drives price their own limits in extended sessions (#2229).** The stuck-EXIT watchdog (`exit_watchdog.redrive_or_escalate_stale_exits`) re-drives market DAY inside 09:30–16:00 as before; in the declared PRE or POST window its re-drive is an extended-hours DAY limit the Clerk prices itself (`recovery_reduction.price_automatic_recovery_reduction`) — the same marketable-limit formula as the operator's suggested price, exactly one sealed exit allowance through the live IBKR touch, durable on the EXIT's acceptance with its reference quote (so realized slippage is reported per fill) and the end of the session it was priced in. Owner decision 2026-09-19 (evening), superseding "automatic re-drives wait for the operator outside the regular session". When no priceable reduction exists — no open session (20:00–04:00, weekends, holidays), no allowance, no live quote, or a book whose spread is wider than the deploy-time cap (`ALPACA_LIVE_XH_EXIT_SPREAD_CAP_BPS`, in bps of mid, bounded [1, 1000], default 50 — the same number `RECOVERY_SPREAD_WARNING_BPS` shows a human; automatic path only, since the operator's ticket shows the wide spread and can override) — the re-drive defers: nothing is sent, the `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` episode stays raised for the operator, and the entry stays free. The watchdog logs `quote_spread_bps` beside every priced send and every refusal, so the cap is tuned from data. A Clerk-priced leg is stamped on the acceptance (`ExitAcceptedFacts.reducing_priced_by`), so a quantity change or an expiry folds with copy that asks no operator to confirm a price nobody confirmed. The sweep hands the watchdog the facade's policy as a *resolver*, so a re-arm is picked up per pass, never a boot-time snapshot.
- **In the panel**, the generic unpriced Execute safe flatten is presented disabled outside the regular session, with a `confirm_in_form` move to Prepare (`BOT_COCKPIT_SAFE_FLATTEN_PREPARE_ANCHOR`), and the bot banner promotes the *enabled* flatten — Prepare — rather than the blocked Execute. Cohort flatten, which sends the unpriced action per leg, is therefore refused in PRE/POST; a cohort is flattened bot by bot there.

Known edges and open risks:

- ~~An unshaped recovery EXIT held for the open owns its entry, so the operator's own extended-hours flatten of that entry is refused~~ — fixed by #2229: a market recovery leg that can no longer be sent folds its EXIT releasably through `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` at every wait site (at creation after side reconciliation, and on resubmission — the outage shape included), freeing the entry in both deadlock shapes.
- The re-drive's automatic price is computed, never operator-confirmed: it is exactly one sealed exit allowance through the live touch, inside the band by construction, but the operator did not see the quote it was priced against — only the reference quote and realized slippage reported after the fills.
- The spread gate trades a certain bad fill for overnight gap risk: refusing means the position carries. The cap is refused, never clamped — a mid-price invents a number nobody chose (ADR 0059 D4).
- `top_of_book`'s `observed_at_ms` is the poll time, not the last tick: a book that stopped ticking reads perpetually fresh, and no spread cap detects a frozen quote. Detecting one needs the feed to carry the last tick time — a code fix, tracked as a follow-up.
- The fold's `EXIT_NOT_FLAT` episode re-drives after the declared age gate (120 s), not instantly: a crossed-session fold waits at most one gate before the priced re-drive.
- A quote is only live while the source keeps publishing it: `MarketLivenessStore.apply_status_snapshot` replaces its whole quote map from each snapshot, so a symbol a snapshot omits (a dropped subscription, a disconnect, a reconnect before the book arrives) has no quote at all rather than a remembered one inside the freshness window.
- The quote is IBKR's consolidated book; Alpaca executes extended-hours orders on its own venues, whose book can be thinner. A price that looks marketable here can fill worse, or not at all, there — unverified against a live extended-hours fill.
- IBKR bid/ask **size** units for US equities (shares vs round lots) are unverified live; the depth warning's threshold rests on them.
- Order-placing code bounds after-hours by the calendar on early-close days (#2440 review): `session_authority.order_session_state_at_ms` ends POST at the earlier of the declared close and the calendar's scheduled after-hours close (17:00 on 2026-11-27) and is CLOSED past it. A regular-hours bot's 13:00 last-bar EXIT is bounded by 17:00, an EXIT delayed to 17:30 is not sent, and an extended run's leg decided after 17:00 refuses `SESSION_CLOSED_AT_DECISION`. The declared window itself — which bars an extended run decides on, the shadow broker's session — is unchanged; #2391 moves the scheduled bounds into the canonical calendar. The vendor's early-close after-hours end is still unverified — see the paper exercise below.
- A paper revision cannot carry the allowances from the configuration page today: the revision form shows the envelope (and so `xh_entry_bps`/`xh_exit_bps`) only for a Live revision, and the schema stores the six envelope values all-or-nothing. Until that changes, the Start/Resume refusal above is fixed for a paper lane only by the lane's environment (`ALPACA_LIVE_XH_ENTRY_BPS`/`ALPACA_LIVE_XH_EXIT_BPS`, for a lane bound without a profile) — a launch hurdle the page cannot yet clear.

## Replay proof

`RunReplayProofService._compute` (`app/services/run_replay_proof.py`) resolves an extended binding's (`use_rth=False`) window from **the run's own durable evidence**, never from the live authority: the runner records its resolved `RunDecisionSession` once at run start — `_RetainedSourceBarFeed.__init__` writes it through `SourceBarLedger.record_decision_session`, into the ledger's `source_run_decision_session` table, beside the bars and continuity facts that session governs. A capability change after the fact therefore cannot reinterpret a finished run, and an on-demand or boot-repair replay does not depend on any authority being active — the live read failed in both directions. The record is write-once and idempotent; a *different* session for the same run identity raises rather than overwriting. A `use_rth=False` run with no recorded session is refused loudly — `RunReplayUnavailableError` with `http_status=503`, naming the missing record — rather than silently replayed as an empty-but-"proven" receipt for bars the run's `RunDecisionSession` could never have matched. An `rth` binding needs no record: `use_rth=True` resolves to the calendar's regular session with no window at all.

## The `sim:` world (R9)

The synthetic broker (`app/broker/alpaca/clerk/synthetic_broker.py::_resolved_order`) asks `fill_models.immediate_fill_price` whether the leg transacts, and records honest `order_type`, `limit_price`, `time_in_force`, and `extended_hours` on the synthesized order either way: a marketable LIMIT leg — or a MARKET leg — fills at the decision bar's close, and a non-marketable LIMIT is cancelled on the spot with zero fills, since the sim world cannot rest an order (ruling R9). The qualification **drill** double (`app/services/alpaca_sqlite_synthetic_drill_support.py::DRILL_CAPABILITIES`) declares no extended session at all — it copies the paper capabilities and does not carry an extended window, so drill runs never exercise this path.

## Hash-chain compatibility

Every durable leg hash — the manual instruction hash, the manual command's `payload_hash`, and the bot-driven ENTER decision's `payload_hash` — routes through the one canonical payload rule, `app/broker/alpaca/clerk/sqlite/facts.py::leg_instruction_payload`, which omits `extended_hours` from the hashed payload when it is `False`, so a leg accepted before the field existed keeps validating against its stored hash. `EXIT_REDUCING_ORDER_CREATED` facts (`ExitReducingOrderCreatedFacts`) apply the same rule to their four shape fields (`order_type`, `time_in_force`, `limit_price`, `extended_hours`): each is omitted from the serialized fact when it equals its regular-session default. `EXIT_ACCEPTED` facts (`ExitAcceptedFacts`) carry the same four plus `reducing_side`, under one shared `_omit_defaults` rule — and record them only for a non-regular shape, so every acceptance written before the fields existed, and every regular-hours one written after, serializes byte-identically.
